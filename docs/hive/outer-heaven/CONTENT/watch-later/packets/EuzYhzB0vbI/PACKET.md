## Transcript said (pointer — read full.txt)

1. Opening demo: four agents looping, calling sub-agents, writing prompts, designing systems — he asks if that is productive or a cool demo. Tweets on tape: stop prompting coding agents; design loops that prompt them. **Boris Cherny** and **Peter Steinberg** (later captions also say **Steinberger**) no longer prompt; they write loops. A loop is **trigger + action + stop condition**. A “meta agent that infers loops from your vibe” is the falling-behind bit he is clearing up, not the prescription.
2. **Loop engineering** = replace yourself as the person who prompts the agent; design the system that does that. A loop = recursive goal: purpose, then iterate until complete. Two pillars: an **objective** goal (not subjective) and **verification** (how the agent knows the stop). 24/7 swarms that do not move the needle just **scale bugs**. Nate: cadence + event actions; around-the-clock fleets do not help his knowledge-work use.
3. He ran a research loop (articles, YouTube transcripts, X; captions say **45 sources**) then built HTML through screenshot-review until it said done (not V1 — captions say **V7**). Definition: reason → act → observe → repeat until checkable done (cake-fork test). Quality vs attempts: outsource the feedback loop so attempt one lands higher. Three shapes: **solo loop** (what he uses most) · **maker-checker** · **manager + helpers**. Majority of tasks do not need a massive architecture — one terminal session + a good prompt is enough.
4. Demos from **Matthew Berman’s Loop Library** + his own `/goal`: (a) 10 thumbnails scored vs a Mr Beast rubric, top three improved, iterate the strongest — **27 minutes**; “until satisfied” is a weak done; best loops = keep iterating until **X metric = Y**. Scores were subjective; a dedicated scorer sub-agent would be the fix. (b) Three.js plane — build, open browser, spin, iterate — **37 minutes**; still not perfect (wanted see-through). (c) Abbey Road recreated in HTML/CSS, no image gen — stop if average **≥9**, hard cap **8** passes; screenshots get closer; final still looks nothing like the photo. Loops get you **closer on the first try**, not 100% perfect.
5. Before you build: **what does done mean** and **how will it check** (visual / functional / play the level / tone). Tools must match the check. What makes a loop work: checkable goal, hard stop, good tools, memory, separate checker, planning first, logging, cost that makes sense. **12-hour+** loops often not useful; he likes **~35 min to a couple hours**, or a chunky overnight **4–8 hours** then a human iterates. Do not drop everything because Steinberger **10x**’d — it does not apply the same to every role.
6. Close: slide deck + “full audit” in the **free School / Skool** classroom → All YouTube resources. Like the video.
