# Business-ethics integrity / stakeholder / CSR checklists (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks whether a business act is ethical (not merely legal), what integrity requires, who the stakeholders are and whose claim wins, whether a program is CSR or greenwashing, what employers and employees owe each other, how to treat diversity/accommodation/pay as rights, or how to stay ethical when work goes remote, gig, or automated — in the standard undergrad business-ethics school.

**Do not use when:** the job is reconstructing or taking a Saylor (or any) exam, dumping Byars/Stanberry, running **BUS205** black-letter law / fiduciary-as-litigation, **PHIL103** moral and political philosophy, **PRDV231** ethical-leadership virtues as a leadership course, **BUS608** ethical-and-strategic management, **BUS101** intro-to-business survey (ethics as a named constraint), **BUS107** frontline-CS golden-model, **BUS208** POLC, **BUS209** three-level OB / culture-as-Schein, **BUS301** HRM procedure, **PHIL203** environmental-ethics seminar, or "how do I hide this / make it look compliant."

This is **one** skill. It is not "Unit 1 skill + Unit 2 skill…". BUS200's seven units are one argument: **business ethics is the practice of deciding — and living — as a professional of integrity inside a stakeholder field, not a shareholder-only profit machine**; you hold one standard, refuse legal-as-ethical, run theory as lenses, map and prioritize claims (including CSR and the planet), honor mutual obligations, respect the rights of all even as work changes, then **commit**.

## 1. Procedure — answer a business-ethics question in five checks

Run in order. Skip a check only if the user already constrained the frame. If a check fails, name it; do not "try harder on the same close."

Before the five checks, write the **identity**:

**Ethics** = the standards of behavior we hold ourselves to (honesty, empathy, trustworthiness, other virtues) in personal *and* professional life. In a firm it governs how we treat customers, clients, colleagues, employees, shareholders, and — in this school — society and the planet.

**Integrity** = unity of word and deed; a code you actually keep; trustworthiness. A **professional of integrity** practices what they preach and does what reason says is right, including when it is expensive.

Twin's survey list (controversial subjects this school expects you to *reason about*, not memorize as a statute): corporate governance, insider trading, bribery, discrimination, CSR, fiduciary responsibilities.

### Check A — Integrity frame (always)

Three tests that fail before any theory table is worth running:

1. **Legal ≠ ethical.** Compliance is the floor, not the ceiling. "Just because it's legal doesn't make it right; just because it's right doesn't make it legal." If the answer is only "counsel signed off," you have not done this course. Hand off **BUS205** for the statute; stay here for the *ought*.
2. **One standard, not two.** A "work ethic" that contradicts the home ethic is the failure mode. Single-code conduct is what engenders trust. Multiple standards (strict with the board, loose with the supplier) is the tell.
3. **Time horizon.** Ethics that "cost money this quarter" can still be the profitable long-term move via goodwill, talent, partners who run due diligence, and customers who stay. Driscoll's course-era line: businesses without values are businesses at risk (reputation, price, recruitment, counterparties passing). Treat the *mechanism* as live; treat any 2017 numbers as dated.

**Success** in this school is not only wealth, status, or power. It includes a reputation you can live with and the ability to think well of yourself. If the prompt defines winning as "close the deal," say so and keep Check A on the table.

If you cannot name the **values** in play (CLO 6) and whether word matches deed (CLO 1), you are still in Check A.

### Check B — Theory lenses (at least two)

This is not a philosophy seminar. Hand off **PHIL103** if the question is "what is the good?" It *is* CLO 2 and the toolkit for CLO 7. Corporate default is often **utilitarianism-as-efficiency**. Do not retire the other lenses.

| Lens | Ask of *this* act | If it "wins" alone, you missed |
|---|---|---|
| **Virtue** (Aristotle / Confucius) | Does this form a virtuous character and keep relationships righteous? West: *phrónēsis* (prudence), *eudaimonia*, ends aligned with means. East: sincerity, constant mean, harmony / *dao*; business as a trust network. | Virtue-signaling; "harmony" as silence |
| **Utilitarianism** (Bentham / Mill) | Greatest good for the greatest number of **stakeholders**, not just shareholders. Outcome is the test. Mill: do not harm. | People as utils; "the number came out" |
| **Deontology** (Kant) | Duty / categorical imperative. Are people **ends**, not means? Intent counts; outcome is secondary. | A clean motive that crushes someone you refused to count |
| **Justice** (Rawls) | Would we choose this from behind a **veil of ignorance** (not knowing our seat)? Freedom, not subjugation. | A fair-looking rule that cannot reward risk or tell the truth about desert |

**How to use:** name the dilemma in one sentence → run two lenses that *disagree* if you can → say what each would permit and forbid → pick with Check C's stakeholder map, not with "I like Kant." Aquinas's reminder (this school): to act dishonorably casts disrepute on all concerned; livelihoods sit inside the city, not outside it.

### Check C — Stakeholders + CSR (whose claim, and is the program real?)

A firm that only counts **shareholders** is running the Friedman / shareholder-primacy school. This course **teaches that debate** (OpenStax: not conclusively resolved in the courts) and then asks you, for CLO 5, to treat CSR as genuine practice in the modern marketplace. Encode the opposition; do not pretend BUS200 is a Friedman course. Fiduciary-as-litigation → **BUS205**. Strategy-level sustainability → **BUS604 / BUS608**.

**Identify before you rank.** Categories this school names:

| Linkage | Who | Why they matter |
|---|---|---|
| **Enabling** | Stockholders, legislatures, regulators, board | They *permit* the firm to function |
| **Normative** | Competitors, peers | They set the informal rules of the industry |
| **Functional** | Input: suppliers, employees, unions. Output: customers, distributors, retailers | They are the operating circuit |
| **Diffused** | NGOs, voters, media, often the planet / future generations | Less direct, still able to move the firm; the set **varies** |

Enabling + functional + normative are **constant**. Diffused shift. Four "publics" (nonpublic / latent / aware / active) warn you that silence is not consent.

**Rank** by urgency, significance, and **power × interest**. High-power + high-interest is the quadrant you cannot ignore. Identities shift (a latent public becomes active after one video). Survey process: establish trust → identify → gather/analyze → present to management → let them know they matter. Customers/donors often sit in the high-priority seat because their expectations *are* the relationship.

**CSR / triple bottom line:** people, planet, profit on equal standing. Planet is a stakeholder whose healthy existence is a condition of operating. **Greenwashing** is the fake: the claim without the commitment. Genuine CSR is internal *and* external ethics; this school says it does not mean lower profitability on a long horizon. Society, environment, and government are the three "special" stakeholders OpenStax names in ch.4 (not a live Saylor unit title — mention them; do not invent a live subsection list). Sarbanes-Oxley is the named transparency stick, not a securities-law course.

If you cannot name **who is owed** and whether the planet/community is in the room, you are still in Check C. Do not skip to a press release.

### Check D — Mutual obligations (employer ↔ employee)

Obligations run **both ways**. A loyalty speech from a firm that monitors without cause, pays the legal floor as if it were fairness, or retaliates after a report is Check A failing.

**Employer owes (survey):**

- A workplace people want: **safety, no harassment, respect**. Perks attract; they do not substitute for the ethical choice. A well-treated workforce is also (this school claims) a productive one — that is a *bonus*, not the only reason.
- **Fair wage** is judgment + moral imperative, not "we met the statute." Legal floor (OpenStax: U.S. federal **$7.25** — **re-fetch**) is not automatically fair. Gender pay gap and real-minimum erosion are the book's illustrations. Contractors vs employees, and who pays benefits, are ethical choices, not only tax choices.
- **Unions** as a lawful negotiating advantage some workforces still use. Encode the *right to organize* as a stakeholder move, not a union-busting or union-campaign playbook (**BUS301 / BUS209**).
- **Privacy:** what monitoring and drug-testing law *allows* is not what you *should* do. Individual privacy is a claim. Hand off **BUS205 / BUS206** for the legal/system design.

**Employee owes (survey):**

- **Basic loyalty:** protect IP and trade secrets; no competing while employed in a conflict-of-interest way; do not loot customers or colleagues on the way out. Not feudal; two-way.
- **Brand and customers:** treat them well. Internal marketing only works if the firm treats *you* well first.
- **Atmosphere:** codes covering money, bribery, harassment; alert to violence-escalation. Be a responsible person in the job.
- **Financial integrity:** U.S. **bribery** and **insider trading** are illegal *and* unethical even if another country's practice is looser. A **gift policy** is how you tell a gift from a bribe. "Everybody does it on this route" is Check A's multiple-standards tell.
- **Whistleblowing:** for **serious wrongdoing**, not revenge. Protections (and sometimes rewards) exist; hostile aftermath is a known cost. Workplace rants and dunking the employer online are generally **not** First-Amendment-protected in this school. Escalate injury, fraud, and regulated-product issues; you are not a lawyer (**BUS205**).

Culture, geography, and time **modify** how "polite," "loyal," and "fair" read (OpenStax ch.5 — not a named Saylor unit). Outsourcing that "looks the other way" on the supply chain is the named failure. Some prohibitions (lying, fraud, murder) look constant; applications shift. Do not dump Hofstede. Do not run BUS209's culture-as-Schein diagnostic.

### Check E — Rights of all, future work, then commit

**Rights of all (CLO 4 applied to people the firm might other):**

- Diversity is a talent/customer/performance claim *and* a rights claim. Inclusion is the manager's job of making it work. Reluctance is not a veto.
- **Reasonable accommodation** for religion, absence of faith, disability (schedule, devices, an understanding manager). Procedure → **BUS301 / BUS205**.
- Sexual orientation / identity: the ethical claim is **skills, not personal life**. The 2018 textbook's "federal law does not" sentence is **stale** (*Bostock*, 2020). Label it. Re-fetch law if the user needs a compliance answer — that answer is **BUS205**, not this skill.
- Pay equity / middle class / who is an employee: firms can lead. Animal welfare has entered the stakeholder field for agribusiness, pharma, cosmetics — encode as a claim, not a seminar.

**Future of work still has an ethical frame (Unit 6):**

| Shift | Ethical move this school names | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Remote / telecommute | Trust, equitable expectations, data privacy, actually transmitting culture, keep comms open | "Flexibility" that is always-on; spying as management |
| Campus / village | Amenities as attraction; watch tethering and community effects | Golden cage |
| Flex / job-share | Equity + written expectations; two minds on a problem | Flex for some, overload for the rest |
| Gig / access economy | Who is responsible in the share; benefits; payroll tax; intern treatment | "You're a contractor" as an ethics-off switch |
| Robotics / AI | Retrain people into the future | Automate and exit; 2018 product names as if current |

Profession-specific overlays (start-ups, ads, insurance, U.S. employer-based health care) live in OpenStax ch.9 — **not a live Saylor unit title**. Mention if the prompt is in that industry; do not invent a live subsection.

**Commit (Unit 7 / CLO 6–7).** Professionalism = craft + conduct. Ethical professionals work where **values align**. Evaluate occupational fit as an ethical screen, not only a salary screen. Shared values beat a compliance culture of distrust. The means of making money ethically are not trivial. Conscience is formed over time; direction of travel matters.

When the user wants a "how I will act" artifact, output this **commitment**, not an exam key: one standard (A), two lenses (B), a ranked stakeholder map including whether CSR is real (C), a named obligation in each direction (D), a right you will not trade and a future-of-work constraint (E).

## 3. Worked micro-patterns

**"It's legal, so we're fine."**
Check A first. Legal floor ≠ ethical ceiling. Then C: who still has a claim the law does not capture. Then B: a duty-lens or virtue-lens will often forbid what util-as-efficiency permitted.

**"Should we maximize shareholder value?"**
Name Friedman as the opposing school. This course's CSR CLO is stakeholder + TBL, not shareholder-only. Run C's table. Profit stays *a* bottom line, not the only one. Courts have not "settled" this — do not fake a holding. Fiduciary litigation → BUS205.

**"Who are the stakeholders / whose claim wins?"**
C's four linkages, then power × interest, then urgency. Do not skip diffused (media, NGOs, planet) because they are quiet today. State how the set will shift.

**"Is this CSR or a campaign?"**
People + planet + profit, internal *and* external, over a long horizon. If the planet is not actually a constraint, it is **greenwashing**. A report without a changed operation is a tell.

**"Can we monitor email / drug-test / pay minimum wage?"**
D. May ≠ should. Privacy is a claim. $7.25 is a legal floor (re-fetch) not a fairness proof. Accommodation and equal pay sit in E.

**"Should I whistleblow / post about my boss?"**
D. Serious wrongdoing → protected channel, not revenge, not a dunk account. First Amendment generally does not cover the rant. Injury/fraud/regulated product: escalate; you are not the lawyer.

**"Our overseas supplier does X."**
A (one standard, not a geography exception) + C (functional + diffused + planet) + the ch.5 warning: looking the other way on the chain is the failure. Culture modifies manners; it does not license fraud.

**"We're going remote / gig / AI."**
E's table. Trust and equity first. "Contractor" and "the model decided" are not ethics-off switches. Retrain rather than discard. Then D for what the firm still owes a human worker.

**"Should I take this job?"**
Unit 7. Occupational fit includes **values alignment**. A professional of integrity does not outsource their standard to a paycheck. Run A–C on the firm's actual conduct, not the careers page.
