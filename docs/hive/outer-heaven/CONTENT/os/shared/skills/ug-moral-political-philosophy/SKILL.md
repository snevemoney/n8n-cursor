### Check B — Utilitarian vs deontological vs natural law; value of a human life (when "may we kill / what is a life worth / greatest happiness" is in play)

CLOs 6–8, 11. Unit 1.

Name, in order:

1. **Hard cases first.** Live frame: justice as the hinge; SNLS residue: intuitions divide on murder/morality cases and force us to name principles. Not "have an opinion."
2. **Utilitarianism in depth.** Bentham's calculus ≠ Mill's refinements (higher pleasures, rights-shaped utility, pitfalls of the greater good). CLO 6's **utilitarianism** name lives here.
3. **Contrasts, not the Kant unit.** Deontology and natural law enter as *alternatives to consequentialism* (SNLS 1.6). The **titled** Kant seat is Unit 3. Do not finish Groundwork here.

**Hand off Unit 2** if they want rights against the state. Stay here for "what makes this killing wrong, and which theory of the good is speaking."

### Check C — Rights, the state, the free market; Locke vs Crito vs libertarian (when "does the individual have a right / may the state tax, draft, take property" is in play)

CLO 6 (libertarianism), CLO 10. Unit 2.

Name the **seat** before naming Rawls:

1. **Leaving consequences.** Libertarianism: justice is **natural rights**; consequences are secondary (SNLS Unit 2 intro).
2. **Crito vs exit.** Plato: no right to defy the city that raised you. Locke: the contract can be **rescinded**; life, liberty, property; labor and property; dissolving government. CLO 10's constitutional-government origin lives here (and again with Hobbes in Unit 4).
3. **Nozick / Friedman** are the contemporary libertarian overlay (property, side-constraints, free market), not Locke's 17th-century founding and not Smith's whole *Wealth of Nations* as this course.

Quiz trap: **Locke ≠ Hobbes.** **Rousseau is not this unit.** **Crito is obligation to law, not a Republic dump.**

**Hand off Unit 3** when the market pits rights against rights. Stay here for "who has a right the state must not touch."

### Check D — Kant after market conflicts (when "duty / using people as means / surrogacy / organs / draft / lying" is in play)

CLO 6 (deontology), CLO 7. Unit 3.

Pick the **job** before maximizing utility:

1. **Rights in conflict inside the market.** SNLS residue: draft, Baby M, organs as commodities. Confirm live case list on login; encode the *job* (rights collide), not a dump.
2. **Kant.** Duties because we are beings who can reason; deontology = rooted in duty; Groundwork / categorical imperative overlay. Using persons as **mere means** is this seat, not a PHIL102 fallacy name.
3. **Do not steal BUS200.** This is justice theory, not a workplace code.

Quiz trap: **Unit 1 already named deontology as a contrast. Unit 3 is the titled Kant seat.** A rights conflict is not automatically "so utility" and not automatically "so Kant wins."

**Hand off Unit 4** for fairness / original position. Stay here for "duty vs market, and whether rights are enough."

### Check E — Hobbes vs Locke vs Rawls (not Rousseau) (when "state of nature / social contract / fairness / redistribution / veil" is in play)

CLO 6 (social contract), CLO 10. Unit 4.

Separate the **three live names** and refuse the textbook fourth:

1. **Hobbes (this unit, lead-in).** Life before government "nasty, brutish, and short"; selfishness; sovereign to keep the peace. Leviathan overlay.
2. **Rawls (this unit, title).** Update of the social contract; starting point **fairness**, not natural rights; political liberalism; whether income / education / opportunity should be redistributed to ensure fairness. Original-position / veil jobs: confirm live wording on login.
3. **Locke is Unit 2.** Natural rights, property, dissolving government — already seated. **Rousseau is not a live listed title and is not in the live intro figure list.** That dump is **POLSC201**.

Quiz trap: **Hobbes ≠ Locke ≠ Rawls ≠ Rousseau.** **Fairness ≠ utility ≠ libertarian side-constraint ≠ Kantian duty.**

**Hand off Unit 5** for virtue / the good we share. Stay here for "what would we agree to as fair, from where."

### Check F — Virtue, community, morality and politics (when "character / desert / community / cultural relativism / morality vs law" is in play)

CLOs 6 (virtue), 9, 11. Unit 5.

1. **Virtue as the older account.** Aristotle: merit, the good human life, the good state. MacIntyre / communitarian return against Enlightenment rights-first politics. Can we agree on what living virtuously means?
2. **Applications (SNLS residue, confirm live):** disability vs desert (Casey Martin / PGA); constrained freedom inside a community; same-sex marriage / the problem of agreement; cultural relativism.
3. **Existentialist contrast, not PHIL304.** Nietzsche will to power; Sartre radical freedom / radical responsibility. Hand off PHIL304 if they want existentialism as a *course*.
4. **Morality and politics / morality and law (CLO 9).** MLK overlay from the live intro. Not a PHIL102 "appeal to authority" dump.

Quiz trap: **Virtue ≠ utility ≠ duty ≠ rights.** **Cultural relativism is not "Units 1–4 do not apply."** **SNLS "Nietszche" is a misspelling; live intro spells Nietzsche.**

**Hand off PHIL203** for environmental community. Stay here for "character, the good, and whether politics can name a virtue we share."
