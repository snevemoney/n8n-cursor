# Intro HRM: recruit, retain, compensation, strategy (clustered)

**Use when:** a prompt needs **introduction to human resource management** — people as the business; HRM more than payroll/benefits/harassment office; recruitment and selection steps; attract/hire/retain; compensation and benefits vs motivation; strategic HRM with managers in the loop — in the Saylor PRDV226 **Introduction to Human Resource Management** school (14 h). **Never send.**

**Do not use when:** reconstructing exams; **PRDV225** line-manager motivate/empower/employee-conflict; **PRDV221** why-managers-exist; **PRDV102** résumé (candidate side); **PRDV103** interviewing (candidate side); **PRDV230** conflict *policy*; writing operator HR policy or **sending** recruiting/employment mail; inventing labor-law cites; treating the reused late-1800s **description** as this course’s job; promising ACE.

This is **one** skill. Four live units, four CLOs. Loop: **right people then recruit/select then attract-hire-retain then compensation/benefits then strategic wrap.** Do not mint four unit-skills.

## 1. Procedure — six checks

Run in order. Stop if Check A says another school or send.

### Check A — Identity overlay (always)

| Prompt smells like | Hand off |
|---|---|
| "Motivate / empower *my* reports / handle their fight" | **PRDV225** |
| "Why do managers exist" | **PRDV221** |
| "Write *my* résumé / ace *my* interview" | **PRDV102 / PRDV103** |
| "Write a conflict-resolution *policy*" | **PRDV230** |
| "Find, hire, keep, pay people (HR + manager)" | **this skill** |
| "Send this job post / offer / rejection" | **HITL — refuse send** |

**Primary goal:** literacy on how HR and managers together get the right people in and keep them. Named failure mode: sending employment mail, writing operator HR policy, or treating this as 225.

### Check B — Right people / why it matters (when "any hire will do" is in play)

CLO 4. Unit 1 overlay. Unit 4 wrap.

**Explain the importance of hiring and retaining the right employees.** Live: people are the business; identify skill sets and personal characteristics that increase productivity in the present and future workforce.

Shape: right people as a productivity/strategy job, not a vibe. Do not write a requisition and send it.

### Check C — Recruitment and selection steps (when "how do we hire" is in play)

CLO 1. Units 1–2.

**List the steps in the recruitment and employee selection process.** Live: historically HR sifted applications; now managers must help screen technical roles because HR staff often lack that knowledge.

Shape: steps as a job. Include manager-plus-HR, not HR-only. **Do not invent** test names or legal cites guest never showed. **Refuse send** of postings, outreach, or offers.

### Check D — Attract, hire, retain (when "we can't keep anyone" is in play)

CLO 2. Unit 2.

**Identify ways to attract, hire, and retain high-quality employees.** Live unit title: Recruiting and Keeping Quality Employees. Intro: companies work harder to attract and recruit talent.

Shape: attract + hire + keep as one loop. If the prompt is a candidate polishing a résumé, hand 102. If it is a line manager motivating a person already on the team, hand 225.

### Check E — Compensation and benefits vs motivation (when "just pay them more" is in play)

CLO 3.

**Discuss methods of employee compensation and benefits and their impact on employee motivation.** Live CLO. Do not steal 225’s “factors that motivate” list.

Shape: pay and benefits as *one* motivation lever inside HRM, not the whole 225 school. No invented salary numbers. Not Personal CFO. Not a send.

### Check F — Strategic HRM (when "HR is just payroll" is in play)

Unit 3. Intro overlay.

Live: modern HRM is developing a more strategic nature; a top HR executive today will most likely report directly to the CEO and play an integral role in executing a company's strategy; managers work in conjunction with HR.

Shape: HR as strategy partner, not the harassment office. Unit 4 “Tying It All Together” is wrap, not a fifth CLO.

## 2. Worked patterns (shape only)
