# Principles of microeconomics: scarce choice, markets, failure (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks how consumers, firms, or government should choose under **scarcity** -- opportunity cost, PPF, supply and demand to equilibrium, elasticity, surplus and the marginal rule, utility / diminishing MU, producer costs in the short run and long run, competitive vs non-competitive market structures, or government tools for market failure (public goods, externalities, public choice, efficient pollution) -- in the Saylor ECON101 **undergraduate principles of microeconomics** school.

**Primary goal:** rational choice under scarcity, made visible by models, evaluated by surplus and efficiency. That is not "pass ACE," not "GDP went up" (**ECON102**), and not "value is subjective as *method*" (**ECON103**).

### Check B -- Scarcity, opportunity cost, PPF, advantage (when "what to give up / can we produce both / who should specialize" is in play)

CLO 1. Unit 1.

Teach terms **once**:

1. **Scarcity** -- wants exceed what resources can deliver.
2. **Opportunity cost** -- value of the next-best alternative.
3. **Margin** -- one more, or one less.
4. **PPF** -- feasible combinations given resources and technology; points inside are inefficient, on the curve are efficient, outside are unattainable now.
5. **Absolute vs comparative advantage** -- fewer inputs vs lower opportunity cost. Trade can raise both sides even when one side is better at everything.

Rittenberg overlay (summarize): economics is the study of choice; micro is consumers and firms; the PPF makes the trade-off visible.

**Hand off ECON102** if the PPF is being used as a *growth/AD-AS* story. **Hand off ECON103** if "scarcity" is being recast as *absolute scarcity of human time* as Austrian doctrine. Stay here for "this intro-micro choice."

### Check C -- Supply, demand, equilibrium, controls (when "who buys, who sells, where is the price, floor/ceiling" is in play)

CLO 2. Unit 2.

Name:

1. **Demand** -- Qd falls when P rises (ceteris paribus). Shifters: income (normal vs inferior), related goods (substitutes vs complements), tastes, number of buyers, expected prices.
2. **Supply** -- Qs rises when P rises (ceteris paribus). Shifters: input prices, technology, number of sellers, expected prices.
3. **Movement vs shift** -- a price change for *this* good moves along the curve; a shifter moves the curve.
4. **Equilibrium** -- Qd = Qs. Surplus above, shortage below.
5. **Controls** -- binding ceiling -> shortage; binding floor -> surplus. Price is a rationing signal.

SNLS residue folds elasticity into this unit. **Live Unit 3 owns elasticity.** Stay here until the question is "by *how much*."

**Hand off Unit 3 / Check D** for responsiveness. Stay here for "which way, where is the new intersection."

### Check D -- Elasticity (when "how much will quantity change / will revenue rise if we cut price" is in play)

CLO 2 overlay. Unit 3. **Live distinctive unit** (not in the 2021 seven-unit shell).

Encode the *job*, not a formula dump:

- **Price elasticity of demand** -- how much Qd responds to P. Elastic: responsive. Inelastic: not.
- **Revenue test** -- if demand is elastic, a price cut raises total revenue; if inelastic, a price cut lowers it.
- **Income elasticity** -- normal vs inferior.
- **Cross-price elasticity** -- substitutes vs complements.
- **Elasticity of supply** -- how much Qs responds to P.

Polar cases (perfectly elastic / perfectly inelastic) are OpenStax labels -- **confirm live**. Do **not** teach calculus derivatives as Saylor doctrine. Do **not** dump OpenStax Netflix/cigarette cases as live readings.

**Hand off ECON102** if "elasticity" is a macro multiplier. Stay here for "this market, this curve, this response."

### Check E -- Maximize: surplus, consumer, producer (when "should they buy one more / produce one more / what does this cost" is in play)

CLO 3-5. Units 4-6.

**Unit 4 (surplus / marginal rule).** Total surplus is consumer surplus + producer surplus. Maximize by comparing **marginal benefit to marginal cost**. **Sunk (fixed) costs** do not decide the next unit. Either/or vs how-much. Market failure is *named* here so Unit 8 has a target.

**Unit 5 (consumer).** **Utility**; **diminishing marginal utility**. Demand is derived from consumer theory. Utility analysis and indifference analysis are two pictures of the same choice (SNLS -- confirm live). Do not require BUS630's culture/involvement stack. Do not invent nudges as live CLO language.

**Unit 6 (producer).** Combine **labor, capital, and natural resources**. **Short run** = at least one input fixed; **long run** = all variable. Diminishing returns is a *short-run* fact. Profit max: produce while **MR >= MC** (CC school -- confirm live). Factors are **inputs**, not a ninth live unit. OpenStax ch.4 labor/financial markets is a parallel chapter -- do not file it as Unit 9.

**Hand off BUS105** for job-costing/CVP. **Hand off BUS300** for operations design. **Hand off BUS630** for graduate CB. Stay here for "this household's utility, this firm's cost curves."

### Check F -- Structure, then government / failure (when "how many firms / should government tax, cap, or define rights / is zero pollution the goal" is in play)

CLO 6-8. Units 7-8.

**Unit 7 structures.** Perfect competition = many firms, price-taker, benchmark. Monopoly = one firm, price-maker. Monopolistic competition = many, differentiated. Oligopoly = few, strategic. Game theory / prisoner's dilemma / Nash are SNLS labels for oligopoly -- confirm live; they are a *tool*, not a math course.

**Unit 8 government.** **Public goods** (nonrival + nonexcludable) and **externalities** (costs/benefits off the price) are why CLO 3's toolkit exists. Public finance and **public choice** (the voter/politician also maximizes). **Coase**: defined rights + cheap bargaining can trade to the efficient point. CLO 8: move toward an **efficient level of environmental pollution** -- **efficient is not zero**. Do not turn this into PHIL203 or POLSC401.

**Hand off ECON102** if "government" means fiscal/monetary stabilization of aggregates. **Hand off ECON103** if "intervention" is being recast as *coercion vs cooperation* as Austrian doctrine. Stay here for "this market failed, this micro tool."
