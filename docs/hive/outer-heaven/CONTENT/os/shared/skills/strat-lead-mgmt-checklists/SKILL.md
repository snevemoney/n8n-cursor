# Strategic leadership checklists (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks how leadership differs from management, which leadership theory applies, how to decide and change an organization, how to influence without a title, how to build or shift culture toward goals, or how to lead a group/team in the Saylor BUS401 undergraduate strategic-leadership school.

**Do not use when:** the job is reconstructing or taking a Saylor (or any) exam, dumping Northouse / Yukl / Kotter *Leading Change*, running BUS208 POLC-as-the-manager's-job checklists, treating the prompt as BUS209 OB specialty or BUS501/BUS608 strategy formulation, selling DISC/MLQ as the course, or writing a manipulation script.

This is **one** skill. It is not "Unit 1 skill + Unit 2 skill…". BUS401's six units are one argument: **leadership and management overlap in the work and split in the concept**; theory is a menu not a stack; every change is a decision under bias; most of the work of leading happens *without* legitimate power; culture is the durable container of that work; groups become teams under cohesion, diversity, and a shared goal.

## 1. Procedure — answer a strategic-leadership question in six checks

Run in order. Skip a check only if the user already constrained the frame.

### Check A — Leadership vs management (always)

**Leadership** (course glossary): a process of social influence where one person enlists the aid and support of others in accomplishing a common task.

**Management** (course glossary): getting people together to accomplish desired goals and objectives using available resources efficiently and effectively.

Live course intro: task-by-task they differ; generally what they *do* is largely the same (data, coordination, responsibility **with or without** formal power). CLO 1 still wants the distinction. Do not collapse the words.

| | Management | Leadership |
|---|---|---|
| Job | Coordinate resources toward goals (systems, plans, standards) | Enlist people toward a common task (influence, vision, meaning) |
| Power | Often **legitimate** (title) — not required | Often **earned** (peers, expertise, referent) — title optional |
| Relationship | Subordinates / resources | Followers who *adopt the goal* |
| Failure mode | Overmanaged, underled: tidy machine, nowhere to go | Inspired people, messy work, no control loop |
| Test | Can they allocate and hit a standard without anyone *wanting* it? | Would people still move if the org chart went silent? |

**Formal vs informal leader** (CC spine): formal leaders hold a position and may use position power *and* personal power. Informal leaders have no position and lead through personal power. Force/punishment → dictator, not leader. People willingly adopt the goal.

**Vision** (glossary): a clear, distinctive, specific view of the future, usually connected with a leader's strategic advances. If there is no picture of *later*, you are coordinating, not leading.

Lewin **leadership typology** (Unit 1, not the change model): authoritarian (close control, distinct professional distance) / democratic (share ideas, social equity among workers) / laissez-faire (maximum autonomy). No best style. Laissez-faire is the one with a documented downside (ambiguity, conflict, weak satisfaction). Environment (athletics, business, politics — Unit 1d) shapes which style even *looks* like leadership.

If asked "what should they *do*?," name whether the bottleneck is **systems** (manage) or **enlistment** (lead) before naming a theory.

### Check B — Theory families (when "what kind of leader," traits, style, or "best theory" is in play)

Course glossary: theories of leadership split into **trait, behavioral, situational, visionary**. They **contradict as often as they complement**. Do not stack them into one super-model. Pick the family the symptom points at. Emphasis should not be placed on any one theory (Unit 2 intro, archived and live).

**1. Trait — who they *are* (emergence vs effectiveness)**

Distal (indirect): personality, cognitive ability, values. Proximal (direct): interpersonal, problem-solving, communication skills.

CC spine (summarize): intelligence is a modest predictor; **EQ** (self-awareness, motivation, empathy, social skill) is the differentiator once IQ is already high. Big Five: extraversion strongest for emergence *and* effectiveness; conscientiousness and openness help; agreeableness (conflict-avoiding) often *hurts* emergence. Integrity is load-bearing — lose it, lose the right to lead. Self-esteem supports both support and effective punishment.

Limits: traits ignore situation; they predict *emergence* better than they mint a leader; "great man" is dead. Dark Triad / Dark Tetrad appear as live Unit 2 resource titles — use as **warning labels** (narcissism, Machiavellianism, psychopathy, plus sadism in tetrad), not as a hiring rubric. Gender-difference readings exist on the map; do not essentialize.

**2. Behavioral — what they *do***

Task-oriented / initiating structure vs people-oriented / consideration (Ohio State / Michigan). Task → productivity; people → satisfaction. "Hi-hi" (high on both) is **not** a proven universal. Blake-Mouton (live resource) is the 9,9 grid of that idea — a teaching diagram, not a law.

Decision style is a behavior: authoritarian / democratic / laissez-faire (Check A). Climate of participativeness matters more than being invited to every decision.

Theory X vs Theory Y (McGregor; glossary spells "MacGregor"): X assumes lazy + must be forced; Y assumes people can enjoy work. The manager's implicit theory writes the org.

**3. Situational / contingency — when the style works**

Situational favorableness (glossary) = the mix of contingencies. Teaching models (CC spine; confirm live names on login):

| Model | Move | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Fiedler LPC | Style is relatively fixed; match person to situation (leader-member relations × task structure × position power). Low-LPC (task) at the extremes of favorableness; high-LPC (people) in the middle. | Partial support. Do not treat LPC as a personality test you owe the user. |
| Hersey-Blanchard SLT | Match directing / coaching / supporting / delegating to follower competence × commitment. | Popular, mixed research. Do not over-rely. |
| House path-goal (glossary) | Leader's job is expectancy: effort→performance, performance→valued reward; **clear the path**. Styles: directive, supportive, participative, achievement-oriented. | Complicated; partial support. The usable bit: *change style*, unlike Fiedler. |
| Vroom-Yetton-Jago | How participative should *this decision* be? Decide / consult individually / consult group / facilitate / delegate. | Use when "should I decide alone?" is the actual question. |

**4. Visionary / contemporary — how they *enlist***

Transformational (glossary): shared vision; people forego personal interests for organizational values. Four I's: idealized influence, inspirational motivation, intellectual stimulation, individualized consideration.

Transactional (glossary): exchange time and effort for rewards. Contingent reward and active management-by-exception can work. **Passive** management-by-exception (wait for the fire) is the failure mode.

Course wants **both** transformational and transactional. Charisma is trainable-ish (~10% personality) and has a dark side (crisis boards hire heroes). CEO charisma often *follows* past success; it does not guarantee future performance.

LMX: high-quality (trust, liking, respect, extra-role) vs low-quality (job-description-only). In-group/out-group is a fairness problem. Build it on justice and early tests of trust, not on demographic similarity.

Servant: the job is to serve followers, customers, community — employee development as an end. Authentic: be yourself; self-aware; values-consistent. Neither is a license to ignore systems.

**Visionary-leader qualities** are a live Unit 2 resource. Treat "seven qualities" as a reading, not a memorized canon, until login confirms the list.

If asked "which theory is right?," answer: **the one whose independent variable matches the bottleneck** (person, behavior, situation, vision). Then name one competing family so you have not stacked them.

### Check C — Change as a decision (when the org is moving, or a choice is stuck)

CLO 3: decision-making and change management are **related**. A change is a decision that has to survive other people. A decision that cannot be implemented is not a change.

**Lewin change model** (glossary; Unit 3a): **unfreeze** (prepare mindset) → **change implementation** (execute the plan) → **refreeze** (reinforce so the new culture/habit is the norm). Skipping unfreeze = resistance. Skipping refreeze = snap-back.

Unfreeze moves (CC spine, summarized): communicate the plan *and* a vision; build urgency without fake crisis theater; coalition of opinion leaders; emotional + instrumental support (training); **participate** people in diagnosing the problem. Execute: keep supporting; small wins; remove obstacles (including quiet saboteurs). Refreeze: publicize results; reward the new behavior; make it the control system.

Resistance is information, not disloyalty. Reasons this school names: disrupted habits, personality (low openness, low self-concept, low risk-tolerance), uncertainty / lost control, fear of failure, personal impact, change fatigue, perceived loss of **power**. Reactions: active resistance / passive resistance / compliance / enthusiastic support. Compliance (glossary) = behaving as expected whether you agree or not — that is not internalization.

Communication of change is its own Unit 3b. If people hear it from the grapevine, you already failed unfreeze.

**Decision process**

Rational (glossary): identify problem → generate alternatives → evaluate → choose optimal → evaluate effectiveness. Teaching ideal. Limits: time, information, politics.

Bounded / satisficing (glossary): search until "good enough." Not laziness; a human constraint. Use when the cost of more search exceeds the value of a better option.

Individual vs group (Unit 3c): groups bring variety and buy-in; they also bring process loss, loafing, time, and **groupthink**. Glossary's cheerful "groups are less biased" is a *potential*, not a result. If the decision needs quality *and* commitment, Vroom-Yetton (Check B) is the tool; do not default to consensus.

**Biases this course names (Unit 3e):** anchoring, groupthink, selective perception (glossary: screen out what we wish to ignore). Also expect confirmation, overconfidence, and "what's your default?" as live titles. Name the bias *before* adding process (devil's advocate, nominal group, devil's data).

Do not please everyone. Anticipate both supporters and protesters. Communication is the manager's lever; the decision method (edict / consult / collective) has an appropriate time and place.

### Check D — Power without formal authority (when there is no title, or the title is not enough)

CLO 4 is the course's distinctive overlay. Everyone will lead without **legitimate power** (glossary: rightful claim from position/title). That is not a consolation prize; it is most of the work.

**Power** (glossary) = ability to influence the behavior of others. **Influence** = emotions, opinions, or behaviors affected by others.

Bases this glossary names (French-and-Raven school, incomplete list):

| Base | Source | Use when | Burns when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legitimate | Title / role | The org chart already authorizes the ask | You have no title, or the title is disputed |
| Expert | Superior skill/knowledge in a *specific* area | You actually know; the group needs that knowledge | You fake it; the domain shifts |
| Referent | Attractiveness / desire to emulate | Trust and identification exist | You spend it on self-promotion |

**Login gap:** live "Sources of Power" almost certainly also teaches **reward** and **coercive** (and maybe information). Do not claim the glossary listed five. If the user needs the classic five, label it as the standard French & Raven set used by this school, not as a harvested glossary line.

Outcomes of influence: **compliance** (they do it, they don't believe it) vs **internalization** (they accept the values). Aim for internalization when the work continues after you leave the room. Compliance is enough for a one-shot safety rule.

Levers beyond bases (Unit 4 map): giving advice, mediation, conflict management, adaptive nonverbal communication, emotional intelligence, working with individuals (including aggressive personality types). Emotional intelligence (glossary): manage one's own emotions *and* those of others. Interpersonal skill is how informal power is spent, not a separate virtue.

Positive of leading without title: the situation can create the team; recognized leaders emerge (founders, experts, the person who actually knows). Negative: no resource rights; influence can slide into manipulation; you can be made the unofficial owner of every fire. Expert power is the cleanest step-up. Referent power is slow to build and fast to burn.

If asked "how do I lead this with no authority?," name (1) which base you actually have, (2) whether you need compliance or internalization, (3) one interpersonal move (advice, mediation, coalition), (4) what you will *not* do (force, fake expertise, gossip politics).

### Check E — Culture as container (when "how we do things," ethics, or "change the culture" is in play)

CLO 5: develop an effective culture **given the organization's goals**. Culture is not vibe. It is the system of shared values, norms, attitudes, and beliefs that guide employee behavior (glossary).

Schein layers this school uses (glossary names):

| Layer | What it is | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Observable artifacts | Surface signals to the outside world | Would a visitor see it in 10 minutes? |
| Shared values | What newcomers are supposed to learn and internalize | What gets praised in public? |
| Underlying assumptions | Taken-for-granted insider knowledge | What would shock them if you said the opposite out loud? |

**ASA** (glossary): Attraction-Selection-Attrition — matching values join; mismatches leave. Strong cultures hire their own continuation. Weak cultures do not actually govern behavior; the org chart and the loudest person do.

**Competing values framework** (glossary): two axes — internal vs external focus × flexible vs stable. Use it to *type* a culture against the goal, not to rank cultures as good/bad. A stable-internal culture can be the right container for safety-critical work and the wrong container for a pivot.

Leadership's job in culture (Unit 5b): role-model, keep policy and behavior consistent, tell the stories, change artifacts *and* assumptions. Creating culture change is Lewin (Check C) applied to the assumption layer — slower, more political, more about who gets hired and fired (ASA).

Ethics and CSR (Unit 5e) are culture outputs, not a poster. Social control (glossary) = internalized norms reduce the need for supervision. If you need a rule for everything, the culture is not doing the job.

Strong vs weak: strong = widely shared and intensely held (high social control, high ASA, high resistance to needed change). Weak = artifacts without assumptions. Match strength to how often the strategy must change.

If asked "how do we change culture?," refuse the poster. Name the goal, the current CVF/assumption, the ASA loop that is reproducing it, and one artifact + one hiring/reward change. That is leadership, not branding.

### Check F — Groups become teams (when the unit of work is more than one person)

CLO 6: qualities to manage **or** lead in a team/group environment. Unit 1 already drew the line; Unit 6 works it.

| | Group | Team |
|---|---|---|
| How it forms | Often organic (shared interest) | Strategic (selected and trained) |
| Goal | May be "share and discuss" | Common performance goal + mutual accountability |
| Atmosphere this school wants | Relaxed enough that people can actually talk | Same atmosphere *under stress* — that is the hard part |
| Leader job | Help a group **become** a team | Keep dynamics productive; do not skip storming |

Tuckman (glossary; adjourning not listed): **forming** (meet, test membership, seek leadership) → **storming** (goals and process contested; leadership tested; **do not skip**) → **norming** (roles, norms, shared values) → **performing** (identity, efficient communication, trust, interdependence).

Cohesion is glue. High cohesion + high task commitment = the cell you want. High cohesion + "don't make waves" = groupthink. Fault lines (glossary) split the group into subgroups; diversity (glossary: how people are similar or different) improves team performance **in this school** when it is managed, not when it is announced. Similarity-attraction (glossary) is the hiring bias that kills that gain.

Design: size (small enough that loafing is visible), complementary skills, named tasks, communication channels (Unit 6b), virtual-team extra structure (glossary: different geography, technology-dependent). Self-managing teams (glossary) are accountable for teaching others about their actions — they still need conflict skill and an external coordinator.

Conflict five-pack (glossary): avoiding, forcing, collaborating, compromising, accommodating. Dual-concern logic: collaborate when both the relationship and the issue matter; force when safety/time is the issue; avoid when it is not the hill. Negotiation = redefining the terms of interdependence.

Leadership effects on teams (Unit 6d): small-group communication, high-performance communication, virtual-team presence. The same theory families in Check B apply; SLT and path-goal are the ones that *change with the team's readiness*. Transformational helps change commitment; transactional keeps the scoreboard honest.

If asked "how do we fix this team?," name (1) group vs team, (2) Tuckman stage, (3) cohesion × task commitment, (4) a fault line or size problem, (5) one leadership move that matches Check B. Do not start with a ropes course.

## 2. Worked shape (not an exam item)

Prompt shape: "I don't have the title. We have to move the team to a new process and they are dug in."

Run the checks, short:

- A: this is enlistment (lead) plus a control system (manage). Both.
- B: situation is unfavorable (weak position power, unstructured new process) — directive enough to unfreeze, then path-goal supportive. Do not announce "I am a transformational leader."
- C: unfreeze first (why the current process fails; small coalition). The decision method should buy commitment (consult/facilitate), not a solo edict, unless safety clocks are running.
- D: name expert and referent bases; do not fake legitimate. Aim for internalization of the new process, not a week of compliance.
- E: if the old process is an underlying assumption ("this is how we do things"), you are in a culture change, not a SOP swap. Change a reward/artifact, not just the slide deck.
- F: they are probably a group in storming, or a team with a fault line. Size, named tasks, don't skip the argument.
