# OB checklists (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks what organizational behavior is, which level a problem sits on, why someone is (not) performing, how a group/team is functioning, what the culture is doing, or how to handle conflict or a negotiation in the standard undergrad OB school.

**Do not use when:** the job is reconstructing or taking a Saylor (or any) exam, dumping Robbins or McShane, running BUS208 POLC-as-the-manager's-job checklists, treating the prompt as BUS640 / BUS401, writing a mediation script to trap a counterpart, or doing I-O test construction.

This is **one** skill. It is not "Unit 1 skill + Unit 2 skill…". BUS209's six units are one argument: **behavior in organizations is diagnosed at three levels**; motivation is how the individual level actually moves; groups are the meso vehicle; power is influence; conflict is the friction; culture/structure/change are the org-level container.

## 0. Hygiene

- Label **school**. This is the **standard undergraduate organizational-behavior intro** (Saylor BUS209; micro / meso / macro; Saylor *Organizational Behavior* v1.1). Shared with almost every undergrad OB survey. It is not the only OB theory (critical OB, practice theory, psychometrics). Say so if the user is not inside this course.
- Label **claim vs data**. NACE 2007 skill lists, Gallup engagement %, 2005 BLS job-change counts, Zappos/Trader Joe's/Nordstrom sidebars, PointCast 1997, WorldCom, DaimlerChrysler are **course-era illustrations**. Re-fetch if the user needs current numbers.
- OB ≠ common sense ≠ HR ≠ BUS208 management. BUS208 asks what the *manager's job* is (POLC). This skill asks how *people behave* inside that job. You will share names (Maslow, Tuckman, dual-concern) with BUS208; you will not collapse into POLC.
- No exam items. No all-rights-reserved chapter dumps. CC OER: summarize.

## 1. Procedure — answer an OB question in four checks

Run in order. Skip a check only if the user already constrained the frame.

### Check A — Three levels (always)

**Organizational behavior** is the systematic study and application of knowledge about how individuals and groups act within the organizations where they work. *Systematic* is the load-bearing word: you are a human, and that does not make you an instrument. Memory, framing, and what you happen to notice will lie.

Name the **level** before naming a theory. Causes often sit on a different level than the symptom.

| Level | Question | Typical objects | Failure if ignored |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Individual (micro)** | What does *this person* bring, see, and want? | Personality, values, perception, attitudes, motivation, stress, role conflict/ambiguity | You "motivate the team" while one person cannot do the job or does not see the same facts |
| **Group / team (meso)** | What does the *interaction* add or subtract? | Identity, norms, Tuckman stage, cohesion, loafing, communication, process loss | You coach individuals inside a groupthink, a dominating member, or a collection that is not a team |
| **Organization (macro)** | What does the *container* reward, mean, and allow? | Structure, culture, power/politics, diversity climate, change system | You run a personality intervention on a matrix-with-two-bosses problem, or a culture that pays for the opposite of what it preaches |

**Quick group vs team (Unit 3 overlay):** a **group** is interacting people whose actions affect one another (formal or informal). A **team** is a small coalition with complementary skills, a common purpose and performance goals, a shared approach, and **mutual accountability**. A book club is a group. A collection of people with a fluctuating goal is still a group. Process loss = anything in the interaction that makes the output less than the sum of the parts.

If asked "what should we *do*?," name the level the cause sits on, then the level you can actually intervene on. Do not skip to Maslow.

**Tuckman (only when a new or stuck group is in play):** forming (polite, uncertain) → storming (conflict; **do not skip**) → norming (rules) → performing (work + process) → adjourning (debrief). Groups that skip storming often return to it late. Punctuated equilibrium: long static stretch, then a burst — plan for the midpoint, not a smooth climb.

Cohesion is glue. High cohesion + high task commitment = the cell you want. High cohesion + "don't make waves" = groupthink or a loafing party. Social loafing grows with size; counter with named tasks, visible evaluation, "you are needed," small enough teams (work teams usually ≤10; 2–20 is the outer teaching band).

### Check B — Motivation (when effort, performance, pay, "they don't care," or incentives are in play)

Write the identity **before** naming Maslow:

**Performance ≈ motivation × ability × environment**, given **accurate role perceptions**.

**Motivation** = desire to hit a goal, producing goal-directed behavior. Motivated ≠ high performer. If ability is missing, train or redesign. If the environment (tools, staffing, two bosses, a printer) is the constraint, fix the environment — do not "motivate" it.

There is **no grand theory**. **Content** theories ask *what is missing*. **Process** theories ask *how they are thinking about the situation*. Use both; pick the one the symptom points at.

**Content (need) — diagnose the missing fuel:**

| Theory | Use | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Maslow | Hierarchy as a reminder that unmet lower needs crowd out higher ones | Not a personality test; not a proven workplace ladder; not one-need-at-a-time |
| ERG (Alderfer) | Existence / relatedness / growth; **frustration-regression** (blocked growth → extra relatedness or pay-seeking) | More than one need at once — that is the point |
| Two-factor (Herzberg) | **Hygiene** (pay, conditions, policy, supervision) prevents dissatisfaction; **motivators** (achievement, the work itself, recognition, responsibility, growth) create satisfaction | Dumping money on a boring job is hygiene. Enrich the job if you want motivation. Pay can still be symbolic (status, fairness). |
| Acquired needs (McClelland) | nAch / nAff / nPow — match task and climate | High nAch often makes a weak *manager* (won't delegate, coaching feels like waste). High nAff avoids hard feedback. nPow is useful when socialized, not when it is personal prestige. |

**Process — diagnose the story they are telling:**

| Theory | Ask |
|---|---|
| Equity / justice | Is the outcome/input ratio fair vs a comparable **referent**? **Distributive** (what they got) *and* **procedural** (voice, notice, consistency, explanation) *and* **interactional** (dignity). Underpayment → less effort, worse quality, distortion, new referent, exit, or legal. Overpayment guilt is weakly supported. Justice is perceived. |
| Expectancy | Do they believe effort → performance (**E**), performance → outcome (**I**), and that the outcome is wanted (**V**)? Break any link and effort dies. Manager: train and clear the path (E); actually pay for performance and say so (I); ask what they value / offer a menu (V). Rotating "employee of the month" kills I. |
| Goal-setting | Specific + difficult + accepted + feedback. "Do your best" is not a goal. SMART is the course's spelling. |
| Reinforcement | What is actually being rewarded, ignored, or punished? You get the behavior you pay for, including the accidental kind (Kerr: rewarding A while hoping for B). Positive and negative reinforcement both *increase* behavior; extinction removes the payoff; punishment adds a cost. Variable-ratio sticks; continuous is clearer. |

**Job design as the environment term (ch.6 overlay):** skill variety, task identity, significance, autonomy, feedback (JCM). Autonomy without skill is abandonment. Incentives without justice become equity problems.

If they "just don't care," run ability / role perception / environment **before** a content theory. Then one content + one process. Do not start with a poster of Maslow's pyramid.

### Check C — Culture (when "how we do things," fit, merger, ethics climate, diversity, or change is in play)

**Culture** = shared assumptions, values, and beliefs that tell people what is appropriate. It is a **control system** that beats a rulebook when the next problem is unique (service recovery: "use good judgment" vs a 400-page handbook).

**Schein layers — always name which layer you can see:**

| Layer | What it is | How you know |
|---|---|---|
| **Artifacts** | Visible: layout, dress, rituals, stories, the handbook card, who has a door | Cheap to copy, easy to fake |
| **Values** | Stated principles and goals | Mission statements that nobody uses are PR, not culture (Enron's values card is the caution) |
| **Assumptions** | Taken-for-granted beliefs about people and reality | You notice them when you *leave*, or when two cultures merge |

Fit the culture to the **environment**. Innovative / adaptable cultures belong in turbulent work; stable / rule-oriented cultures belong where reliability is the product. A strong culture (widely shared) is an asset when the values are right *and* the world is stable. It is a liability when the values are wrong (outcome-obsession + no ethics), when the world shifted, or when two strong cultures are mashed together (DaimlerChrysler as the teaching clash).

OCP-style labels (use the one the prompt needs; do not recites all seven): innovative, aggressive, outcome-oriented, stable, people-oriented, team-oriented, detail-oriented; plus **service** and **safety** as add-ons. Subcultures are real (sales floor ≠ warehouse). Countercultures can be tolerated while they produce; they are a threat when they become the unofficial firm.

**How culture persists (do not "poster" a new value):**

1. Founder values that *worked* + industry demands.
2. **ASA** — attraction, selection, attrition. The culture hires itself.
3. Onboarding / stories / rituals.
4. Leader role-modeling (what they ask in the meeting *is* the culture).
5. Reward systems — what you pay for, rank, and ignore.

**Change skeleton:** urgency → change the people who are the old culture → role-model → train → **change the rewards** → new symbols and stories. Pair with Check A's org level and a new **standard**, or it snaps back (Lewin unfreeze–change–refreeze). Burning the 800-page manual only works if the next 80 pages and the next bonus plan agree.

Diversity sits here as a constraint on all three levels (LO 3 / 11): benefits are not automatic; unmanaged diversity has process-loss costs; national culture (power distance, collectivism) changes which conflict style and which justice rule feel fair. "We hired diversely" is an artifact. Integration is the culture move.

Structure is the other org-level container (LO 10): mechanistic vs organic; functional / divisional / matrix. Matrix is a **conflict machine** unless the two bosses are aligned. Formal chart vs informal network — culture lives in the informal.

### Check D — Conflict (when disagreement, negotiation, "they're fighting," or "nobody speaks up" is in play)

**Conflict** = a process of people disagreeing. It is not a failure by default. Insults are not productive; a real debate can be.

**Type (name it):**

- **Intrapersonal** — role conflict, role ambiguity, "I am not enough for this task."
- **Interpersonal** — two people (style, values, competition).
- **Intergroup** — departments, union–management, two suppliers, two merged seniority lists.

**Task vs relationship:** moderate **task** conflict (how we do the work, what the idea is) can raise quality. **Relationship** conflict (who you are) does not. Inverted-U: too little (silence, groupthink) and too much (attacks, paralysis) both kill performance. If there is *no* idea-conflict, stimulate it (devil's advocate, permission to disagree, compete on proposals). Keep it on the problem, not the person.

**Causes to check before picking a style:** structure (two bosses), scarce resources, task interdependence, incompatible goals (sales bonus vs shipping cost), personality, communication (lost email is enough).

**Dual-concern grid — match style to stakes × relationship:**

| Style | Assert × coop | When | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Avoid** | Low / low | Trivial, cooling-off, no power | Habit → fester |
| **Accommodate** | Low / high | They care more; relationship is the asset | Perpetual self-erasure |
| **Compromise** | Mid / mid | Split the difference, time is short | Nobody's real need met; used as a substitute for talking |
| **Compete** | High / low | Ethics line, emergency, zero-sum and you must not lose | Default manager move; trains subordinates to hide |
| **Collaborate** | High / high | Both goals matter and you have time | Not free; fake-collaborate is compete with extra meetings |

There is no one best style. Collaboration is often the quality move when both sides' goals are real. It is not a personality.

**If it is a negotiation** (often someone outside the firm; prep is the difference):

1. Investigate — what do *you* actually want, and what will you not concede?
2. **BATNA** — best alternative to a negotiated agreement. Improve yours; do not announce it if it is weak. The better BATNA is the better chair.
3. Present facts that support the position.
4. Bargain — concessions are expected, not weakness. Ask *why* a constraint exists (shipping vs manufacturing).
5. Close — agreement or walk. A rejected deal is information; sometimes you can reopen.

Distributive = split a fixed pie. **Integrative** = enlarge the pie so both goals fit under a bigger umbrella. Mistakes: take the first offer, ego, fantasy targets, flooding emotion, yesterday's failed deal. Mediation = facilitated agreement; arbitration = binding award. Ethics: do not take the whole pie if you want another deal; Platinum Rule.

Power (Unit 4 overlay, only when influence is the question): bases (legitimate, reward, coercive, expert, referent). Influence ≠ manipulation. Politics = informal influence; not automatically dirty, not automatically fine. Socialized power that changes the workplace is the course's preferred form. Do not write a capture-the-committee guide.

## 2. How to answer

- Start from Check A unless the user already named the level. A motivation speech with no group or culture diagnosis is not an answer in this school if the cause is meso/macro.
- Use the table the check provides. Do not recites all four checks if only one is in play.
- Neighbor courses: **BUS208** is POLC (the manager's *job*). **BUS210** is the communication channel. **BUS403 / BUS641** go deeper on negotiation. **BUS640** is the graduate overlay. **BUS401** is strategic leadership as capstone. Do not import their depth unless asked.

## 3. Worked micro-patterns

**"What is OB / why does it matter?"**
Check A in one paragraph. Definition. Name the three levels with one example each. Add: systematic, not common sense. Friction costs money; some friction is useful.

**"Why won't they work?"**
Check B identity first (ability / role perception / environment / motivation). Then direction of effort vs missing need vs broken E-I-V or inequity. If the bonus plan pays for volume and you wanted quality, that is Kerr, not laziness.

**"This team is a mess."**
Check A meso: group or team? Which Tuckman stage? Cohesion vs task commitment? Loafing vs dominating member vs no contract? Do not skip storming by forcing "nice."

**"That's just how we do things here."**
Check C. Artifact, value, or assumption? Fit to environment? Strong-and-wrong? What do the rewards actually pay for? ASA explains why posters fail.

**"They're fighting / nobody will disagree."**
Check D. Type. Task vs relationship. Inverted-U (maybe you need *more* idea-conflict). Then one style from the grid. If it is a deal, BATNA before tactics.

**"We're merging / changing."**
Org level of A + C change skeleton + D (intergroup conflict, incompatible goals, two seniority lists). Culture clash is the foreseeable cost; speed without a new standard is a speech.
