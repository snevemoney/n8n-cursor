# American government: foundations, then behavior, then institutions (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks how to **place a U.S. government question** in the Saylor POLSC232 school — Constitution/federalism/culture, who participates and who organizes, the three branches, rights vs liberties, or which policy type.

**Do not use when:** POLSC101 discipline survey; POLSC201 Plato-to-Mill; POLSC221 comparing systems; POLSC402 global justice; ENGL; PSYCH; exam reconstruction.

This is **one** skill. Six live units, one argument: **most students already have a news-cycle / branch-trivia story; this course's pass is US system as structure plus process — foundations first, then behavior and organizations, then institutions, rights, then policy types.**

## 0. Hygiene

- Label **school**. Undergraduate **American government** (Saylor POLSC232). **31 h / 3.1 CEU.** LMS **726** (`/course/polsc232`). ACE **N**. Cert **70% / 14-day / max 3**. Syllabus **2025-10-03 15:16**. No guest-visible textbook.
- Six **live** units: American Political Foundations; American Political Behavior; Political Parties, Campaigns, Elections, and Interest Groups; American Political Institutions; Civil Rights and Civil Liberties; Policymaking in American Government.
- Six **live** CLOs.

## 1. Procedure — six checks

### Check A — Identity

| Prompt smells like | Hand off |
|---|---|
| Power/IR as *intro survey* | **POLSC101** |
| Hobbes/Locke as canon | **POLSC201** |
| Comparing democracies | **POLSC221** |
| Global justice as a course | **POLSC402** |
| Take the final | **refuse** |
| How the *US* system works | **this skill** |

### Check B — Foundations (Unit 1)

Political culture, Constitution development, federalism. Not POLSC201's influence job as the whole SKU.

### Check C — Behavior (Unit 2)

Socialization, public opinion, media, participation — a *relationship*, not four isolated vocab words.

### Check D — Organizations (Unit 3)

Interest groups and parties on campaigns and elections. Four levers, one unit.

### Check E — Institutions and rights (Units 4–5)

Executive, Legislative, Judicial: roles, functions, structure. **Civil rights ≠ civil liberties** and their impact on American politics.

### Check F — Policy (Unit 6)

Define public policy. Distinguish **economic, domestic, and foreign**. Foreign here is US policymaking, not IR survey.

## 2. Typical traps

- Branches trivia without foundations.
- Rights = liberties.
- Policy as one lump.
- POLSC101 or POLSC221 reused as this map.
- Constitution as POLSC201 thought.
- Foreign policy as POLSC402.
- ACE invented. 31 h ignored.

## 3. Refuse

Exam reconstruction; inventing SAYA; sitting the final; ENGL/PSYCH harvest.

## 4. Adjacent

POLSC101, POLSC201, POLSC221, POLSC401/402, ACE sitting (none).

## 5. Output shape

1. Name the school (POLSC232 American government) and what it is not.
2. Name the **unit seat**.
3. Keep **rights ≠ liberties** and **economic/domestic/foreign** if policy.
4. Do not invent ACE. Do not sit the final.
