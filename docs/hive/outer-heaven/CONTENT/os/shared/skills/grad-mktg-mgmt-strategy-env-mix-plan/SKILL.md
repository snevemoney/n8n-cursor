# Graduate marketing management: strategy → environment → mix → plan (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks how a marketing manager or upper-level marketing executive should use marketing to develop and execute the firm's business strategy — how marketing nests in strategic planning, how to scan the environment and the firm's resources/capabilities, who to segment/target/position/brand across consumer vs organizational vs service markets, how to spend product/price/promotion/place under a dynamic environment, or how to write a marketing plan that aligns with mission, vision, and stated strategic objectives.

**Do not use when:** the job is reconstructing or taking a Saylor (or any) exam, dumping Tanner / OpenStax / Kotler / Aaker / Keller originals, running **BUS203** undergraduate principles-of-marketing checklists ("what *is* marketing vs advertising"), treating the prompt as **BUS502**'s five-unit undergrad *plan overlay* (research → CB → brand → digital comms as the course), treating it as **BUS501** firm-level strategy (VRIN, five forces, Porter generics as the *course*), answering **BUS630** CB-lab / **BUS631** brand-architecture / **BUS632** ad-ops / **BUS633** sales management / **BUS634** research-methods / **BUS615** international-marketing depth, treating the free 70% certificate as **MBA602** or **MMKT601**'s 80% proctored exam, promising ACE/transfer credit, or writing deceptive-ad / fake-engagement / dark-pattern scripts.

This is **one** skill. It is not "Unit 1 skill + Unit 2 skill…". BUS602's eight live units are one argument: **marketing management is how the marketing manager spends the mix, under a scanned environment and the firm's capabilities, so the plan executes the firm's strategy.**

## 1. Procedure — answer a graduate marketing-management question in six checks

Run in order. Skip a check only if the user already constrained the frame.

### Check A — Identity, level, overlay (always)

**The course question:** is the user asking for a **principles recap**, an **undergrad plan overlay**, a **firm-strategy capstone**, or a **marketing manager's decision** that is supposed to develop and execute the firm's business strategy?

Live test:

| Prompt smell | Hand off |
|---|---|
| "What is marketing vs advertising / run STP for intro homework" | **BUS203** |
| "Write a strategic marketing plan (environment → brand → digital comms) that builds on principles" | **BUS502** |
| "Should the *firm* acquire / cost-lead / VRIN this resource" | **BUS501** |
| "Should we spend this mix, for this target, given this environment and these capabilities, nested in this mission" | **this skill** |

**Primary goal:** marketing that **creates and captures value in a way that executes the firm's strategy** — not "maximize this quarter's awareness," not "post more," not "keep every stakeholder equally happy." Mission, vision, resources, risk, law, and ethics constrain the path.

**Think as a marketing manager / upper-level marketing executive** (live intro). CLO 3: unstructured situations, **incomplete information**. Name what you do not know; do not wait for a perfect brief.

**Level check:** if the prompt is actually undergrad homework in BUS203's seven units, **hand off BUS203** and recap only. If it is BUS502's five-unit overlay, **hand off BUS502**. If it is MBA602's or MMKT601's proctored 80% exam, **refuse exam reconstruction**; this skill is the *content* overlay, not the degree exam.

### Check B — Nest in strategy; scan environment and capabilities (Units 1–2)

CLO 1, 2, 4, 9. Before picking a P, name **where marketing sits** and **what it is allowed to do**.

**Nest the plan (OpenStax/Tanner, named split):**

| Level | Question | Owner | Hand off |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Corporate** | Which businesses / markets? | Top / board | **BUS501** |
| **Business / SBU** | How do we compete *here*? | BU GM | **BUS501** generics if that is the real question |
| **Functional — marketing** | How will we create, communicate, deliver, and exchange value so the SBU's objectives happen? | Marketing manager | **this skill** |

OpenStax 2.2 overlay (summarize): marketers (1) keep a **marketing philosophy** in the firm's plan, (2) gather the **current situation** (gap: where we are / where we want to be / what blocks us), (3) identify **environmental trends** and their impact. Without customers, the finest plant does not ring.

**Environment (external, mostly uncontrollable — CLO 9):** PESTEL (political, economic, social, technological, environmental, legal) plus industry/competitive scan. **SWOT**: S/W internal, O/T external. Do not list a *move* as an opportunity; do not mix internal and external; SWOT is brainstorming, not a selection algorithm.

**Resources and capabilities (internal — live Unit 2 title):** what can this firm actually do, with whose assets, better than rivals *in this market*? That is the constraint on the 4Ps. **Hand off BUS501** the moment they need VRIN / five forces / generic strategy as a *course*. Stay here for "this capability set is why we can (or cannot) support that positioning."

**Named allocation tools** (manager depth, not a strategy seminar):

- **BCG:** star / cash cow / question mark / dog — scarce marketing and R&D dollars. Dogs are divestiture candidates; cash cows fund stars and selected question marks.
- **Ansoff growth paths:** penetration / product development / market development / diversification. Diversification is the riskiest path; do not pick it because it sounds like "strategy."
- **SMART** goals vs objectives. Monitor on a **dashboard** of KPIs (Unit 8 will lock them; do not wait until the campaign to invent them).

**Ethics floor starts here (CLO 9):** the uncontrollable environment includes law, culture, and society. A growth path that trips a regulator is a dead plan.

If asked "what should we do?," refuse to pick a channel or a price until **nest, environment, capability, target, and differentiation** are named.

### Check C — Who: STP, brand, and which market (Units 3–4)

CLO 4 (buying behavior), 6, 7 (branding/packaging). Pick *who* before *how*.

**STP:**

1. **Segment** on bases buyers actually differ on. CLO 6 names **demographic and psychographic** plus consumer vs **industrial** goods classifications. Also behavioral / geographic as the usual extra bases.
2. **Target** with attractiveness tests: size, growth, crowding, access, resources, **mission fit** (Check B nest). Width: mass / differentiated / niche / one-to-one.
3. **Position** on a perceptual map using criteria *buyers* use. **Differentiate**: why this offering is not a substitute (USP). A target without a +D is a mailing list.

**Brand** is a managerial asset (awareness, associations, perceived quality, loyalty, marks) — not a color palette. Packaging is a CLO-7 named lever. **Hand off BUS631** for architecture / measurement systems as a *course*. **Hand off BUS502** if they only need the undergrad brand-strategy overlay (Aaker/BAV named) without the rest of this mix.

**Three markets the manager must be able to decide in (live Unit 4):**

| Market | Manager question | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| **Consumer** | Need → search → evaluate → buy → postpurchase; involvement changes search depth; culture/social/personal/psychological | Treating stated reason as the only reason |
| **Organizational** | Derived demand; buying-center roles; straight / modified / new buy | Selling as if it were a household |
| **Service** | Intangibility / inseparability / variability / perishability (IHIP as taught); people / process / physical evidence as named extras | Running a goods mix on a service |

**Hand off BUS630** for experimental CB / full models. Stay here for "what the manager must know about this buyer to spend the mix."

### Check D — Spend the mix under the environment (Units 5–7)

CLO 5, 7, 8. The 4Ps are **how**, spent **in accordance with the firm's plan** while the environment moves (live intro). Do not let a P eat the nest.

**Product / PLC / NPD (Unit 5):**

- **PLC** as a management schedule: intro (make it known), growth (make it chosen), maturity (extend/repair without cheapening), decline (harvest or divest). The mix *changes* with the stage.
- **NPD** at name depth: idea → screen → concept → test → launch. Most new products fail; stage-gates exist because of that.
- Differentiation and packaging from CLO 7 live here as *offering* decisions that Unit 3's brand has to carry.

**Research as a managerial tool (Unit 5, CLO 5) — not a methods course:**

1. Define the **problem**. Symptoms ≠ causes. "Sales are down" is a symptom.
2. Write a research **objective** a manager can use.
3. **Secondary first**, then primary. Exploratory / descriptive / causal.
4. Skip when cost > benefit, time is gone, or the answer is already obvious (decorating a decision already made).
5. MIS / market intelligence = ongoing scan feeding Check B.

**Hand off BUS634** for instrument design / sampling theory. **Hand off BUS206 / BUS250 / BUS607** if the object is an IS or analytics pipeline. Stay here for "what would change the manager's decision."

**Place / channel / logistics (Unit 6, CLO 8):**

- Intensity: intensive / selective / exclusive — match the positioning (exclusive + mass advertising is a fight).
- Push vs pull. Channel conflict as a named risk. Logistics = cost *and* service.
- A channel that cannot deliver the positioning is a failed P.
- **Hand off BUS300 / BUS606** for ops/SCM as a *management course*.

**Promotion and pricing (Unit 7):**

- **IMC = one consistent message.** Mix as taught: advertising, personal selling, PR, sales promotion, direct, digital/social. Objectives: awareness → persuasion → action. Budget: prefer **objective-and-task**; percent-of-sales is easy and backwards. Digital is a vehicle set, not a replacement for positioning.
- **Price** is the only revenue P. Value-based vs cost-plus vs competition; skimming vs penetration on new products. A price that undoes Unit 3's differentiator is a failed P. Hidden fees / drip pricing fail CLO 9.
- **Hand off BUS632** ad-ops / performance media; **BUS633** sales management; **BUS210** audience/tone as message design; **COMM411** PR-as-profession.

### Check E — Write the plan that nests (Unit 8)

CLO 10 (+ 2, 5). The output is a **strategic marketing plan** aligned with **mission, vision, and stated objectives of the firm's strategic plan** (live intro). That nest is the graduate extra.

**Skeleton (enough to write it; do not dump a textbook chapter):** executive summary; mission/vision nest; situation (Check B); objectives (SMART + dashboard); STP / personas / positioning / brand (Check C); 4P strategy and tactics (Check D); implementation (who, when); budget / forecast; controls / audit. Forecasts are educated guesses; audit = strategy *as implemented*.

**Unstructured-problem test (CLO 3):** if they cannot name target, differentiation, mix, metric, and nest, it is not yet a plan. "Grow 40%" is a **goal**. Price cut / logo refresh / TikTok are candidate **hows**.

### Check F — Does it survive law, ethics, and incomplete information? (always before you lock)

CLO 9 is not a last chapter. CLO 3 says you will not have a perfect brief.

- Environment scan already includes **legal** (PESTEL-L). If the growth path trips a regulator, it is a dead plan.
- Research: consent, privacy, no espionage.
- CB: do not exploit a vulnerable need as a segment "insight."
- Brand: reputation you cannot defend in public is not equity.
- Mix: no deceptive claims, fake scarcity, fake reviews, undisclosed sponsorships, drip pricing, dark patterns.
- Incomplete information: name the gap; decide whether Unit 5 research is worth it; do not invent facts.

If the only way to hit the KPI is a CLO-9 failure, **change the plan**, do not write a cleverer tactic. Re-fetch current statutes; do not invent compliance procedures for evasion.

## 2. Worked micro-patterns

**"What is marketing management / how is it not principles?"**
Check A in one paragraph. Past vs decide-what-to-do-as-a-manager. Hook: marketing develops and executes the firm's business strategy. Name the overlays: BUS203 teaches the toolkit; BUS502 spends a plan overlay; BUS501 positions the firm; this course *spends the full mix nested in strategy*.

**"Should we cut price, refresh the logo, or go hard on TikTok to grow 40%?"**
Checks B–E. 40% is a goal. Price / logo / TikTok are hows. Name nest, environment, capability, target, +D before picking a how. A price cut can un-stick a differentiator. A logo without equity is theater. TikTok is a vehicle (Check D promo), not a strategy. Then Check E: can the plan be resourced and audited. Check F: disclosure / targeting.

**"We're entering a new country / a new SBU."**
Check B first (Ansoff market development vs diversification; BCG question-mark spend). Global overlay is CLO 1/4 — **hand off BUS615** if they need the international-marketing *course*. Stay here for "does this environment + capability set support this mix."

**"What does the buyer actually do / B2B buying center / this is a service."**
Check C. Then spend Check D's mix *for that market* (services get people/process/physical evidence). Hand off BUS630 if they want a CB lab.

**"Should we launch this product / where is it on the PLC?"**
Check D product + Check B capability + Check C target. PLC stage changes the mix. NPD stage-gates exist because most launches fail. Research (problem first) before you decorate a launch already decided.

**"Which channel / exclusive vs intensive?"**
Check D Place, matched to Check C positioning. Hand off BUS300/BUS606 if the real object is warehouse math.

**"Walk me through undergrad 4Ps / STP as seven units."**
Wrong course. **BUS203**. Recap a term only if this prompt still needs it.

**"Write the strategic marketing plan overlay (research → CB → brand → digital comms)."**
Wrong course. **BUS502**. Stay here only if they also need resources/capabilities, services, PLC/NPD, channels/logistics, and pricing as *one manager's mix nested in corporate strategy*.

**"Should the firm cost-lead / acquire / VRIN this?"**
Wrong course. **BUS501**.

**"FX / standardization vs adaptation as the course."**
Wrong course. **BUS615**.
