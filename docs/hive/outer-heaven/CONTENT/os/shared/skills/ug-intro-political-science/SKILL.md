# Intro political science: concept first, then institution, then IR (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks how to **place a political-science intro question** in the Saylor POLSC101 school — power vs legitimacy vs authority, how people participate and what they think, which ideology or political-economy origin, which state/institutional type, or IR as law/IOs/conflict.

**Do not use when:** POLSC201+ as the whole course; GEOG101 regions; HIST103 dates; PHIL103 Rawls/Kant; ENGL; PSYCH101; reconstructing or sitting exams.

This is **one** skill. Six live units, one argument: **most students already have a US-civics story; this course's pass is comparative intro — concepts, then participation, then ideologies, then the state, then institutions, then international politics.**

## 0. Hygiene

- Label **school**. Undergraduate **introduction to political science** (Saylor POLSC101). Social Science / Political Science. **111 h / 11.1 CEU.** LMS **46** (`/course/polsc101`). ACE **absent** — do not invent a SAYA id. Free 70% certificate is **not** transfer credit. Cert **14-day / max 3**. Syllabus edit **2025-10-03 15:12**. No guest-visible textbook. Primary: articles, lectures, videos.
- Six **live** units: Foundational Concepts of Politics; Participation and Public Opinion; Ideologies; The State; Political Institutions; International Politics.
- Fifteen **live** CLOs (power/legitimacy/authority through causes of international conflict).
- Neighbors: POLSC201+ are **other courses**. GEOG101 maps regions. HIST103 dates 400 years. PHIL103 is justice philosophy.

## 1. Procedure — six checks

### Check A — Identity

| Prompt smells like | Hand off |
|---|---|
| World *regions* / maps | **GEOG101** |
| 1648 / 1789 / 1914 survey | **HIST103** |
| Rawls / Kant as moral philosophy | **PHIL103** |
| Comparative politics as a *later* SKU | **POLSC201** |
| Take the Saylor final | **refuse** |
| Power, participation, regime type, IR intro | **this skill** |

### Check B — Concepts (Unit 1)

Power ≠ legitimacy ≠ authority. Nation-state as *development* (leaks Unit 4). Approaches: ideologies and political economy as *ways of studying*, not yet Unit 3's origin-stories in full.

### Check C — People (Unit 2)

Socialization ≠ participation ≠ public opinion. Comparative participation. Electoral systems (implications) vs parties vs party systems — three jobs.

### Check D — Ideologies (Unit 3)

Four origin-stories: **democratic capitalism, democratic socialism, communism, fascism.** Do not collapse to left/right. Political economy lives here with CLO 3.

### Check E — State and institutions (Units 4–5)

Nation-state origins/development. Presidential vs parliamentary. Authoritarian vs totalitarian. Institutions are typed comparatively, not "the US Congress unit."

### Check F — International (Unit 6)

International law; major IOs; causes of conflict. Not HIST103's war survey and not GEOG101's regions.

## 2. Typical traps

- Authority as a synonym for power.
- Electoral system = party system.
- Presidential = democratic; parliamentary = European trivia.
- Authoritarian = totalitarian.
- Four -isms collapsed to two.
- IR as history or geography.
- ACE invented. 111 h ignored. POLSC201 treated as this map.
- ENGL/PSYCH collision.

## 3. Refuse

Exam reconstruction; inventing SAYA; sitting the final; harvesting ENGL* or PSYCH101; writing under GEOG101/PHIL*/HIST*.
