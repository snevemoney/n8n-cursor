# American Renaissance: context, then form, then the slavery question (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks how to place an 1830s–1860s US literature question in the Saylor ENGL405 school — Transcendentalism, poetic form, short story, novel, nature/technology and identity, women’s place, or slavery/abolitionist writing.

**Do not use when:** ENGL095/101/102/210; ARTH101; ESL*; ESLHub; CS120; sitting ACE; exam reconstruction; sending; dumping Emerson/Thoreau/Whitman/Hawthorne/Melville/Dickinson.

This is **one** skill. Seven live units, one argument: **the American Renaissance (as Matthiessen labeled it) is US writers 1830s–1860s inventing a distinct literature — start from context and Transcendentalism, then track form (poem, short story, novel), then identity under nature/technology and gender, then the slavery question. Name writers. Do not paste their books.**

### Check B — Context + Transcendentalism (Unit 1; CLOs 1–4)

Economic, technological, educational, social, cultural, religious transformations. Second Great Awakening. Transcendentalism vs Romanticism. Emerson’s place vs Thoreau, Fuller, the Ripleys.

### Check C — Poetic form (Unit 2; CLOs 5–6)

Competing ideas of poetry: Poe, Emerson, Whitman. Dickinson as a nineteenth-century woman writer: form and content. Name; do not dump poems.

### Check D — Short story + novel (Units 3–4; CLOs 7–8)

Short story, Gothic, crime fiction: Hawthorne, Poe, Melville. Novel forms: Hawthorne, Lippard and Thompson, Fern.

### Check E — Identity, gender, slavery (Units 5–7; CLOs 9–13)

Industrialization/capitalism as threat (Melville, Brownson, Davis, Thoreau). Thoreau’s nature + politics vs Emerson. Conventional gender roles; sentimentalism constrained and empowered (Fern, Anthony, Fuller, Alcott, Stowe). Slavery question on major writers and abolitionist literature.

### Check F — Never send / never dump

Encode the job. Stop. Do not paste chapters.
