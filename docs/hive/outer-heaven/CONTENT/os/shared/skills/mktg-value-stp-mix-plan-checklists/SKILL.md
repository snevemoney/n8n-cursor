# Principles of marketing: value, STP, mix, plan (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks what marketing is (vs advertising or sales), how value is created for a customer, who to segment and target, how to position against rivals, how to run a research process, how to shape an offering through its life cycle, how to set price as the revenue P, how to pick a channel, how to assemble a promotion mix (including social), or how to write a marketing plan with a budget and a forecast.

**Do not use when:** the job is reconstructing or taking a Saylor (or any) exam, dumping Tanner or OpenStax, running **BUS101** survey-only 4Ps / “why study business,” **BUS107** frontline customer-service scripts, **BUS210** audience/purpose/channel/tone/feedback as message design, **BUS300 / BUS606** operations or end-to-end supply-chain *management*, **BUS502** strategic marketing planning overlay, **BUS602** marketing management, **BUS630** consumer-behavior depth, **BUS631** brand equity, **BUS632** digital advertising ops, **BUS633** sales management, **BUS634** research methods, **PRDV217** intro sales closing, or dark-pattern / deceptive-ad coaching.

This is **one** skill. It is not "Unit 1 skill + Unit 2 skill…". BUS203's seven units are one argument: **marketing creates, communicates, delivers, and exchanges customer-determined value** — STP picks who can get a positive personal-value equation, research learns them, the mix (offering / PLC / channel / price) and one IMC message (social included) deliver the exchange, and the plan is how you write it down with a budget and a forecast.

## 1. Procedure — answer a principles-of-marketing question in five checks

Run in order. Skip a check only if the user already constrained the frame.

### Check A — Identity: value, not ads (always)

**Marketing ≠ advertising ≠ sales.** Advertising is paid, identified-sponsor communication to many people at once. Sales is personal, two-way, and closes exchanges. Marketing decides *which* offering, *who* it is for, *how* it is priced and delivered, *and* how it is communicated.

**AMA (as this school quotes it):** the activity, set of institutions, and processes for **creating, communicating, delivering, and exchanging** offerings that have value for customers, clients, partners, and society at large.

Map the four activities to the classic **4Ps** so you can talk either language:

| Activity (verb) | Classic P (noun) | What it forces you to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Creating | Product | What is the **offering** (good + service + price as one bundle)? |
| Communicating | Promotion | What do we say, and what do we learn back? |
| Delivering | Place | How does the offering actually reach and get used? |
| Exchanging | Price | What is traded (money, points, data, time) and who owns what after? |

The 4Ps alone understate the work (title transfer is part of exchange; “product” understates the experience). **Service-dominant logic** in this school: customers judge the *experience*, not “product vs service.”

**Personal value equation (load-bearing):**

**value = benefits received − [price + hassle]**

Hassle = time and effort of shopping and use. Value is **customer-determined**. Same dish, four diners, four values. A deal only works if the customer's equation is positive *and* the firm still profits.

**Marketing concept** = seek to satisfy wants and needs (market oriented). Not an excuse to fail to make money. Competing orientations this school names: **production** (“good products sell themselves”), **selling** (push/ads), **product** (innovation for its own sake). The named failure mode is still “pushing metal” while customers want value. “Great products sell themselves” is **false** here.

Who markets: for-profit B2C and B2B (makers, wholesalers, and retailers all market), **nonprofit marketing**, **social marketing** (behavior change — different from “sell enlistments”), and individuals (a résumé as a value pitch).

If the prompt is “write the greeting / handle the angry caller,” **stop and hand off BUS107**. If it is “pick a legal form / read an income statement,” **hand off BUS101**.

### Check B — Who: STP + competitive position

You do not market to “everyone.” **Segment** → **target** → **position**.

1. **Segment** buyers into groups similar enough that the same offering will appeal. Use **more than one** base:

| Base | Asks | Intro examples |
|---|---|---|
| Behavioral | How do they act toward the offering? | Benefits sought; usage rate; usage situation; loyalty / buyer status |
| Demographic | Who are they on paper? | Age/generation, income, gender, family life cycle, ethnicity, occupation, education |
| Geographic | Where are they? | Region, city size, density, climate; geodemographics / geocoding; proximity |
| Psychographic | How do they live and what do they value? | Activities, interests, opinions, attitudes, lifestyles (VALS as a *named commercial* scheme — label it) |

B2B: usually fewer need-based segments (price-focused / quality-and-brand / service-focused / partnership-focused). Buying centers have roles — name who is in the room (users, influencers, gatekeepers, deciders, buyers, initiators).

2. **Target** only what clears the attractiveness tests: sizeable enough given your costs; growing; not swamped (or you can stand out); accessible; you have the resources; it fits the mission. Then pick a width:

| Strategy | Means | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Mass / undifferentiated | Same offer to all (shotgun) | A rival with a rifle eats a segment |
| Differentiated / multisegment | Different mixes for different groups | Cost and brand blur |
| Concentrated / niche | Big fish, small pond | All eggs in one basket |
| One-to-one | Collaborate and customize | Data, cost, privacy |

Microtargeting / narrowcasting exists; **name the privacy issue**. Keeping a current customer is usually cheaper than finding a new one. Some customers are unprofitable to serve — say so; do not romanticize “everyone.”

3. **Position** = how buyers see you **relative to rivals**. Tools: perceptual map (plot on criteria *buyers* use; do not sit in the cluster); **USP** (the memorable difference); tagline as a short essence. **Reposition** only when you mean to move the product in the mind, not just redraw the logo. Competitive analysis (CLO 6): who else can deliver a similar personal-value equation, and on which map axes?

If the job is a consumer-behavior *experiment* or a brand-architecture system, **hand off BUS630 / BUS631**.

### Check C — Learn: customers and research

Before you spend mix dollars, know whether the need is real.

**Consumer path (intro depth):** need recognition → information search → evaluation of alternatives → purchase → postpurchase → disposal. Involvement (routine / limited / extended) changes search depth. Overlay situation (store, time, mood, social), psychology (motivation, perception, learning, attitude), and culture/class/reference groups/family life cycle. Enough to diagnose; not a CB dissertation.

**B2B path:** derived / fluctuating / joint demand; straight rebuy / modified rebuy / new buy; stages from need through supplier search, proposal, order routine, performance review. Ethics conflicts → **BUS200**.

**Research vs intelligence:**

- **Market intelligence** = ongoing scan (prices, buzz, regs). Legal and identified. Dumpster diving / hacking = espionage — refuse.
- **Marketing research** = project-based answer to a written objective. Secondary first; primary when you must.
- Process: **define the problem** (symptoms ≠ causes — New Coke's “win the taste test” is the caution) → design (exploratory / descriptive / causal) → forms (no leading or double-barreled questions) → sample (probability vs convenience; frame ≠ population) → collect → analyze (validity ≠ reliability; margin of error) → report (executive summary first for busy readers).
- Skip research when cost > benefit, time is gone, or the answer is already obvious. Do not research to decorate a decision already made.

MIS / clickstream / CRM are *inputs*. This is not BUS206 and not BUS250.

### Check D — Mix: offering, PLC, channel, price, IMC

Build the mix for the target you chose. Every P except price **costs** money; price is the only revenue P.

**Offering.** Good + service + price as one bundle. Branding/packaging/labeling at *offering* depth. NPD path when “new” is the job: ideas (employees, customers/lead users, suppliers, rivals) → screen (value, feasibility, fit; **investment risk vs opportunity risk**) → features (start from benefits) → develop → test (alpha lab, beta real users, market test of the *whole* plan) → launch (big-bang vs rolling) → evaluate. Kill bad ideas early; idea generation is the cheap step.

**PLC — match the mix to the stage:**

| Stage | Sales / rivals | Mix posture |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | Low; few rivals | Awareness + trial; skimming *or* penetration; high marketing cost |
| Growth | Rising; rivals enter | Differentiate; expand distribution; protect brand |
| Maturity | Level; crowded | Extend: new markets, new variants, mix repair; avoid pure price wars |
| Decline | Falling | Harvest (cut spend) or divest; prefer to modify in maturity |

**Channel / delivery.** Direct vs indirect; intensity = intensive / selective / exclusive. Pick from the **customer first**, then product, capabilities, rivals, environment. Name vertical vs horizontal conflict; channel leaders resolve. Supply chain (sourcing, forecasting, inventory, logistics, reverse logistics) is **delivery of value** here. Ops design → **BUS300 / BUS606**.

**Price.** Set the **objective** first (profit, sales, share, ROI, status quo). Then costs, customers, environment, rest of mix / PLC stage. Revenue must cover total cost. Named tactics: cost-plus, odd-even, prestige, bundling, going-rate, captive, sealed bid; skimming vs penetration at intro. Adjustments are short-term. CVP / make-or-buy as relevant *cost* → **BUS105**. Project NPV / WACC → **BUS202**.

**IMC — one consistent message.** Promo mix as taught: advertising, personal selling, PR, sponsorships/events, sales promotion, direct marketing, online/social. Objectives move awareness → persuasion → action. Budget: prefer **objective-and-task**; percent-of-sales is easy and backwards; all-you-can-afford and competitive parity are weaker defaults. Sales promo = action now; trade promo = push through the channel. PR ≠ guaranteed coverage. **Social media lives inside IMC** (CLO 10): interactive platforms, content marketing as a system (keywords, ongoing), email as **owned** media (you control list and content). Buzz ≠ society. Platform lists go stale — re-fetch. Digital ad ops → **BUS632**. Sales funnel management → **PRDV217 / BUS633**.

Ethics floor on the mix: no deceptive claims, fake scarcity, or undisclosed sponsorships.

### Check E — Write the plan (assembly)

A **marketing plan** is the functional-level document for creating, communicating, delivering, and exchanging. It flows from mission → objectives → strategy. Different targets can get different **value propositions** (30-second specific value vs the alternative).

Minimum skeleton this school expects:

1. Executive summary (market, offering, strategy, budget — readable alone)
2. Situation (customers, competitors, collaborators, climate / SWOT)
3. Goals (measurable)
4. Strategy (STP + value proposition + Ansoff choice if relevant)
5. Tactics (the mix from Check D)
6. Implementation / action plan (who, when)
7. Budget and forecast (educated guess; market potential ≠ sales potential; use more than one method; track and revise)
8. Controls / audit (how you will know causality — do not credit the wrong lever)
9. Exhibits

If they need a *strategic marketing planning process* with brand architecture and a digital portfolio, **hand off BUS502** (explicitly builds on BUS203). Stay here for the principles-level plan.

## 2. Worked micro-patterns

**"What is marketing / how is it not advertising?"**
Check A. Four activities + personal value equation. Advertising is one communication tool. Sales closes. Marketing chooses who and what.

**"Who should we sell to?"**
Check B. Name bases, run attractiveness tests, pick width (mass / differentiated / niche), draft a position vs named rivals. Refuse “everyone.”

**"Should we launch this / what do we call it?"**
Check C then D. Write a research objective; secondary first. Screen the idea for value and feasibility. Do not skip problem definition (New Coke caution).

**"What price / skim or penetrate?"**
Check D price + PLC stage. Objective first. Skim when you can sell value to the less price-sensitive early; penetrate when share and trial matter more. Cover total cost.

**"Where should it be sold / do we need a middleman?"**
Check D channel. Customer first. Intensive for convenience; exclusive for specialty. Name conflict if the channel already fights itself.

**"What should the campaign say / which social?"**
Check D IMC. One message; USP; objective-and-task budget. Social = a zone of IMC, platform-specific behavior, owned/paid/earned. Email is owned. Do not replace the plan with a TikTok tip list.

**"Write us a marketing plan."**
Check E, filled from A–D. Situation → STP → mix → budget/forecast → controls. Keep it principles-depth unless they asked for BUS502.

**"Handle this angry customer / write the apology email."**
Wrong course. **BUS107** or **BUS210**.

**"Optimize our Google Ads / rebuild brand architecture."**
Wrong course. **BUS632** / **BUS631**. **BUS502** if they need the strategic overlay.
