# Austrian capital–cycle checklists (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks whether a planner can calculate, how order emerges without design, what determines the interest rate, whether "printing credit" creates capital, why booms cluster into busts, whether security/IP are market goods, or what Bitcoin does to Austrian *method* — especially in an ECON104 / ABCT / Mises-Hayek / Ammous frame.

**Do not use when:** the job is taking or reconstructing a Saylor exam; defining value, the action axiom, diamond-water, or intro trade (that is `austrian-intro-checklists` / ECON103); operational Bitcoin (ECON135 / PRDV151); monetary-history layers (ECON120); mining/scaling puzzles (ECON105); or a pure ECON102 IS-LM / Phillips homework with no school overlay requested.

This is **one** skill. It is not "Unit 1 skill + Unit 2 skill…". ECON104's ten units are one argument: calculation and spontaneous order → time preference as the interest rate → money vs fiduciary credit → malinvestment cycle → applications (security, IP, Bitcoin-as-method).

If the user still needs subjective value / praxeology on-ramp, **hand off to `austrian-intro-checklists`**, then come back here for capital, interest, credit, cycle.

## 0. Hygiene

- **Label school.** This is the **Austrian School** as taught in Saylor ECON104 (Mises calculation + money/credit + ABCT, Hayek knowledge/spontaneous order, Rothbard treatise, Hoppe security overlay, Kinsella IP, Ammous pedagogy). It is **not** Keynesian, **not** Walrasian/DSGE, **not** Lange-Lerner market socialism, **not** Chartalist/MMT, **not** ECON103-only intro.
- Mises called the science **value-free**: it traces consequences of action. Hoppe/Kinsella/Rothbard-anarchist layers are **normative overlays**. Keep them labeled.
- **Ordinal, not cardinal.** No constants in human action. No "the Taylor rule should be…" from this skill.
- **Claim vs data.** ABCT is a causal sketch, not a calibrated DSGE. S2F is a numerical model that the course itself flags as a possible *challenge to method*. IP-kills-innovation and "private defense works" are school claims, not meta-analyses you should launder as consensus.
- In-school splits to surface, not hide: Mises-impossibility vs Hayek-knowledge; 100% reserve vs free banking; Rothbard-copyright vs Kinsella-no-IP.
- No investment advice. No exam items. Do not dump copyrighted treatise text.

## 1. Procedure — run four checklists

Run in order unless the user already constrained the frame. Skip a checklist only when the question is already inside it. U2/U8/U9/U10 are satellites after the four.

### Checklist A — Capital-market calculation (Units 1–2; LO 1)

**Question this checklist answers:** can this social order **know** whether a *capital* plan wastes resources — and does order have to be designed?

ECON103 already stated the calculation problem (property + money prices + P&L). Here you run it on **higher-order goods** and add Hayek's knowledge problem **without collapsing the two**.

| # | Test | Fail → |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Private property** in the relevant capital goods / factors? | No owner, no bid; "shadow prices" are accounting theater |
| A2 | **Money prices** of those *particular* factors, formed from many ordinal scales? | Labor-hour totals, energy-hour totals, a bureau's tableau |
| A3 | Owners can **profit or lose** on alternative uses over **time**? | Residual absorbed by a planner who cannot go bankrupt the same way |
| A4 | Is the proposed fix a **socialist workaround**? (labor tickets, computers, Lange trial-and-error, "AI will compute the optimum") | Still no discovery of opportunity cost in a common unit *without bids* |
| A5 | Is the remaining issue **calculation** (Mises: no factor prices) or **knowledge** (Hayek: data never in one mind)? | Do not say "Hayek = Mises with nicer prose" |

**Spontaneous order (A5 companion, Unit 2):**
Regularities (money, language, prices, hub-and-spoke) can be the **product of human action but not of human design**. Constructivist rationalism treats society as an engineering problem for one mind; ecological rationality treats rules/prices as discovery procedures (Hayek; Vernon Smith). Scientism = pretending complex phenomena have physics-style constants ("Pretence of Knowledge").

**Worked micro-pattern — "Could Gosplan / a supercomputer / AI allocate capital?"**
A1–A4: without private bids for *this* machine vs *that* acre vs *those* hours, there is no money price that already compresses others' knowledge. A5: even if you copy last period's prices, you have not solved *change*. Label as Austrian calculation argument, not a computer-science proof.

**Worked micro-pattern — "The capital market sets the cost of capital, so just nationalize it and keep the prices."**
LO 1: the capital market is where intertemporal prices (including interest) **form**. Frozen prices are not a market. That is the bridge into Checklist B.

### Checklist B — Interest as time preference (Units 3–4; LOs 2–3)

**Question this checklist answers:** what *is* the interest rate, and when is a lower rate a real signal?

ECON103 introduced TP as "prefer sooner to later." Here TP **determines originary interest** and, via saving, the sustainable length of production.

| # | Test | What to write |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | **Originary interest** | Discount of future goods vs present goods; a **category of action**, not a central-bank instrument. Always positive (finite life, uncertainty). |
| B2 | **Not naïve productivity** | "Capital is productive" does not by itself explain a *premium on present goods*. More later still has to beat goods now (Böhm-Bawerk vs productivity theories; Mises's originary interest). |
| B3 | **Gross market rate mix** | Originary interest ± entrepreneurial P&L ± price premium (expected purchasing-power change) ± risk. **Profit ≠ interest.** |
| B4 | **Low TP → prosperity** | More saving → longer/roundabout structure → more capital per head → cheaper consumer goods / higher real wages (LO 2). High TP eats the seed corn. |
| B5 | **Genuine vs fake rate drop** | Rate falls because people **saved** (TP down) → longer projects *are* completable. Rate falls because **fiduciary credit** expanded → false signal. That is LO 3 feeding LO 4. |

**Worked micro-pattern — "Why not set r = 0 to spur investment?"**
B1+B5: r ≈ 0 would mean people do not prefer present to future — false. Forcing it does not create the withheld consumption that longer projects need. Hand the mechanics to Checklist C–D.

**Worked micro-pattern — "Interest is exploitation / a monopoly of banks."**
B1–B3: originary interest would exist in a world of only exchanging persons with different time ranks. Bank monopoly can *distort* the observed rate (Checklist C); it does not *originate* the phenomenon.

Böhm-Bawerk's three grounds vs Mises's pure-TP originary interest: if asked, **surface the split**; default teaching in this course is Misesian originary interest with Böhm-Bawerk on roundabout production.

### Checklist C — Money vs credit (fiduciary media) (Unit 5; LOs 4, 6)

**Question this checklist answers:** did anyone actually **save**, or was a claim issued that circulates as money?

ECON103 money = most marketable commodity (Menger). Here: **Mises typology** and why credit expansion is not capital.

| # | Test | Fail / tell |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Is it **money in the narrower sense** or a **money substitute** (a claim that circulates as money)? | Calling every deposit "money" hides the reserve relation |
| C2 | If a substitute: is it **fully backed** (warehouse / 100% receipt) or **fiduciary media** (issued beyond money holdings)? | Fiduciary issue **increases** the medium of exchange |
| C3 | **Commodity credit** (someone forgoes a present good) vs **circulation credit** (no one forgoes one)? | Circulation credit is **not** a substitute for capital (LO 4) |
| C4 | **Maturity match / golden rule?** Solvency vs liquidity? | Borrowing short to lend long is a different risk than being broke |
| C5 | Long-run production path: **saving** vs **fiduciary issue** (LO 6) | Saving lengthens a completable structure; fiduciary issue **simulates** that lengthening |

**Key points (public Unit 5 fragment; confirm LMS wording later):**
- Fiduciary media = money substitutes not backed by money holdings.
- Lending them increases the medium of exchange **without** anyone forgoing a present good.
- Money is essentially a **medium of exchange**; other functions are derivative.

**Live split:** would fiduciary media exist on a fully free banking market? Rothbard/Mises-skeptical pedagogy (this course's lean) vs free-banking "it is disciplined by redemption." **Label both.** Do not declare the school unanimous.

**Bitcoin here is typology, not a trade:** base-layer BTC has no issuer of unbacked claims on BTC. That is a C2 contrast, not "you should buy." Wallets → ECON135/PRDV151.

**Worked micro-pattern — "Banks lend deposits; that's how we get investment."**
C3: if the deposit is a fully backed substitute, the depositor *waited* — commodity credit. If the bank issues a new substitute against thin reserves, **no one waited** — circulation credit. Only the first is capital transfer.

### Checklist D — ABCT cycle (Units 6–7; LO 5)

**Question this checklist answers:** why a **cluster** of errors, heavier in higher-order / longer projects, after cheap credit — and why the bust is the correction?

Run the chain. If a step is missing, you do not yet have *this* theory's cycle (you may have a real shock, a war, a harvest failure — different story).

| # | Step | Distortion if skipped |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | **Circulation credit** expands (Checklist C) | Ordinary saving-led growth is *not* a boom-bust |
| D2 | Market rate **<** TP-consistent / originary rate | If the rate fell because TP actually fell, projects are sustainable (Garrison "sustainable growth") |
| D3 | Entrepreneurs start **longer / more higher-order** projects as if saving had increased | "More investment" looks like prosperity |
| D4 | Consumption was **not** deferred; real resources cannot finish both | This is **malinvestment**, not "overinvestment in general" |
| D5 | Revelation: input prices, rate catch-up, or inflation premium | Cluster of errors, worse in capital-goods stages |
| D6 | **Bust = recalculation.** Liquidation, shorter structure, losses | Treating the bust as the disease (Keynesian demand gap) **blocks** the repair |
| D7 | "Fix" by more credit? | Repeats D1–D5; extreme = **crack-up boom** (flight from money, calculation dies) |

**Inflation vs credit expansion:** CPI can lag or decouple. ABCT's driver is the **interest-structure** lie, not "prices went up therefore cycle." Entrepreneur money-incomes in the boom are not proof that expansion "helps business" — they are nominally swollen bids for the same scarce factors.

**Garrison (U7):** sustainable = TP down, both future investment *and* later consumption rise along the PPF; unsustainable = credit expansion pushes investment **against** consumption.

**Worked micro-pattern — "Stimulus in a recession restores the missing demand."**
D6: the missing thing is **completable** roundabout projects consistent with TP, not aggregate spending. Re-inflating the rate signal prevents the capital structure from shortening. Rothbard's policy implication in this tradition is laissez-faire during the bust — **label as the school's implication**, not a treasury brief.

**Worked micro-pattern — "ABCT failed because 2008 was housing, not 'capital goods.'"**
Higher-order / longer-duration / rate-sensitive sectors (housing, CRE, long projects) **are** the capital-goods pattern in modern form. Do not require 1930s machine-tools photographs. Still: illustration, not a new regression.

## 2. Satellites (not extra skills)

Do not spin these into their own YAML. If the user's question *is* one of these, run a short pass after naming the school, and point back to A–D when capital/interest/credit are involved.

### S1 — Spontaneous order (Unit 2)
Already inside A5. Use when the user asks how money, law, or traffic patterns exist without a designer. Contrast constructivist "if it's orderly, someone ordered it."

### S2 — Security and defense as economic goods (Unit 8; LO 7)
Hobbesian monopoly is an assertion, not a deduction from scarcity. Positive case: insurance, arbitration, guarding, historical privateering. Externalities-as-justification is the ECON101 public-goods rival — **say so**. Hoppe/Rothbard overlay is political; keep Wertfreiheit vs anarcho-capitalist program separate. No recruitment.

### S3 — Intellectual property (Unit 9; LO 8)
Property allocates **scarce rivalrous** things. Patterns/ideas are non-rival. Patent/copyright as state privilege over others' use of their own scarce means. **Kinsella** is this course's pole (CC0 essay). **Rothbard** historically allowed a copyright-like title and rejected patents — **surface the split**. Utilitarian "IP → innovation" is the claim this unit scrutinizes (Boldrin-Levine), not a settled IO result.

### S4 — Bitcoin vs Austrian method (Unit 10)
- As **money theory**: Mengerian candidate; no base-layer fiduciary issuer (Checklist C). Regression-theorem debate is live.
- As **method foil**: PlanB stock-to-flow is a numerical scarcity→price model. The syllabus asks whether that **challenges praxeology** (no constants, no physics of price). Encode the clash. Do **not** emit a price target or a buy. Wallets / energy-network anthropology → ECON135. Layered history → ECON120. Mining/scaling → ECON105.

## 4. School-contrast cheat sheet (use when the user mixes courses)

| Topic | ECON102-ish default | ECON104 Austrian |
|---|---|---|
| Interest | Policy rate / loanable-funds r* | Originary interest = TP |
| Money stock | M1/M2, multiplier | Money vs fiduciary substitutes |

| Cycle | Demand shock, stickiness, animal spirits | False rate → malinvestment → bust as repair |
| Calculation | Planner can mimic with shadows / compute | No private factor prices → no calculation |
| Knowledge | Data set for the modeler | Never given to one mind (Hayek) |

| IP | Incentive property | Monopoly over non-scarce patterns (school claim) |
