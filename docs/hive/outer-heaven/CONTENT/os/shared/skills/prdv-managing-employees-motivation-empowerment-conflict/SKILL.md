# Managing employees: motivation, empowerment, conflict (clustered)

**Use when:** a prompt needs **managing employees** — a manager motivating people toward corporate goals; giving freedom to act on knowledge and skills; recognizing and resolving workplace conflict that comes from mixed personalities and styles — in the Saylor PRDV225 **Managing Employees** school (8 h). **Never send.**

**Do not use when:** reconstructing exams; **PRDV221** why-managers-exist; **PRDV224** leadership/teams (team conflict / stages); **PRDV226** HR *function*; **PRDV230** workplace conflict *policy*; **PRDV231** ethical leadership; inventing Maslow or other theory names; inventing conflict-model names; treating the reused late-1800s **description** as this course’s job; promising ACE; **sending**.

## 1. Procedure — six checks

Run in order. Stop if Check A says another school or send.

### Check A — Identity overlay (always)

| Prompt smells like | Hand off |
|---|---|
| "Why do managers exist" | **PRDV221** |
| "Lead this *team* / stages / team conflict" | **PRDV224** |
| "HR hiring / department policy" | **PRDV226** |
| "Write a conflict-resolution *policy*" | **PRDV230** |
| "Ethical leadership" | **PRDV231** |
| "Motivate, empower, handle employee conflict" | **this skill** |
| "Send this" | **HITL — refuse send** |

**Primary goal:** a line manager who motivates, empowers, and resolves conflict without turning it into a document or a send. Named failure mode: dumping theory names, writing a policy, or sending.

### Check B — Factors that motivate (when "why won't they work" is in play)

CLO 1. Unit 1.

**List the factors that motivate people.** Live intro: motivate employees to do their best while meeting corporate goals; when motivated they seek ways to improve production and maximize performance.

Shape: factors as a job. Do not invent a named-theory roster. If they ask "which theory," go to Check C without naming one guest never showed.

### Check C — Theories overlay (when "which motivation theory" is in play)

Unit 2 title only: **Theories of Motivation in Management.** Guest did not name any theory.

Shape: admit theories exist as a unit. **Do not name Maslow, Herzberg, or any other model** unless a later logged-in harvest (after BUS502) actually shows them. Unnamed overlay, same rule as Tuckman on 213/224.

### Check D — Empowerment (when "just let them do it" / "I have to approve everything" is in play)

CLO 2. Unit 3.

**Explain the value of empowering employees.** Live intro: by giving employees the freedom to act on their own knowledge and skills, you encourage them to be more productive by fully utilizing their skill sets and, in the process, grow as professionals.

Shape: empowerment = freedom to act on knowledge and skills, tied to production and professional growth. Not an HR empowerment *program*. Not 226.

### Check E — Define conflict (when "is this even conflict" is in play)

CLO 3 overlay. Unit 4.

**Analyze methods of recognizing and managing conflict** starts with knowing it is conflict. Live: every work environment mixes personalities and professional styles; conflicts are sure to arise.

Shape: conflict is expected from style mix, not a failure of hiring. If they want a *policy document*, hand 230. If it is *team* conflict / stages, hand 224.

### Check F — Recognize and manage (when "this fight is happening" is in play)

CLO 3. Unit 5.

Live: effective managers know how to address a conflict when it arises and how to frequently work in concert with others to ensure a speedy resolution.

Shape: recognize, address, often with others, speedy. Not a send. Not a policy. Hand 212 if it is a dyad *comms* problem, 213 if small-group talk, 230 if they want a written workplace policy.

## 2. Worked patterns (shape only)
