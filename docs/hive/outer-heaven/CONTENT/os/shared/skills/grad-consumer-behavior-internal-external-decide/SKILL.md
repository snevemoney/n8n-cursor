# Graduate consumer behavior: internal vs external, then decide (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks how a consumer (individual, group, or organization) **chooses, purchases, or discards** products, services, experiences, or ideas — which internal factor (learning, perception, emotion, personality, attitude), which external factor (groups, class, ethnicity, age, culture, religion), which decision-process or situational force, what happens after the purchase, how the internet expands data access, or how emotional branding should sit in a message — in the Saylor BUS630 **graduate** consumer-behavior school.

**Do not use when:** the job is reconstructing or taking a Saylor (or any) exam, dumping Solomon/Kotler item banks, running **BUS203** undergraduate principles of marketing (mix / STP / IMC; CB as a *chapter*), **BUS602** graduate marketing *management* (MBA602 / MMKT601 / MMGT602 / MEI602), **BUS631** brand management as a *course*, **BUS632** digital marketing / advertising as a *course*, **BUS633** sales as a *course*, **BUS634** marketing research as a *course*, **BUS615** international marketing as a *course*, **PSYCH101** intro psych, treating the free 70% certificate as **MMKT602** 80% proctored exams, promising ACE/transfer credit, inventing **MBA630 / MMGT630 / MEI630 / MMKT630**, or inventing a live learning-theory / four-buying-types stack.

This is **one** skill. It is not "Unit 1 skill + Unit 2 skill…". BUS630's ten live units are one argument: **a campaign resonates only when the marketer asks the right questions about this consumer — internal vs external, through the decision including discard and post-purchase, with internet data access and emotional branding as live overlays.**

## 1. Procedure — answer a graduate consumer-behavior question in six checks

Run in order. Skip a check only if the user already constrained the frame.

### Check A — Identity, overlay (always)

**The course question:** is the user asking for an **intro principles / mix / STP** answer, a **marketing-management** decision, a **brand-architecture** answer, a **digital-marketing course**, a **sales process**, a **research-methods** study, an **international-entry** plan, an **intro-psych** mechanism, or a **consumer-behavior overlay** that has to explain why *this* audience chooses, purchases, or discards so the campaign resonates?

Live test:

| Prompt smells like | Hand off |
|---|---|
| "What are the 4Ps / STP / IMC as intro" | **BUS203** |
| "Run the marketing function / MBA marketing management" | **BUS602** / **MMKT601** / **MBA602** |
| "Brand architecture / equity / portfolio as a course" | **BUS631** |
| "Paid media / SEO / ad ops as a course" | **BUS632** |
| "Sales funnel / quota / selling process as a course" | **BUS633** |
| "Survey design / research methods / capstone" | **BUS634** |
| "Country entry / global mix" | **BUS615** |
| "Intro psych: cognition, social, personality as a *course*" | **PSYCH101** |
| "Why this consumer chose / discarded; internal vs external; post-purchase; emotional branding as a *message*" | **this skill** |

**Primary goal:** a campaign that **resonates** because the marketer asked the **right questions about specific consumers**. That is not "pass Solomon," not "ship a brand bible" (**BUS631**), and not "define the mix" as intro (**BUS203**).

**Named live failure mode:** treating CB as the **immediate experience of the buyer at the register** and skipping discard, society, post-purchase, and the external seats.

**Named live definition** (encode this; do not silently shrink it): individuals, **groups, or organizations**; **choose, purchase, and discard**; products, services, **experiences, or ideas**; effects on consumers **and society**.

**Level check:** if the prompt is actually undergrad homework in BUS203's principles units, **hand off BUS203** and recap only. If it is PSYCH101 homework, **hand off PSYCH101**. If it is MMKT602's proctored 80% exam, **refuse exam reconstruction**; this skill is the *content* overlay, not the degree exam. If they name **MBA630 / MMGT630 / MEI630 / MMKT630**, say those codes were **not found** — twin is **MMKT602 only**.

### Check B — Internal influences: learning, perception, emotion, personality, attitudes (when "why they feel / learn / like this" is in play)

CLO 2. Units 2–4. CLO 8 overlays on Unit 4.

Internal factors the live CLO names: **perception, emotion, personality, and attitudes**. Live unit titles add **learning** (Unit 2) as a first-class seat the intro books fold into "psychological factors."

Name:

1. **Which internal seat** is actually doing the work (learning vs perception vs emotion vs personality vs attitude).
2. **What the marketer can influence** (especially attitudes / Unit 4) vs what they can only *ask about* (personality).
3. **Emotional branding** only as a **message** job (CLO 8) — not brand architecture (**BUS631**).

Tanner overlay (summarize): psychological factors = motivation, perception, learning, attitude; personal = personality, self-concept, gender, age, life stage, lifestyle. Age also has Unit 8 — here it is "who this consumer is internally."

Do **not** invent classical vs operant vs observational, or a named attitude model (Fishbein, ELM), as live Saylor doctrine until login. **Logged-in unit tree is a remaining gap.**

**Hand off PSYCH101** if they want internals as intro psych. **Hand off BUS631** if they want brand as a course. Stay here for "this audience's internal engine, this attitude the message can move."

### Check C — External influences: groups, class, ethnicity, age, culture, religion (when "who else / which class / which culture" is in play)

CLO 3. Units 7–9.

External factors the live CLO names: **social class, cultural values, religion, and ethnicity**. Live unit titles add **groups** (Unit 7) and **age** (Unit 8) as first-class seats. Religion's live unit seat (8 vs 9) is a login gap — encode the *job* either way.

Encode:

- Whose **presence** changes choose/purchase/discard (groups / family / opinion leaders — Tanner overlay; confirm live names).
- **Class, ethnicity, age** as one unit (Unit 8).
- **Culture** as its own unit (Unit 9) — **consumer culture**, not country-entry (**BUS615**).

Do **not** invent a Solomon tribe / subculture taxonomy as live CLO language. Do **not** import OpenStax/Lumen **B2B** as a BUS630 unit (live units do not isolate it).

**Hand off BUS615** for international marketing. **Hand off BUS203** for intro reference-group-as-a-bullet. Stay here for "this external seat, this CLO 3 job."

### Check D — Decision process + situational influences (when "how they decided / store / time / mood / involvement" is in play)

CLO 4. Units 5–6.

Evaluate the **consumer decision-making process** and **situational influences** on it. Live intro already put **choose, purchase, and discard** in the definition.

Tanner overlay (summarize, do not dump): six-stage (need recognition → search → evaluation → choice/purchase → postpurchase → disposal); situational (store / time / reason / mood); low- vs high-involvement. OpenStax overlay: black box; four buying types — **do not freeze** those four types as live Saylor doctrine until login.

Unit 6 is **consumer analysis informing marketing strategy** — CB in, strategy out. **Not** BUS634 research methods. **Not** BUS602 marketing management. **Not** BUS633 the selling process.

**Hand off BUS633** for sales-as-a-course. **Hand off BUS634** for research-as-a-course. Stay here for "this process, this situation, this discard."

### Check E — Post-purchase → marketing strategy (when "after they bought / returned / reviewed / discarded" is in play)

CLO 5 and CLO 6. Units 5 and 10.

Keep **both** live CLO lines (near-duplicates in the Oct 2025 syllabus). Processes **after** a purchase affect **marketing strategy**. Disposal / discard is in the live intro.

Encode: what happens after is *part of CB*, not a support ticket. Strategy that ignores post-purchase fails CLO 5/6.

**Hand off BUS634** if they want measurement/research methods. **Hand off BUS602** if they want marketing management as a course. Stay here for "post-purchase as a CB input to strategy."

### Check F — Internet data access + emotional branding as message (when "online data / social influence / emotional brand message" is in play)

CLO 7 and CLO 8. Overlay across Units 1, 4, 6, 10.

**Internet (CLO 7).** Explain how the internet is **expanding consumer data access** and **influencing consumer behavior**. Encode "new inputs into CB analysis / new influence channel," **not** BUS632 digital marketing as a course (paid media, ad ops, SEO as a *course*).

**Emotional branding (CLO 8).** Analyze the role emotional branding plays in **designing marketing messages**. Encode the *message*, **not** BUS631 brand management as a course (architecture, equity, portfolio).

**Hand off BUS632** for digital-as-a-course. **Hand off BUS631** for brand-as-a-course. Stay here for "internet as a CB data/influence overlay; emotion as a message overlay."
