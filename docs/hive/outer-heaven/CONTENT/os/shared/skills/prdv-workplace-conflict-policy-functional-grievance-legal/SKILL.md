# Workplace conflict policy: functional, grievance, legal (clustered)

**Use when:** a prompt needs **create a workplace conflict resolution policy** — promote functional conflict, discourage dysfunctional conflict, name resolution methods, grievance, legal ramifications, inclusive policy — in the Saylor PRDV230 **Create a Workplace Conflict Resolution Policy** school (13 h). **Never send.** Not operator HR. Building mode: never pitch.

**Do not use when:** reconstructing exams; **PRDV213** small-group *talk*; **PRDV224** team-conflict / stages; **PRDV225** line-manager motivate/empower/handle a fight *without* a policy; **PRDV212** dyad comms; **PRDV226** HR as course; **PRDV107** crisis comms; **BUS641** strategic negotiations; inventing Thomas-Kilmann or statute names; writing and **sending** operator policy; promising ACE.

This is **one** skill. Four live units, five CLOs. Loop: **functional vs dysfunctional then styles then resolution steps then grievance then legal.** Do not mint four unit-skills. Method names that **are** live: negotiation, mediation, arbitration, litigation, informal remedies. Style names and statute names are **not** live.

## 1. Procedure — six checks

Run in order. Stop if Check A says another school or send.

### Check A — Identity overlay (always)

| Prompt smells like | Hand off |

**Primary goal:** literacy on a thoughtful, inclusive conflict-resolution policy. Named failure mode: sending it, inventing statutes, or treating 225 as this course.

### Check B — Functional vs dysfunctional (when "all conflict is bad" is in play)

CLO 1. Unit 1.

**Describe the role of functional and dysfunctional conflict in an organization's success.** Live: functional → creative thinking, problem-solving, innovation. Dysfunctional → misunderstanding, clashing personalities, negative stereotypes, bias.

Shape: keep functional, discourage dysfunctional. 224’s “productive professional conflict” is overlay — **this** is the policy school.

### Check C — Styles of responding (when "how do people fight" is in play)

CLO 2.

**Identify different styles of responding to conflict.** Guest named **no** styles.

Shape: styles exist as a job. **Do not invent** competing/collaborating/avoiding or Thomas-Kilmann. Unnamed overlay.

### Check D — Steps and methods (when "how do we resolve it" is in play)

CLO 3. Units 2 and 4 overlay.

**Outline steps for effective conflict resolution.** Live methods: negotiation, mediation, arbitration, litigation, plus informal remedies. Those five method words **are live**. Step *lists* are not.

Shape: name the live methods. Do not invent a numbered model. **Refuse send** of the procedure email.

### Check E — Formal grievance policies (when "what does the document do" is in play)

CLO 4. Unit 4.

**Explain the role of formal grievance policies.** Live: elements needed to create a thoughtful, inclusive conflict resolution policy.

Shape: explain the *role*. Literacy for a document. **Do not** draft-and-send operator policy. HITL if they want a real file.

### Check F — Legal ramifications (when "can we get sued" is in play)

CLO 5. Unit 3.

**Discuss legal ramifications for workplace conflict and/or harassment.** Live intro: avoid negative ramifications from hostile work environments, discrimination, and harassment. Guest named **no** statutes.

Shape: ramifications as a job. **Do not invent** Title VII, ADA, or case names. Not legal advice. Not a send.
