# Financial-accounting cycle and statements (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks what financial accounting is, how to record a transaction under GAAP, how the accounting equation or debits/credits work, how to run the accounting cycle (journal → ledger → trial balance → adjust → close), how to build the four statements, how to measure inventory / receivables / PPE / bonds / equity, or how to read ratios and internal controls from books you can construct.

**Do not use when:** the job is reconstructing or taking a Saylor (or any) exam, dumping OpenStax/Wiley/Horngren, running **BUS101** survey-only "what is an income statement," **BUS105** managerial/CVP/budgets, **BUS202** TVM/capital-budgeting/securities, **BUS210** audience/channel/tone, ECON120/135 money-and-banking, or tax-prep / audit-engagement procedures.

This is **one** skill. It is not "Unit 1 skill + Unit 2 skill…". BUS103's ten units are one argument: **financial accounting turns transaction data into the four statements, under GAAP, by running the accounting cycle** — then you apply account-class measurement rules and read ratios / controls so stakeholders can decide.

## 0. Hygiene

- Label **school**. This is the **standard undergraduate introduction to financial accounting** (Saylor BUS103; US GAAP; OpenStax *Principles of Accounting, Volume 1*, Franklin / Graybeal / Cooper, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). Shared with almost every undergrad financial-accounting survey. It is not the only way to keep books (cash-basis bookkeeping, IFRS-first, critical accounting). ## 1. Procedure — answer a financial-accounting question in five checks

Run in order. Skip a check only if the user already constrained the frame.

### Check A — Identity, equation, GAAP (always)

Accounting is the **language**. **Data** (raw events: someone puts in $30,000 cash) is not **information** (the journal, ledger, and statements that let a lender see cash and equity). Financial accounting compiles data into a standard format so managers, investors, lenders, stakeholders, and regulators can decide.

**Financial ≠ managerial.** Financial = external users, historical, GAAP, four statements. Managerial = internal, forward-looking, no GAAP constraint. If the prompt is cost behavior, CVP, job-order, budgets, or variances, **stop and hand off BUS105**.

**Users want different sentences from the same event.** An owner asks "did we earn?" A lender asks "can they pay?" A regulator asks "did they follow the rules?"

**Accounting equation — every event must leave this true:**

**Assets = Liabilities + Equity**

Equity splits by form (sole prop: capital − draws; corp: common stock + APIC + retained earnings). Revenues increase equity; expenses and dividends/draws decrease it. Analyze with the **expanded** equation *before* you touch a journal. If you cannot name the accounts and the +/−, you are not ready to debit/credit.

**GAAP vocabulary this school expects** (US GAAP / FASB; IFRS named as the other rule-set, not as a second course):

| Kind | Named items | Failure if ignored |
|---|---|---|
| Assumptions | Economic entity; monetary unit; time period; going concern | Mixing personal and firm cash; reporting in "favors" |
| Principles | Historical cost; revenue recognition (earned); expense recognition / matching; full disclosure | Booking market dreams as assets; cash-only timing |
| Named bias | Conservatism (do not overstate assets/income) | Cookie-jar reserves are *not* this; that is earnings management |

**Four statements and how they hook (build, do not only name):**

| Statement | Time | Question | Feeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Income statement** | Period | Did we earn? | Net income → equity statement |
| **Statement of stockholders' (or owner's) equity** | Period | What happened to residual interest? | Ending equity → balance sheet |
| **Balance sheet** | Snapshot | What do we own and owe *right now*? | Cash ending balance must match SCF |
| **Statement of cash flows** | Period | Where did cash come from / go? | Explains the cash line the BS just showed |

BUS101 Check D *reads* IS + BS. Stay here if you must **construct** or **close into** them.

### Check B — Record the event (journal → ledger → unadjusted TB)

If the prompt is a transaction, run this before writing numbers.

1. **Source document.** Invoice, receipt, note, deposit slip. No document, no entry (or you are guessing).
2. **Classify.** Asset, liability, equity, revenue, expense. Current vs long-term if a BS is in play.
3. **Debit / credit (normal balance = the increase side).**
   - Debit increases: assets, expenses, dividends/draws.
   - Credit increases: liabilities, equity (stock/capital), revenue.
   - Debits = credits on *every* entry. Unbalanced is not a style.
4. **Journalize** (book of original entry: date, accounts, amounts, brief explanation).
5. **Post** to the ledger / T-accounts.
6. **Unadjusted trial balance.** Totals equal. Equal totals do **not** prove you used the right accounts — only that you didn't break arithmetic.

Typical first-month patterns (enough to journal, not a problem-set dump): owner invests cash; buy equipment for cash or on note; pay rent; provide service for cash or on account; collect AR; pay AP; buy supplies.

If the books are already posted and the question is "what's still wrong with this month," skip to Check C.

### Check C — Adjust, statement, close (finish the cycle)

Accrual is this course's basis. **Revenue when earned; expense when incurred.** Cash timing lives on the SCF (Check D).

**Adjusting families — name which, and never touch cash:**

| Family | What happened | Skeleton |
|---|---|---|
| Accrued revenue | Earned, not yet billed/collected | Dr AR (or accrued rec.) / Cr revenue |
| Accrued expense | Incurred, not yet paid | Dr expense / Cr payable |
| Deferred revenue | Cash already in; now earned | Dr unearned revenue / Cr revenue |
| Deferred expense | Cash already out; now used | Dr expense / Cr prepaid (or supplies) |
| Depreciation | Long-lived asset's cost allocated | Dr dep. expense / Cr accum. dep. |

Unadjusted TB → adjustments → **adjusted TB** → statements. Skipping the adjust is how used-up rent stays an asset.

**Then build, in this order:** IS → statement of equity (RE) → BS. SCF is Check D / Unit 10; you can draft it once the accrual books exist.

**Close temporary accounts** so the next period starts at zero:

- Revenues → Income Summary (or RE)
- Expenses → Income Summary
- Income Summary → RE / capital (net income)
- Dividends / draws → RE / capital

**Post-closing TB** = permanent accounts only; still balances.

**Full cycle (encode as one list, CLO 4):** identify → journalize → post → unadjusted TB → adjust → adjusted TB → statements → close → post-closing TB. Worksheets organize; they do not replace the cycle. Reversing entries are optional, not required by the Saylor unit list.

### Check D — Account-class measurement (when the prompt names a line)

CLO 3 is not "assets are good." It is **the specific rule for this class**.

**Inventory (Unit 6).** Merchandiser: sales − COGS = gross profit. Perpetual vs periodic is *when* COGS is computed. Cost-flow assumption (FIFO / LIFO / weighted average) is **not** physical flow. Rising prices: FIFO → higher EI, lower COGS, higher income; LIFO the reverse. US GAAP still allows LIFO; IFRS does not — name it. EI error this year reverses next year. Freight: FOB shipping point vs destination decides who owns goods in transit.

**Receivables (Unit 7).** Earn, then collect. Allowance method (aging or % of sales) is GAAP when uncollectibles are material; direct write-off is not. Allowance is a contra-asset; report **NRV**. Notes receivable add formality and interest. Changing the allowance to smooth income is a control/fraud problem (Check E), not a forecast.

**Payables / current liabilities (Unit 7).** AP, short-term notes, unearned revenue (customer deposits), current portion of LT debt, contingents (probable+estimable → record; reasonably possible → disclose). A promise to pay is a liability even if no invoice has arrived (accrual).

**PPE and other long-lived (Unit 8).** Capitalize costs to get ready for use; expense ordinary repairs. Depreciation **allocates cost**; it is not mark-to-market. Straight-line / units-of-production / declining-balance. Book value = cost − accum. dep. Disposal: remove cost and accum. dep.; cash vs book value → gain/loss. Intangibles: finite-life amortized; goodwill not amortized at intro level. Natural resources: depletion.

**Long-term debt and equity (Unit 9).** Two ways to fund: debt (obligation, keep control) vs equity (dilute). Bonds: par / premium / discount; interest expense vs cash coupon. Equity: issue at par vs APIC; treasury stock contra-equity; cash / stock dividends; splits change count and par, not total equity. RE = profits kept, not cash. EPS is a *read*, not a CFA worksheet. More on pricing, TVM, and markets → **BUS202**. "Should I incorporate?" → **BUS101**.

If the class is unnamed, default to Check B/C (plain service-firm cycle) and say which class rule you did **not** apply.

### Check E — Cash-flow translation, ratios, controls (always when deciding)

**Statement of cash flows.** Accrual income is not cash. Classify every cash (and, for indirect operating, every noncash accrual) as:

- **Operating** — customers, suppliers; under US GAAP, interest and tax paid sit here
- **Investing** — PPE, investments, loans *made*
- **Financing** — debt principal, stock issued/repurchased, dividends

Indirect method is the intro default: net income → add noncash expenses → strip gains/losses on investing → working-capital changes. Direct method exists; do not require it unless asked. Ending cash on the SCF **must** match the BS cash line.

**Ratios (CLO 6) — implications with a baseline.** Compare to last period, a competitor, or the industry. A lone current ratio is a number.

| Family | Asks | Survey examples | Future-performance read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Liquidity | Can we pay soon? | Current; acid-test; working capital | Weak → hiring, inventory, and credit plans are fragile |
| Profitability | Are we earning? | Gross / net margin; ROA; ROE | Falling → mix or cost structure, or a Unit 6 method change |
| Leverage | How much is borrowed? | Debt-to-equity; TIE | High + a rate move = interest just entered the P&L |
| Activity | Are assets working? | Inventory turns; AR days | Slow turns → cash trapped on the SCF |

**Fraud and internal control (CLO 7).** Opportunity + incentive + rationalization (fraud triangle, as taught). Controls exist to make the statements *believable*: segregation of duties, authorizations, source documents, independent checks, bank reconciliation. Management owns the system. SOX is the named US overlay for public-company IC / certification — **course-era**; do not give 2026 legal advice. Allowance games, inventory-count games, and "capitalize the repair" are the intro-level earnings-management list.

## 2. Worked micro-patterns

**"What is financial accounting / why does it matter?"**
Check A in one paragraph. Language of business. Data ≠ information. External users + GAAP + four statements. Hook: the $30,000 contribution is data until the cycle turns it into a BS a lender can read.

**"Journal this."**
Check B. Name accounts, debit/credit, prove the equation. If the date is period-end and cash already moved (or never will this period), you probably need Check C instead of another cash entry.

**"The trial balance balances but the statements look wrong."**
Equality ≠ correctness. Hunt missing adjustments (C), misclassified accounts (D), or a close that never happened. Then rebuild IS → equity → BS.

**"FIFO or LIFO?"**
Check D inventory. Ask whether they need higher income (FIFO in rising prices, US) or lower taxable income (LIFO, US-only). State the EI/COGS tradeoff. Do not pick a method to hide a bad year — that is Check E.

**"Can they pay us / should we lend?"**
Build or read the BS (A/C), then liquidity + leverage + SCF operating cash (E). Accrual profit with negative operating cash is a warning, not a rounding error.

**"How do we account for the truck / the patent / the bond / the new shares?"**
Check D for that class. Capitalize vs expense; depreciate vs amortize vs leave goodwill; bond issue/interest/retire; stock vs APIC vs dividend. Then show the SCF investing or financing section so cash is not confused with income.

**"Is this fraud / do we have controls?"**
Check E. Name the triangle side and the control that would have made the lie harder (segregation, rec, documentation). Do not write an investigation plan or a SOX opinion.
