# Strategic marketing: plan → research → buyer → brand → comms (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks how to turn marketing knowledge into a **strategic marketing plan**, how internal/external environment shapes that plan, who to segment/target/position/**differentiate**, how to commission research that actually answers the planning question, how consumer behavior should change a decision, how to treat brand as equity (not a logo), or how digital communications should execute the plan’s objectives.

**Do not use when:** the job is reconstructing or taking a Saylor (or any) exam, dumping Tanner / OpenStax / Kotler / Aaker / Keller originals, running **BUS203** principles-of-marketing checklists (“what *is* marketing vs advertising”), treating the prompt as **BUS501** firm-level strategy (VRIN, five forces, Porter generics as the *course*), promoting into **BUS602** graduate marketing management, answering **BUS630** CB-lab / **BUS631** brand-architecture / **BUS632** ad-ops / **BUS634** research-methods depth, promising ACE/transfer credit, or writing deceptive-ad / fake-engagement / dark-pattern scripts.

This is **one** skill. It is not "Unit 1 skill + Unit 2 skill…". BUS502's five live units are one argument: **a strategic marketing plan moves the brand from the current situation to a defined future** — diagnose the environment, learn the market and the buyer, make the brand preferred, and say one thing digitally — while staying legal, ethical, and socially responsible.

## 1. Procedure — answer a strategic-marketing question in six checks

Run in order. Skip a check only if the user already constrained the frame.

### Check A — Is this even a marketing *plan*? (always)

**The course question:** is the user asking for a **goal**, a **promo tactic**, a **principles recap**, or a **strategic marketing plan** that moves a named brand from here to a defined future?

Live test: “we need more awareness / we should post more / marketing is the 4Ps” is **not** yet a plan. “In 18 months, brand X is the preferred offering for segment Y because Z, funded by this mix, measured by these KPIs” is a plan — it names **who, why-not-a-substitute, how, and how we will know**.

**Nest the plan (OpenStax/Tanner, named split):**

| Level | Question | Owner | Hand off |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Corporate** | Which businesses / markets? | Top / board | **BUS501** |
| **Business / SBU** | How do we compete *here*? | BU GM | **BUS501** generics if that is the real question |
| **Functional — marketing** | How will we create, communicate, deliver, and exchange value for a named target? | Marketing | **this skill** |

If the prompt is actually “should we acquire / diversify / cost-lead,” **hand off BUS501**. If it is “what *is* marketing vs advertising,” **hand off BUS203**.

**Planning kit this unit names (search-indexed Unit 1):**

1. **Environment — CLO 5.** **PESTEL** = political, economic, social, technological, environmental, legal (*general* environment). **SWOT** = S/W internal, O/T external. SWOT cautions (same as BUS203/BUS501 neighbor): do not list a *move* as an opportunity; do not mix internal and external; SWOT is brainstorming, not a selection algorithm.
2. **STP + D.** Segment → target → position, plus **differentiation** (why this offering is not a substitute). Attractiveness tests still apply (size, growth, crowding, access, resources, mission fit). Mass / differentiated / niche / one-to-one as width choices. Perceptual map on criteria *buyers* use. **USP** as the memorable difference.
3. **4Ps** as the *tactical* mix the plan will authorize (product / price / place / promotion). Map to BUS203’s four activities if the user talks in verbs: creating ↔ product; communicating ↔ promotion; delivering ↔ place; exchanging ↔ price. Do not let the 4Ps eat the plan.
4. **Value proposition** = 30-second specific value for a **named** target vs the alternative. Different targets can get different propositions.
5. **SMART** goals vs objectives. Goals are 3–5 year desired outcomes; objectives are nearer action items. Monitor on a **dashboard** of KPIs (Unit 5 will fill communications metrics; do not wait until the campaign to invent them).
6. **Plan skeleton** (enough to write it): executive summary; mission; SWOT; objectives; segmentation / target / personas; positioning; current situation (market, product, competitors); marketing strategy (product / price / promo / distribution); action programs (who, when, cost); budget; controls. Forecasts are educated guesses; audit = strategy *as implemented*.

**Ethics floor (CLO 7) starts here:** the company’s *actions* communicate. A plan that hits the KPI by lying, hiding fees, or targeting people who cannot reasonably buy is a failed plan in this school.

If asked “what should we do?,” refuse to pick a channel or a price until **target, differentiation, and the goal** are named.

### Check B — What must we still learn? (when research, data, or “we think customers want…” is in play)

CLO 1. Research exists to **improve a decision in the plan**, not to decorate a decision already made.

**Split the information jobs:**

| Job | What it is | Typical use in the plan |
|---|---|---|
| **Market intelligence** | Ongoing scan (prices, buzz, regs) | SWOT O/T; competitor watch |
| **Marketing research** | Project that answers a written objective | “Will segment Y pay for D?” |
| **Market research** (narrow) | Size and trends of a *market* | TAM/SAM as situation, not as strategy |

**Process (encode the steps, not a stats engine):**

1. **Define the problem.** Symptoms ≠ causes. “Sales are down” is a symptom. “Category switchers no longer see D” is a problem the plan can act on. New Coke’s “win the taste test” remains the caution.
2. Write a research **objective** that a manager can use.
3. **Secondary first**, then primary. Exploratory (qual) vs descriptive vs causal.
4. Forms: no leading, double-barreled, or jargon questions.
5. Sample: probability vs convenience; frame ≠ population; sampling error as a named caution.
6. Collect → analyze (validity ≠ reliability) → report (**executive summary** first).

Skip research when cost > benefit, time is gone, or the answer is already obvious.

**Ethics:** legal, identified, public sources. No dumpster / hacking / undisclosed tracking. Privacy is a CLO-7 constraint, not a footnote.

**Hand off BUS634** if they need instrument design, sampling theory, or a methods course. **Hand off BUS206 / BUS250** if the real object is an information system or an analytics pipeline. Stay here for “what would change the plan.”

### Check C — What does the buyer actually do? (when “why they buy,” motivation, or journey is in play)

CLO 2. Apply CB **to a marketing decision**.

**Consumer path (intro depth):** need recognition → search → evaluation → purchase → postpurchase → disposal. Involvement changes search: routine / limited / extended (OpenStax also: complex / dissonance-reducing / habitual / variety-seeking). Overlay situation (store, time, mood, social), culture/class/reference groups/family life cycle, and psychology (motivation, perception, learning, attitude).

**Motivation names (undergrad trio — confirm live resource; do not dump originals):**

| Name | What this school uses it for | Failure if you ignore it |
|---|---|---|
| **Maslow** | People try to satisfy the most pressing need first | Selling esteem to someone still solving safety |
| **Freud** | Unconscious motives; offerings can be symbols | Taking the stated reason as the only reason |
| **Herzberg** | Hygiene vs motivators — no dissatisfiers ≠ a reason to buy | Fixing price/availability and wondering why no one prefers you |

**B2B overlay (name only):** derived / fluctuating / joint demand; buying-center roles; straight / modified / new buy. If the job is a B2B lab, stay at naming depth unless they asked for BUS602.

**Hand off BUS630** for experimental CB / full models. Stay here for “what the plan, the brand, and the message must know about this buyer.”

### Check D — Is the brand doing the work? (when equity, loyalty, reputation, or “refresh the logo” is in play)

CLO 3. Brand is a **competitive asset**, not a color palette.

**Distinctions the indexed Unit 4 intro insists on:**

| Term | Means in this school | Not the same as |
|---|---|---|
| **Recognition** | They can identify it | Preference |
| **Loyalty** | They come back / resist rivals | Recognition |
| **Equity** | Assets (and liabilities) linked to the name that add or subtract value | Either of the above alone |

**Named diagnostics (tools, not monographs):**

- **Aaker** assets: awareness, associations, perceived quality, loyalty, other proprietary assets (marks, channel relationships). Equity can go *down*.
- **Brand Asset Valuator** (as taught): **differentiation, relevance, esteem, knowledge**. A brand can be known but not different (commodity), different but not relevant (curiosity), etc. Use it to *diagnose*; do not treat the four bars as a campaign slogan.
- **Keller CBBE** staircase (OpenStax overlay): identity → meaning → response → resonance. If live resources use Keller, encode the staircase.

**Build:** positioning + differentiation (Check A’s +D) + brand platform (promise, values, voice/personality, positioning statement) + **consistency** across every brand-related activity. A chart that aligns brand / messaging / marketing is the named test: three reasons for consistency beat one clever one-off.

**Manage through the PLC:** intro (make it known), growth (make it chosen vs new rivals), maturity (extend / repair without cheapening), decline (harvest or divest the *brand*, not just the SKU). Social media makes reputation a **real-time** asset/liability — Unit 5 is how you speak; Unit 4 is what you are trying to protect.

**Hand off BUS631** for architecture, portfolio brands, and measurement systems as a *course*. Stay here for “does this plan create preference, and can we keep it.”

### Check E — What should we say, where, digitally? (when campaign, content, email, social, or IMC is in play)

CLO 4. Communications **execute the plan’s objectives**. “Post more” is not a strategy.

**IMC = one consistent message** across the mix, even as media shift. Promo mix as taught: advertising, personal selling, PR, sponsorships/events, sales promotion, direct marketing, online/social. Objectives move **awareness → persuasion → action**. Each brand still needs a **USP**. Budget: prefer **objective-and-task**; percent-of-sales is easy and backwards.

**Digital overlay this unit owns:**

| Lever | Job in this school | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Content marketing | A *system* (keywords, ongoing), not a post | Treating buzz as society |
| Email | **Owned** media — you control list and content | Buying lists; undisclosed spam |
| Social / platforms | Platform-specific behavior; owned vs paid vs earned | A follower-count KPI with no plan objective |
| Metrics | Feed Unit 1’s dashboard | Vanity metrics that cannot falsify the plan |

Search-indexed subsection title: **content marketing and email marketing** as a named pair. Re-fetch platform lists; they go stale.

**Hand off BUS632** for media buying / performance ops. **Hand off COMM411** for PR-as-profession. **Hand off BUS210** for audience/tone as message design. Stay here for “this mix is how the plan talks.”

**Ethics floor (CLO 7) again:** no deceptive claims, fake scarcity, fake reviews, undisclosed sponsorships, or dark patterns. Email/disclosure rules are **named legal constraints** — re-fetch the current statute; do not invent compliance procedures for evasion.

### Check F — Does it survive law, ethics, and CSR? (always before you lock the plan)

CLO 7 is not a last chapter. Run it against A–E:

- Environment scan already includes **legal** (PESTEL-L). If the growth path trips a regulator, it is a dead plan (same cash-and-law instinct as BUS501, different object: the *marketing* move).
- Research: consent, privacy, no espionage.
- CB: do not exploit a vulnerable need as if it were a segment “insight.”
- Brand: reputation you cannot defend in public is not equity.
- Comms: truth, disclosure, no manufactured urgency.

If the only way to hit the KPI is a CLO-7 failure, **change the plan**, do not write a cleverer tactic.

## 2. Worked shape (not an exam item)

Prompt shape: “We want to grow 40% next year. Should we cut price, refresh the logo, or go hard on TikTok?”

Run the checks, short:

- A: 40% is a **goal**. Price / logo / TikTok are candidate **hows**. Name the level: price is a 4P; logo is brand; TikTok is a communications vehicle. Name the **target** and the **+D** before picking a how. SWOT/PESTEL: is the category even growing; is a price war legal/rational.
- B: what evidence do you have that the target *would* buy more at a lower price vs would *prefer* a clearer brand vs is even on that platform? Write a research objective; secondary first.
- C: is this high-involvement (complex) or habitual? A price cut trains a different motive than a brand refresh.
- D: a logo refresh without equity (awareness/associations/quality/loyalty) is theater. A price cut can **un-stick** a differentiator into a commodity (BAV: differentiation collapses; Aaker: perceived quality and loyalty take the hit).
- E: TikTok is a vehicle. If the plan’s objective is awareness in a platform-native segment, it can be *part* of IMC — not a replacement for positioning or for owned email. Objective-and-task budget; one message.
- F: disclosure, targeting, and whether “hard on TikTok” means engagement bait.
