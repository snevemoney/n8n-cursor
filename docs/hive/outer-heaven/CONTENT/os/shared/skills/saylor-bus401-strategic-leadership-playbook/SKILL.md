# Clustered skill: Saylor BUS401 strategic leadership playbook

**School:** Saylor Academy  
**Course:** BUS401 — Strategic Leadership Management (LMS alias: Management Leadership)  
**Type:** one clustered skill (five public units → one operating playbook)  
**Not in this skill:** exam stems, options, keys, "what Saylor will ask," item clones.

Work every prompt through the five gates in order. Stop when the gate answers the job; do not dump the whole theory catalog.

---

## Gate 0 — Frame the situation (30 seconds)

Write four labels before any model:

| Label | Prompt |
|---|---|
| Work type | Complexity (known process, resources, variance) or change (new direction, new coalition)? |
| Power | Legitimate/position, personal only, or both? |
| Collection | Group (shared interest) or team (shared performance goal + mutual accountability)? |
| Time/info | Enough to optimize, or must we satisfice? |

If the lever is force or punishment, do not call it leadership (Saylor PoM: willing adoption of a goal; coercion is dictatorship).

---

## Gate 1 — Management vs leadership vs both

**Management:** allocate resources; plan, budget, organize, staff, monitor, correct deviations (Kotter/Moua: cope with *complexity*).  
**Leadership:** influence people toward a goal; set direction, align, motivate, make meaning (cope with *change*).  
**BUS401 insistence:** the words are confused in practice; organizations need both in tandem. A manager may run data, not people. A leader may have no office.

Checklist:

- [ ] Name the resource problem (management) and the people/meaning problem (leadership) separately.
- [ ] If you only have a control plan, add a vision/alignment move.
- [ ] If you only have a speech, add owners, budgets, and feedback loops.
- [ ] Informal leader? Skip position-power tools; go to Gate 4.
- [ ] Formal manager who must still lead change? Run Gates 2–3, then 5.

Lewin style overlay (Unit 1, not a complete theory): autocratic / democratic / laissez-faire. Default is **match the situation**, not "always democratic." Laissez-faire is usually a deficit (ambiguity, conflict), not a philosophy.

---

## Gate 2 — Pick a theory like a toolbox, not a creed

Unit 2 framing: theories contradict; do not crown one. Filter:

1. **Trait** — IQ, emotional intelligence, extraversion, conscientiousness, openness, integrity help *emergence*. Useless as "born leader" fatalism. BUS401: most leaders are made.
2. **Behavioral** — gap in **task/initiating structure** or **people/consideration**? Blake-Mouton grid: people vs production; 9,9 is an aspiration, not a law.
3. **Follower readiness (Hersey-Blanchard SLT)**

   | Follower | Leader move |
   |---|---|
   | Low competence, high commitment | Direct |
   | Low competence, low commitment | Coach |
   | Moderate–high competence, variable commitment | Support |
   | High competence, high commitment | Delegate |

4. **Path-goal (House)** — repair the broken expectancy, then choose directive / supportive / participative / achievement-oriented.
5. **Fiedler** — if style is sticky, change situational favorableness (leader–member relations, task structure, position power) rather than preaching a new personality.
6. **Vroom-Yetton-Jago** — participation amount from: decision significance, need for commitment, leader expertise, likely acceptance, goal alignment, group expertise, team competence.
7. **Transformational (4 I’s)** — idealized influence/charisma, inspirational motivation, intellectual stimulation, individualized consideration. Use for change and identity.
8. **Transactional** — contingent reward; active management-by-exception. Use for exchange, quality, compliance. Avoid **passive** MBE (wait for failure).
9. **LMX / servant / authentic** (PoM companion) — watch in-group unfairness; serve follower development; stay consistent with values.

Output of this gate: **one primary style + one backup**, with the situational reason. Never "transformational because that is the best theory."

## Gate 3 — Change and decision, in that pairing

Unit 3 treats change and decision as one leadership problem.

### Change (Lewin)

1. List **driving** vs **restraining** forces.
2. **Unfreeze** — why this, why now; make the old pattern unsafe to keep.
3. **Move** — implement; expect supporters *and* protesters; communicate more than you announce.
4. **Refreeze** — write the new way into process, metrics, and culture so it does not snap back.
5. Do not optimize for universal consent.

Communication check: who hears first, what they can still influence, what is already decided, where to send grief.

### Decision

1. Classify the method: autocratic (A), consultative (C), consensus/team.
2. If quality **and** buy-in are high and your information is incomplete → consult or consensus.
3. **Optimize** only with time, information, and cost. Otherwise **satisfice** and record the constraint.
4. Bias sweep before lock-in:
   - **Anchoring** — discard the first number and re-estimate.
   - **Groupthink** — invite dissent, assign a critic/devil’s advocate, bring an outsider, delay consensus.
   - **Selective perception** — steel-man the opposite case; look for disconfirming data.
5. Individual vs team is a design choice: teams add information and process loss. Default is not a meeting.

## Gate 4 — Lead without legitimate power

Unit 4 is the BUS401 differentiator.

1. Inventory bases you **actually have**: expert, referent, reward, information, coalition, moral. Note what you **lack**: legitimate, coercive.
2. Prefer influence over dominance. Dominance without office expires.
3. Move people: **compliance → identification → internalization**. Non-authority work lives at internalization ("this is our idea").
4. Empower: give a goal, a constraint, and decision rights; do not give a task and hover.
5. Emotional intelligence (Mayer-Salovey / Goleman as used in the course): name own emotion, name group emotion, choose behavior. EI is not "be nice."
6. Interpersonal kit: one-message communication, assertiveness without aggression, a conflict *process* (interests, options, criteria) rather than a personality verdict.
7. Minority / cultural difference is a leadership design problem. Moua CQ companion: CQ is strategy **and** tool — pause, question assumptions (time, hierarchy, directness), adapt behavior, keep the balcony view (Heifetz).

Stop-do: fake authority, manipulate, or confuse identification-with-you for shared purpose.

## Gate 5 — Groups, teams, diversity, transition

### Group vs team

- Group: shared interest, optional coordination.
- Team: complementary skills, common purpose, joint performance goal, mutual accountability (Katzenbach/Smith as used in PoM).
- If there is no performance goal, do not run a team-building ritual.

### Design

- [ ] KSAs cover the task (contractor, creator, contributor, completer, critic + social roles + boundary spanning).
- [ ] Size small enough to coordinate (often ≤10; else subteams).
- [ ] Name interdependence: pooled / sequential / reciprocal.
- [ ] Diversity of expertise and perspective is a performance asset **if** inclusion and conflict process exist.
- [ ] Manager-led vs self-managed vs self-directed — match autonomy to skill and task interdependence.

### Develop (Tuckman + punctuated equilibrium)

| Stage | Leader move |
|---|---|
| Forming | Direct; make purpose and membership obvious |
| Storming | Surface conflict; prevent cliques from becoming the process |
| Norming | Facilitate; lock a **team contract** (values, roles, decision rights, communication, poor-performance rule) |
| Performing | Coach / delegate; protect the work system |
| Adjourning / shock | Debrief; or treat a jolt as a punctuated burst, not a failure |

### Health

- Cohesion helps **only if** task commitment is high. High cohesion + low task = social club or groupthink.
- Counter social loafing: identifiable work, fair rewards, smaller pods.
- Virtual: manage deliverables and trust, not line-of-sight. Write more; meet less; meet on purpose.
- Meetings: need, agenda, start/end on time, action owners. If it is an FYI, it is not a meeting.

### Transitional leadership

A team that depends on one hero is not yet a team. Before a leader changes: document decision rights, name an acting owner, keep rituals, separate loyalty to the person from loyalty to the purpose (Kouzes-Posner credibility: do what you said).

## Single-pass operating sequence

When a BUS401 case, workplace prompt, or study question lands, run:

1. Gate 0 labels.
2. Gate 1 — are we managing, leading, or both?
3. Gate 2 — one theory/style + why this situation, plus a backup.
4. If the work is a change or a choice → Gate 3 (Lewin + decision class + bias sweep).
5. If authority is thin → Gate 4 (power inventory, influence path, EI).
6. If more than one person must perform together → Gate 5 (group/team, contract, stage, cohesion, transition).
7. Answer in checklists and named moves. Do not narrate every model you did not use.

## Language to prefer

- "Match style to follower readiness" not "the best style is X."
- "Unfreeze / move / refreeze" not "get buy-in" as a slogan.
- "Satisfice under this constraint" when optimizing is theater.
- "Informal / personal power" not "fake the org chart."
- "Team = mutual performance goal" not "we collaborated."

## Language to avoid

- Exam prediction, "likely multiple-choice," stems, options, keys.
- Copying Time, Mind Tools, Forbes, or YouTube wording from the 2015 reading list.
- Absorbing BUS501 corporate-strategy tools (five forces, generic strategies) into this skill.
