## 1. Procedure — answer a firm-strategy question in six checks

Run in order. Skip a check only if the user already constrained the frame.

### Check A — Is it even a strategy? (always)

**The course question:** is this a **goal**, a **wish**, or a **deliberate, goal-oriented plan of action**?

Live test: “My strategy is to win / grow 50% / be the best” is **not** a strategy. “Apply defensive pressure to force turnovers…” / “lower prices *and* keep profits up by …” / “acquire X because …” is a strategy — it names **how**.

**Mintzberg 5 Ps** (MSM ch.1, named tool; all five, none sufficient):

| P | What it is in this school | Failure |
|---|---|---|
| **Plan** | Intended steps the firm will follow | A slide deck no one acts |
| **Ploy** | A specific maneuver to outwit a rival | A trick with no position behind it |
| **Pattern** | Consistency in *realized* behavior | Intended plan that never shows up in actions |
| **Position** | Place vs competitors / customers | “Unique” with no relative map |
| **Perspective** | How executives see the world | A culture that cannot see the threat |

**Intended vs emergent vs realized:** realized strategy = what was intended *plus* what emerged *minus* what was abandoned. If the user only has a plan, ask what pattern would count as evidence.

**Hierarchy (do not collapse levels):**

| Level | Question | Typical owner |
|---|---|---|
| **Corporate** | In which businesses / markets should we compete? | Top / board |
| **Business-level** | How will we compete *in this* industry? | BU / general manager |
| **Functional / department** | How do we support that how? | Marketing, ops, finance… |

A subunit strategy that fights the corporate position (SNLS BMW: a cost-leadership unit inside a premium-quality firm) is a failure mode, not clever local optimization.

**Market structures (CLO 1):** name the *arena* before the generic. Perfect competition, monopolistic competition, oligopoly, monopoly change whether cost leadership or differentiation can even stick. Identifying the **market** is not the same as identifying the **competitor** (SNLS: Google and Microsoft compete, but not in every market; Apple hosts rivals on its platform). Hand off full microeconomics to **ECON101**.

If asked “what should the firm do?,” refuse to pick a generic until the **goal** and the **level** (corporate vs business) are named.

### Check B — Diagnose internal and external (when “where do we stand,” SWOT, industry, or resources are in play)

CLO 2: apply tools to diagnose internal **and** external factors. Live named pair: **SWOT or PESTLE**. MSM splits the work:

**External — general environment: PESTEL** (political, economic, social, technological, environmental, legal). Use to find *opportunities and threats that would exist even if this firm disappeared*. Antitrust, capital markets, regulation, tech shifts live here.

**External — industry: five forces** (Porter, named tool). Purpose = **profit potential of the industry**, not a score of “how mean rivals are.”

| Force | High when | Strategy implication |
|---|---|---|
| Rivalry | Many similar competitors, slow growth, high exit barriers | Advantage must be sharp; price wars eat everyone |
| New entrants | Low barriers, weak retaliation | Cost leadership’s scale *or* differentiation’s loyalty are the fences |
| Substitutes | Other industries solve the same job | Define the market by the *job*, not the product |
| Supplier power | Concentrated, switching costs, no substitutes | Vertical integration / dual-source become corporate questions (Check D) |
| Buyer power | Concentrated, low switching, backward integrate threat | Differentiation that creates loyalty, or cost that they cannot undercut |

Strategic groups: rivals whose *strategies* are similar, not every firm in the NAICS code.

**Internal — resource-based / VRIN:** a resource can found a **sustained** advantage only if it is valuable, rare, costly to imitate, and nonsubstitutable. Resources = what the firm *has*; capabilities = what it *can do* with what it has. Value chain maps *where* cost or uniqueness is actually produced.

**SWOT cautions (MSM):**

1. Do not list a *move* (“enter Canada”) as an opportunity; the opportunity is the unmet market, the move is a strategy.
2. Do not mix internal (S/W) with external (O/T).
3. SWOT is **brainstorming**, not a selection algorithm. It starts the conversation that Checks C–E finish.

SNLS planning constraint still useful: a growth goal with no capital, or an expansion that trips a regulator, is a dead plan. Cash and law are diagnosis, not afterthoughts.

**Hand off BUS208** if the prompt is “what is the manager's job this Tuesday / POLC.” Stay here for **this firm's position**.

### Check C — Business-level advantage (when “how do we compete here” is in play)

CLO 3 / 5. Two dimensions (Porter via MSM): **source** (cost vs uniqueness) × **scope** (broad vs narrow).

| | Broad scope | Narrow (focus) |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost** | Cost leadership — acceptable quality, low price, volume, efficiency | Focused cost — low price *relative to the niche* (not necessarily industry-lowest) |
| **Uniqueness** | Differentiation — premium for features + communication of those features | Focused differentiation — specialized uniqueness for a defined niche |

**Best-cost** (relatively low price *and* substantial uniqueness) is attractive and **attacked from both sides**. Do not recommend it as the default “we’ll do both.”

**Stuck in the middle:** not cheap enough, not unique enough. Trying to please every segment is the Aesop failure. Strategy is also deciding **what not to do**.

Recipes this school expects (summarize, not dump):

- Cost leadership: economies of scale, squeeze suppliers, minimize R&D/ad *unless* the industry requires them (Walmart advertises; Waffle House barely does). Risk: volume dependence, slow detection of change, “acceptable quality” falling below acceptable.
- Differentiation: R&D + brand so customers **pay a premium and stay**. Risk: uniqueness erodes, knockoffs, “won’t pay extra,” a rival matching the service at a lower price.
- Focus: expertise in the niche; premium or efficient-for-the-niche. Risk: niche ceiling, niche disappears, a narrower specialist or a broad rival with a “good enough” line.

**Sustainable** (SNLS Unit 3 takeaway): as soon as it works, rivals **copy or leapfrog**. Jumping ahead ≠ staying ahead. VRIN (Check B) is the test for *staying*. Acquire-the-innovator (SNLS 21st-century; Google/Microsoft-era counts are course-era) is one corporate *response* to the innovator’s dilemma — name it, do not treat 2009 acquisition tallies as 2026 facts.

If asked “which generic?,” name (1) source, (2) scope, (3) whether VRIN supports *sustaining* it, (4) one rival move that would copy or leapfrog it. Do not stack all four generics as a “portfolio of positioning.”

### Check D — Corporate-level scope (when “which businesses,” M&A, integrate, diversify, or shrink is in play)

Corporate strategy answers **in what industry or industries**. Login gap: live Unit 3 vs Unit 5 vs old SNLS Unit 4. Encode the tools regardless.

**Concentration** (stay in one industry) — Ansoff three:

| Move | Existing product | New product |
|---|---|---|
| Existing market | **Market penetration** (more share, often ads) | **Product development** |
| New market | **Market development** (new channel or geography) | (that cell is diversification — next) |

**Horizontal integration** = acquire/merge a rival. Reasons: scale, brand, share, channel. MSM warning: most deals **destroy** shareholder wealth; culture clash and overpayment are the usual kills. Do not cheer M&A.

**Vertical integration** = new portion of the *same* value chain. **Backward** = enter a supplier’s business (reduce supplier power). **Forward** = enter a buyer’s business (capture margin, control the customer experience). Risk: you are now in a business you do not know; captive suppliers get complacent.

**Diversification** = a **new value chain**. Porter’s three tests (named tool): (1) is the target industry attractive? (2) is the cost of entry recoverable? (3) will the new unit *and* the firm be better off (shared advantage)? **Related** (similarities you can actually exploit — Honda engines) beats **unrelated** (Harley water, Starbucks furniture) in this school. “Synergy” without a named core competency is a slogan.

**Get smaller:** retrenchment (shrink a unit, often layoffs, to survive) vs restructuring (divest / spin-off / liquidate). Diversification discount is why breakups happen.

**BCG portfolio** (stars / cash cows / question marks / dogs): two axes only (share × industry growth). Limits: oversimplifies, demotivates “dog” units, cannot *find* new industries. Use as a **resource-allocation sketch**, not a strategy.

If asked “should we buy / diversify / integrate?,” run the three tests and name the level (Check A). Do not treat every growth wish as a merger.

### Check E — Execute and control (when “we have a strategy, nothing moves” is in play)

CLO 4 / 5. Live Unit 4 title is **Executing Strategy**. Formulation without implementation is theater.

MSM ch.9 skeleton: **structure** (how activities connect) must match the strategy (cost leadership usually wants simple, efficient structure; differentiation usually wants looser, more specialist structure). **Control** = standards, compare, correct — financial and operational. Legal form (sole prop / partnership / corporation) is a structure implication, not a law-school answer (**hand off BUS205**).

Live intro loop: execute → monitor → refine as conditions change. A change in the arena (Check B) that does not change the plan is how you become stuck in the middle.

Leadership / culture / motivation appear here as **constraints on execution**, not as BUS401’s object. People are not identical (SNLS Unit 5 residue). **Hand off BUS401** for theory families, power-without-title, Lewin-as-leadership. **Hand off BUS209** for three-level OB.

If asked “how do we implement?,” name (1) which structure/control loop would make the generic in Check C *true in the P&L*, (2) one metric that would show the pattern (Check A), (3) what you will stop doing.

### Check F — Globalization, ethics, adaptability (when “will this travel,” CSR, or “21st century” is in play)

CLO 6: competitive advantage **in the era of globalization**. Live intro: leadership, ethics, adaptability.

- Even a small firm can outsource across borders; a global view is a **success factor**, not an elective chapter. International strategy types (MSM ch.7) are named tools: whether you replicate, adapt, or mix. Confirm live names on login; do not dump the international-business course (**BUS613**).
- Ethics / CSR / boards (MSM ch.10) are **whether the strategy survives the firm’s values and its governors**, not BUS200’s theory course. **Hand off BUS200**.
- Innovator’s dilemma: winning by innovating, then losing to the next innovator. Responses this school names: keep innovating, or acquire. Neither is free.
- Adaptability is the monitor/refine half of Check E applied to a moving global arena.

If asked “does this work abroad / is this responsible?,” name the arena shift (PESTEL in the *other* country), whether the advantage is still VRIN there, and one ethics/governance constraint. Do not treat “go global” as a strategy.

## 2. Worked shape (not an exam item)

Prompt shape: “We want to grow 50% in three years. Should we cut price or buy a competitor?”

Run the checks, short:

- A: 50% is a **goal**. Price-cut vs acquire are candidate **hows**. Name the level: price-cut is business-level; acquire is corporate (horizontal integration) *if* the target is a rival in this industry.
- B: PESTEL (antitrust on the deal; capital for either path). Five forces (will a price cut start a war you cannot survive?). VRIN (do you have a cost position that survives lower prices? does the target have resources you lack?).
- C: if you are a differentiator, a price cut may **un-stick** you into the middle. If you are already cost leader, a price cut is a weapon only if scale still yields profit (SNLS: “how do you lower prices *and* keep profits up?”).
- D: three tests on the acquisition; most deals fail; culture + overpay.
- E: whichever how you pick, name the control loop and what you stop doing.
- F: if the growth is geographic, this is market development *and* a CLO-6 question, not a domestic price war.

### Check E — Score: measures vs referents, including ethics (when “are we winning / is this sustainable / what number matters” is in play)

CLO 5. A measure without a referent is a glance, not a judgment.

| | Measure (metric) | Referent (benchmark) |
|---|---|---|
| Financial | Profits, margin, ROA, stock price | Own history; industry; strategic group |
| Customer | Attraction, satisfaction, retention | Same, plus the right the customer actually has |
| Internal process | Cycle time, quality, safety | The duty you claimed in Check A |
| Learning and growth | New skills, innovation rate | Can we still improve (MSM scorecard question) |
| Ethics / sustainability | CSP-class / triple-bottom-line indicators | The code, the models, the sustainability goal — not “we donated once” |

MSM teaching example (course-era numbers, keep the *logic*): 20% margin looks fine until last year was 35% and the industry is 40%. Graduate overlay: a “great” quarter that fails deontology/rights/virtue is a referent fail on the ethics dimension, even if NI is up.

### Check F — Hold: code, training, example, close the loop (when “will this last / culture / scandal / can we still claim this strategy” is in play)

Unit 4 + 3d. Implementation is where character shows (ALS: the decisions define credibility).

- **Code of ethics** — expected behavior, specific enough to decide. A poster is not a code.
- **Training** — people can identify and resolve ethical situations the strategy will actually produce.
- **Lead by example** — if leadership’s private strategy contradicts the public one, virtue already failed.
- Boards / governance / SOX-as-named: **course-era** check on reporting and personal responsibility, not 2026 legal advice (**BUS205**). Scandal-class stories (Enron-era) are why this unit exists; **do not dump the film**.
- If the strategy cannot be *claimed* by the people who have to live it, you are back in Check C. A project that never ends has become operations (**BUS300**). A strategy that cannot be said under the three models was never formulated.
