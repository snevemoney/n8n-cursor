# Principles of finance: TVM, value, and capital (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks what finance is (vs accounting), how to maximize shareholder wealth, how to read statements *as inputs*, how to compute PV/FV/annuities, how to price a bond or a dividend-growth stock, how risk and return (β, CAPM) set a required rate, whether to accept a project (NPV/IRR/PI/payback), how debt vs equity changes WACC, or how to run cash / receivables / inventory / short-term credit.

**Do not use when:** the job is reconstructing or taking a Saylor (or any) exam, dumping Boundless or OpenStax, running **BUS103** journals / the accounting cycle / *constructing* the four statements, **BUS105** cost/CVP/make-or-buy, **BUS101** survey-only "what is an income statement," **ECON120/135** what money is, **BUS200** CSR ethics dilemmas, **BUS601** graduate financial management, or CFA item-set coaching.

This is **one** skill. It is not "Unit 1 skill + Unit 2 skill…". BUS202's eight units are one argument: **finance decides what to do with scarce money under uncertainty** — statements become cash-flow forecasts, TVM discounts them, markets price the claims that fund them, capital budgeting spends them, WACC is the hurdle the capital structure implies, and working capital keeps the firm solvent this quarter.

## 1. Procedure — answer a principles-of-finance question in five checks

Run in order. Skip a check only if the user already constrained the frame.

### Check A — Identity, goal, agency (always)

**Finance ≠ accounting.** Accounting organizes the past. Finance allocates scarce money **over time under uncertainty**. The named one-liner: *if I give you money today, how much do I get back later?*

**Primary goal: maximize owner / shareholder wealth** (market value of equity). That is not "maximize this year's net income," not "maximize size," and not "keep every stakeholder equally happy." Liquidity, risk, ethics, and sustainability constrain the path; they are not a second objective that replaces value.

Three decision types this school names:

| Decision | Asks | Later check |
|---|---|---|
| Investment | What real assets / projects do we buy? | E (NPV) |
| Financing | Debt, equity, or internals? | E (WACC / structure) |
| Dividend / payout | Return cash or reinvest? | Named in Unit 1; Boundless ch.15 is **not** a live Saylor unit — don't fake a dividend-policy course |

**Forms** change *financing and agency*, not just the name on the door: sole prop / partnership (unlimited liability, hard to raise), corporation (limited liability, perpetual, agency appears because owners ≠ managers), LLC / co-op as named variants. "Should I incorporate?" as a *survey* pick → **BUS101**. Stay here for "this form just created an agency problem / a public market."

**Agency:** principal (owners) vs agent (managers). Conflict of interest = the agent's paycheck, perks, or horizon ≠ the owners'. Also named: **shareholders vs bondholders** (equity likes upside; debt likes not being looted via dividends or risk-shifting). Governance (board, disclosure, internal control) exists to make the wealth-max goal *believable*. Enron / WorldCom → **SOX 2002** as the named US overlay — **course-era**, not 2026 legal advice. A CSR / stakeholder *ethics* write-up → **BUS200**.

If the prompt is journalize / adjust / close / *construct* an IS+BS, **stop and hand off BUS103**.

### Check B — Statements as inputs (ratios, FCF, pro forma)

You do not build the books here. You **read them to decide**.

**Four statements, finance-side questions:**

| Statement | Time | Finance question |
|---|---|---|
| Income statement | Period | Did operations earn? EBIT is the operating-profit line capital budgeting starts from |
| Balance sheet | Snapshot | What assets are we funding, and with whose money? |
| Statement of cash flows | Period | Where did cash actually move? (O/I/F) |
| Equity statement | Period | Did NI stick as RE or leave as dividends? |

**Free cash flow** (named live) is what valuation and project analysis eat — cash operations could distribute without hurting the asset base. Accrual NI with negative operating cash is a warning, not a rounding error. MVA / EVA are named; intro depth is "value added vs book," not a Stern Stewart implementation.

**Ratio families (always with a baseline — own history, peer, or industry):**

| Family | Asks | Survey examples | Finance use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Liquidity | Can we pay soon? | Current; quick | Working-capital policy (Check E) |
| Asset management | Are assets working? | Inventory turns; DSO; TAT | Cash trapped in the cycle |
| Debt management | How much is borrowed? | D/E; TIE | Capital structure / distress (Check E) |
| Profitability | Are we earning? | Margins; ROA; ROE | DuPont: **ROE = PM × TAT × equity multiplier** |
| Market | What is the claim priced at? | P/E; M/B | Stock as a source of capital (Check D) |

Horizontal = trend. Vertical = common-size. A lone current ratio is a number.

**Pro forma / percentage-of-sales:** project the IS, plug the BS, read **external financing needed**. A cash budget is timing (Unit 8), not a GAAP statement. BUS105's *master budget* is an internal operating plan — different course.

Depreciation methods: enough to treat dep. as **noncash** on the SCF and as a **tax shield** in project CF. The journal → **BUS103**.

### Check C — Time value of money (the toolkit)

If money moves at different dates, you **cannot add the nominal amounts**.

**Lump sums**

- Compound: **FV = PV (1 + r)^n**
- Discount: **PV = FV / (1 + r)^n**
- Simple vs compound (LO): simple ignores interest-on-interest; compound does not. More frequent compounding raises FV.

**Non-annual compounding (Unit 3 LO 5):** if the stated rate is quoted m times a year, periodic rate = r/m, periods = n·m. Effective annual: **EAR = (1 + r/m)^m − 1**. Compare products on EAR, not on the tattooed APR.

**Multiple cash flows:** move each flow to the *same* date, then add. If they are the same size, same gap, same r:

| Pattern | Intro formula (ordinary / end-of-period) |
|---|---|
| Ordinary annuity PV | PMT × [1 − (1+r)^(−n)] / r |
| Ordinary annuity FV | PMT × [(1+r)^n − 1] / r |
| Annuity due | ordinary × (1+r) |
| Perpetuity PV | PMT / r |

Yield / I/Y is the rate that prices the stream — this is the bridge to **YTM** (Check D) and **IRR** (Check E).

Do not paste a 12-key financial-calculator sequence unless the user asks. Show the relation, then the number.

### Check D — Price the claims; price the risk (stocks, bonds, CAPM)

**Compare the instruments first (CLO 4 / Unit 4 LOs):**

| | Bond (debt) | Common stock (equity) |
|---|---|---|
| Claim | Contractual coupons + par | Residual |
| Upside | Capped (unless convertible) | Unlimited |
| Control | Covenants; no vote (usually) | Votes |
| Failure mode | Default / distress | Dilution, no dividend, price drop |
| Intro price | PV of coupons + PV of par at y | Discounted expected dividends (or FCF to equity) |

**Bond.** Price at the **market** rate, not the coupon. Coupon > y → premium; coupon < y → discount; equal → par. Interest-rate risk: longer maturity and lower coupon move more when y moves. Other named risks: reinvestment, default/credit, liquidity, inflation. Term structure / liquidity preference are *why y exists*, not a Fed course.

**Stock.** Common vs preferred (preferred: dividend priority, usually no vote). Intro default: **Gordon / constant-growth P₀ = D₁ / (k − g)** with g < k; no-growth = D / k. P/E, dividend yield, book vs market are *reads*, not a third model. Value / growth / income are named styles. Indexes (DJIA, S&P 500, NASDAQ) are the market proxy β lives on.

**Markets.** Primary (issuer gets cash) vs secondary (investor-to-investor; liquidity and price discovery). OTC vs exchange. Intermediaries match surplus and deficit units (Unit 4 LO 4). EMH: prices reflect information (weak / semi-strong / strong as the standard three forms). Encode as "you don't get a free lunch from public info," not as a theology.

**Convertibles / options / derivatives (Unit 4.4):** name them. Convertible = cheaper coupon for the issuer, dilution on convert, bond floor for the holder. Option = right not obligation (call/put). Forwards/futures/swaps = hedge, speculate, or arbitrage. **Do not** run Black-Scholes unless asked.

**Risk and return (CLO 5):**

- Stand-alone: expected return = Σ pᵢ rᵢ; σ as spread.
- **Systematic** (market; survives diversification) vs **unsystematic** (specific; diversification kills it). Recommend diversified portfolios (Unit 5 LO 4); do not promise zero risk.
- **β:** move vs the market. β=1 with the market; >1 amplifies; <1 dampens.
- **CAPM / SML: r = r_f + β (r_m − r_f)**. This *is* the required return that feeds cost of equity and, through WACC, NPV. If they hand you a "higher profit plant in a riskier country vs a safer smaller plant," do not pick on profit alone — that is the Unit 5 worked fork.

### Check E — Spend and fund: projects, WACC, working capital

**Capital budgeting (CLO 6).** Process: identify → estimate **incremental** CF → evaluate → select → monitor.

Relevant CF = future cash that **differs** with the project. Sunk is out. Cannibalization / synergies in. NWC up-front, recover at the end. Depreciation tax shield in. Financing CF (interest) stays **out** of project CF when you discount at WACC — don't double-count.

| Rule | Accept if | Good at | Failure if used alone |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NPV** | NPV > 0 | Value added; the default when methods fight | Garbage CF / wrong r |
| **IRR** | IRR > hurdle | Intuitive % | Mutually exclusive scale/timing; multiple IRRs on sign-flipping CF |
| **PI** | PI > 1 | Ranking when capital is rationed | Same scale fights as IRR |
| **Payback** | Years < cutoff | Liquidity / "how fast am I out" | Ignores TVM and cash after cutoff (discounted payback is the patch) |

MIRR / ARR are *named* in the Unit 6.1 blurb; do not require them. **Hand off BUS105** if the real question is make-or-buy, drop-a-line, or special order (relevant *operating* cost, not a multi-year project). Stay here for "does this long-lived project earn more than the cost of capital."

**Capital structure and WACC (CLO 7).** Mix of debt + equity (+ preferred when it exists). Measure with D/E, debt-to-capital, TIE; prefer **market** weights over book (Unit 7 LO 3).

- **M&M (as taught):** perfect markets, no taxes, no distress → structure does not change firm value, which is why adding *tax-deductible* debt looks free. **With taxes:** interest shield. **In reality:** distress / bankruptcy costs rise with debt. Live sentence: find a *balanced* structure.
- **Trade-off:** shield vs distress (marginal benefit of debt falls as you lever). **Pecking order:** internal cash → debt → equity last (asymmetric information). Signaling / agency theories named; don't write a journal article.
- **WACC = w_e r_e + w_d r_d (1 − T_c)** (+ w_p r_p). r_e from CAPM (Check D) or dividend-growth; r_d from YTM, **after tax**. WACC is the NPV discount rate when project risk ≈ firm risk. A riskier project needs a higher hurdle, not the company WACC by default.

**Working capital (CLO 8).** NWC = CA − CL. Objective: operate without bouncing checks and without trapping cash. Four named buckets: **cash, AR, inventory, AP**. CCC ≈ inventory days + AR days − AP days.

- Cash: timing of inflows vs bills. Float, target balance, parking in marketable securities.
- AR: who gets credit, on what terms, how you collect. Tighten terms → faster cash, maybe lost sales.
- Inventory: too much spoils/obsolesces; too little loses the sale. Minimize the *cost of the policy*. Ops transformation → **BUS300**. Allowance *journals* → **BUS103**.
- Short-term sources: trade credit, bank lines, paper, factoring, seasonal loans. Match maturities. The catering-job picture: you buy inputs *before* the invoice pays.

Poor short-term management is a named reason firms die. A beautiful NPV does not save a firm that cannot make payroll.

## 2. Worked micro-patterns

**"What is finance / how is it not accounting?"**
Check A in one paragraph. Past vs decide-what-to-do. Shareholder-wealth goal. Hook: "if I give you money today, how much do I get back?"

**"Should we take this project / buy this building?"**
Check E. List incremental CF (including NWC and dep. shield). Discount at a hurdle that matches risk (WACC if firm-like). Report NPV first; IRR/PI/payback as supporting screens. If methods fight, say why and keep NPV.

**"What's this bond / stock worth?"**
Check D. Bond: PV coupons + PV par at y. Stock: DDM if a dividend path is given; otherwise say which input is missing rather than inventing g. Required k from CAPM if they gave β.

**"Is this firm healthy / should we lend or invest?"**
Check B with a baseline, then D (price vs book / CAPM required vs expected) and E (leverage + CCC). Profitable + negative operating cash is the trap.

**"How much should we borrow?"**
Check E structure. Name the tax shield *and* distress. Pecking-order default if they have unused internal cash. Compute WACC at two mixes only if they gave costs; don't optimize a 40-line model.

**"We're profitable and still bouncing checks."**
Check E working capital. Name the lag (AR, inventory, seasonal gap) and a short-term source. Do not rebuild the accounting cycle unless they also need BUS103.

**"FIFO or LIFO / journal this / close the books."**
Wrong course. **BUS103**.

**"Break-even units / make-or-buy / drop the line."**
Wrong course. **BUS105**. (If they then ask "and should we buy the machine that replaces the line," come back here for NPV.)

**"What is money / why gold / Fed history."**
Wrong course. **ECON120 / ECON135**.
