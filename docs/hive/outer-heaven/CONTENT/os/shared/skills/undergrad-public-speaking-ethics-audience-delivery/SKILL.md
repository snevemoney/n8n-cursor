**Primary goal:** an **ethical public speech that a specific audience can follow**. That is not "pass Toastmasters," not "write the memo" (**BUS210**), and not "define human communication" as a survey (**COMM100**).

**Named live tying themes:** **information and ethics**. Abundance is the extra challenge on top of anxiety. Ethical communication = **own what you present** and **speak up when others abuse platforms**. Named live Unit 1 tools (guest Course Map): **dialogical theory**; **ethics pyramid** (evaluate speaker *or* listener choices); **three most common types** of public speaking (inform / persuade / entertain — Units 7–8 spend them).

**Named live failure modes:** presenting information you have not earned; ignoring the audience; letting apprehension stop the job; collapsing this course into business writing or a human-communication survey.

**Named live apprehension job (CLO 12 / Unit 1):** physiological symptoms; **four types of communication apprehension**; techniques for coping before and during. Named map titles: trait anxiety, state anxiety, scrutiny fear, cognitive restructuring, coping with the unexpected. Encode the *job* (name the type, pick a before/during technique). Exact four-type wording is a **login gap** if the readings differ from the map titles.

**Level check:** if the prompt is actually COMM100 five-unit survey homework, **hand off COMM100** and recap only. If it is BUS210 nine-unit bizcomm, **hand off BUS210**. If it is PRDV008's mini, **hand off PRDV008**. If it is "give me the COMM101 final items," **refuse exam reconstruction**.

### Check B — Audience analysis and effective listening (when "who is in the room / are they even hearing this" is in play)

CLO 3 and CLO 2. Unit 2.

Live order: **audience before listening**. SNLS/SUSO put listening first. Do not teach the stale order as live.

CLO 3: audience analysis that includes **purpose, topic selection, and delivery strategies**. Map: establish **credibility** from audience traits; **demographic, psychographic, and situational** information.

CLO 2: critical listening and constructive criticism as **listener and speaker**. Map: listening styles; eliminate **noise**; stages of listening; improve critical listening habits. Map 2c Recognize vs Describe is **claim vs data** — encode the job (styles), confirm the verb on login.

**Hand off BUS210 Unit 3** for perception / iceberg *in a business-writing* key. **Hand off COMM100** if listening is being asked as a survey competency across contexts. Stay here for "this audience, this speech."

### Check C — Purpose, topic, outline (when "what is this speech *for* / how do I outline it" is in play)

Map: **general purpose**; common forms of informative, persuasive, and entertaining speeches; **specific purpose statements**. Three outline types: **working outline, full-sentence outline, speaking outline**. Five outline principles: **singularity, consistency, adequacy, uniformity, parallelism**.

Live fold: outlining sits **with purpose**, not after the body (SUSO ch.12 / SNLS Unit 10). Encode "you outline because you have a purpose," not "outlining is a late chapter."

CLO 8 is also spent in Units 7–8 (evaluate informative / persuasive outlines). Do not force a 1:1 CLO=unit story.

**Hand off ENGL101** if they want composition as a *course*. Stay here for speech-assignment prep.

### Check D — Research, support, cite (when "where did this come from / is this ethical to say" is in play)

Map: primary vs secondary; research needs from topic/purpose; academic vs nonacademic; value of sources by guidelines; **paraphrase and summarize** for citing; revise passages that display ethical issues in source use; **three reasons and four criteria** for using support; **five types of supporting materials**.

### Check E — Organize body, introduction, conclusion (when "how does this speech *move*" is in play)

CLO 6: select an effective **pattern of organization** for a listening audience. CLO 7: effective **introduction and conclusion**.

Map: main points that support specific purposes; organizational patterns; **transitions and signposts**; intro functions; attention-getters **based on audience analysis**; **clear and complete thesis statements**; conclusion functions and techniques.

**Persuade (Unit 8).** Map: apply **social judgment theory, cognitive dissonance theory, and the elaboration likelihood model**; **four types of persuasive claims**; **two types of policy claims**; organizational patterns for persuasive speeches; evaluate a persuasive outline. ## 2. Worked patterns (shape only — not exam items)

**"I have a topic and I freeze."**
Check A (ethics + apprehension: name the type; before/during technique; information-responsibility still applies) → Check B (who is listening — freeze is often an audience-picture failure) → Check C (specific purpose + speaking outline, not a manuscript). Do not turn it into a therapy course. Do not skip ethics because they are anxious.

**"Write me a persuasive speech."**
Check A (is this actually a public speech, or a BUS210 memo / COMM411 campaign?) → Check B (audience: demographic / psychographic / situational; credibility) → Check C (specific purpose; working then full-sentence then speaking outline) → Check D (research + cite spoken and written) → Check E (pattern, thesis, open/close) → Check F (Monroe / claims / SJT-CDT-ELM as the *job*, four delivery modes, aids). Do not dump a canned Monroe script as an exam key.

**"Is this informative or persuasive?"**
Check C (general purpose: pick one primary) → Check F Units 7 vs 8. Informative is not "persuade but nicer." Entertaining/special-occasion lives in Unit 7 with inform. Do not smuggle a sales pitch into an informative record.

**"Just give me the slides."**
Check F Unit 6: aids support words; they do not replace them. Critique organization / simplicity / clarity. If they want the PD mini's online-audience stack as a *course*, **hand off PRDV008**. If they want a workplace deck inside bizcomm, **hand off BUS210**.
