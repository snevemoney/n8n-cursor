# Intro project management: process groups + people (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks what a project *is*, how project management differs from ongoing operations or from general management, which process-group work is needed now, how to initiate/plan/execute/control/close, which knowledge area is the bottleneck, or how to lead a temporary team.

**Do not use when:** the job is reconstructing or taking a Saylor (or any) exam, dumping PMI *PMBOK Guide* / PMP item banks, running **BUS208** POLC-as-the-manager's-job, **BUS401** leadership-vs-management theory overlay, **BUS300** continuous operations/transformation, **BUS605** graduate strategic PM (ten KAs as the spine), or writing a plant SOP that never ends.

This is **one** skill. It is not "Unit 1 skill + Unit 2 skill…". BUS402's five units are one argument: **a project is a temporary unique effort, so you initiate it, plan it, do it, control it against a baseline, close it, and you do all of that through people.**

## 1. Procedure — answer an intro-PM question in six checks

Run in order. Skip a check only if the user already constrained the frame.

### Check A — Project vs operations (always)

Write this in one sentence before you name a Gantt chart:

1. **What unique result** is supposed to exist that does not exist now?
2. **When is it over** (objectives met, terminated, or need gone)?
3. **What standing process** (operations) will own the result after close — or is this work being allowed to live forever?

| | Project (this school) | Operations (BUS300 / the standing org) |
|---|---|---|
| Time | Temporary; defined start and finish | Ongoing; continuous improvement |
| Result | Unique product, service, or result | Repeatable goods/services |
| Manager's job | Start up, deliver under constraints, **close** | Keep the transformation running |
| Team | Temporary; often matrix; people keep a "real" boss | Standing reporting line |
| Failure mode | Never ends; becomes shadow operations | Treated as a one-off "project" every week |

**Project management** (school definition, PMI-class wording): application of knowledge, skills, tools, and techniques to project activities to meet the project requirements.

**Not this school:**

- BUS208 POLC: planning/organizing/leading/controlling as the *job of the manager of an ongoing firm*. Same verbs, different object.
- BUS401: "are they leading or managing?" / which leadership theory. Here the question is whether the *temporary unique effort* is authorized, planned, controlled, and closed.
- BUS300: capacity, inventory, process choice. Those tools can *serve* a project; they are not the definition of one.

Constraint triangle: **scope, time, cost** (quality the usual fourth). Change one, name what moves. "Yes to all three plus extra scope" is not a plan.

If asked "should we treat this as a project?," require unique result + finite end. If it is really BAU, send it to operations.

### Check B — Process groups vs knowledge areas vs phases (when "what kind of work" or "where are we" is in play)

Live CLO 2–3 want both **process groups** (the *kind of work*) and **knowledge areas** (the *what*). Do not stack them into one super-diagram.

**Five process groups** (live course intro — use these names):

| Group | Job | You are not done until |
|---|---|---|
| Initiating | Authorize; name the PM; identify stakeholders; write why | Someone with authority said *yes* (charter-class) |
| Planning | Progressively elaborate how: scope, schedule, cost, quality, resources, comms, risk, procurement | There is a **baseline** you will control against |
| Executing | Produce deliverables; coordinate people and vendors | Work is actually happening, not just scheduled |
| Monitoring and controlling | Compare actuals to baseline; change control; forecast | Variance is visible *and* acted on |
| Closing | Accept, close contracts, lessons, archive, release, celebrate | Operations (or the client) owns the result; the project does not linger |

Groups **overlap**. Planning is not a gate you never revisit. Monitoring runs for the whole middle. Closing can start on a contract while other work still executes.

**Phases** (CC spine; coarser, more sequential): initiation → planning → implementation → closeout. Watt sometimes folds execute+control into "implementation." Live Saylor **split** executing and monitoring. If the user says "implementation phase," translate to execute **and** monitor/control.

**Knowledge areas** (the *what*; CC spine is PMBOK-4 nine-KA; live list unconfirmed):

Integration (start-up, charter, change control, close) · Scope · Time/Schedule · Cost · Quality · Human resources / team · Communications · Risk · Procurement. **Stakeholder** may be its own KA (PMBOK 5–6; Watt remix; BUS605) or folded into start-up/comms (PMBOK 4 / Darnall). If the bottleneck is "who cares and who can kill this," name stakeholder work even if you do not pick an edition.

If asked "which theory/process is right?," answer with **the process group whose work is missing** and **the knowledge area that is the constraint**. Then name the people risk (Check F).

### Check C — Initiate and plan (when the project does not yet have a baseline)

**Initiate**

- Assign a project manager. Unowned projects are wishes.
- Charter-class authorization: purpose, high-level scope, high-level constraints, who has authority, known stakeholders. Alignment / kickoff. If the powerful stakeholders disagree, that *is* the initiation work.
- Selection: why this project, why now. Do not skip into a WBS because the idea is exciting.

**Plan** (Unit 2) — build a baseline, not a slogan:

| KA | Planning move | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Decompose work (WBS-class; 100% rule: everything in, nothing extra) | Gold-plating; fuzzy "and also" |
| Time | Activities, dependencies, **critical path**, float | Calendar wish without logic |
| Cost | Estimate → budget baseline | Mixing estimate with hope |
| Quality | How we will know it is good enough | "We'll test at the end" |
| Resources | Who/what, when; matrix reality | Assuming the org chart will free people |
| Communications | Who needs what, when, which channel | Status theater |
| Risk | Identify → analyze → respond (avoid/mitigate/transfer/accept) | Risk register as a graveyard |
| Procurement | Make vs buy; contract type | Vendors without acceptance tests |
| Integration | One plan, one change process | Ten plans that do not talk |

Progressive elaboration is allowed. "We will figure out scope in execution" is not planning.

If asked "what should we do first?," name authorization and stakeholder alignment before tools.

### Check D — Execute and monitor/control (when work is in motion, or variance is hiding)

**Execute:** produce the deliverables; manage the team; engage stakeholders; perform quality; conduct procurements. Most hours live here. Leadership here is coordination under a deadline, not a theory family (leave that to BUS401).

**Monitor and control** is its own live process group. Ask:

1. What was the **baseline** (scope/schedule/cost/quality)?
2. What is **actual**?
3. What is the **forecast** if nothing changes?
4. What **change** is being requested, and did it go through change control?

Scope creep is executing unplanned work because it felt helpful. The fix is a change decision (yes / no / defer), not a pep talk.

Earned-value *vocabulary* (if the numbers exist): planned value (what the baseline said we'd have earned by now), earned value (what we actually completed, valued at baseline), actual cost. SPI/CPI as ratios. Definition level only. No PMP formula dump. If the user has no PV/EV/AC, use percent-complete + remaining duration + remaining cost honestly.

Status reports that are always green until they are red are not monitoring.

### Check E — Close (when "we're basically done" or the work is trying to become operations)

Closing is a different style: details, contracts, decisions. Teams already looking at the next project will drift.

Closeout checklist (CC spine, summarized):

1. **Accept** the deliverables against the scope. Punch list / performance tests. If it was not in scope and they still want it, that is a change (or a new project), not a free add-on.
2. **Close contracts.** Pay what was earned; adjust for rejected or missing work.
3. **Lessons learned** / post-project review (what to repeat, what to stop). Archive.
4. **Release** people and stuff back to operations or to the next project.
5. **Celebrate** (Watt and Darnall both name this). Peak-performance teams are thanked on purpose; skipping it teaches the org that finishing does not matter.
6. **Hand off** the unique result to operations (BUS300's world). A project that never closes is a standing department with worse governance.

If asked "can we just keep a skeleton crew on it?," name whether this is still a project (unique remaining work + finite end) or operations in disguise.

### Check F — People skills / Talent Triangle (when the bottleneck is humans)

Live Unit 5 + course intro. CLO 4: skills **and** tools.

Course wording of PMI's Talent Triangle:

| Course phrase | Job on a project |
|---|---|
| Strategic thinking / business insight | Why this project exists for the firm; constraints; stakeholder map |
| Interpersonal communication | Temporary team, matrix bosses, client, vendors |
| (Ways of working — tools) | Process groups, baselines, change control |

CC spine (Darnall 1.4, summarized): the PM needs ordinary operational leadership (communication, team building, planning, expediting, motivating, political sensitivity) **plus** extra skill because the environment is time-sensitive, goal-driven, and usually a **matrix** (role ambiguity, conflict of loyalty). People seldom report only to the PM; project success may not be how they are personally rated. Plan for that. Do not assume BUS208's standing org chart.

Stakeholder work: identify → analyze interest/power → engage. Client expectations (Darnall 4) are a control loop, not a kickoff slide. Lack of stakeholder support is a named decelerator in the Watt remix — treat as a risk, not as personality.

Conflict and culture: a project culture can be built for the life of the effort (Darnall 5) without rewriting the firm's culture (that is BUS401 Unit 5). When the team dissolves, the standing culture remains.

If asked "how do I lead this with no solid line?," name (1) the authorization you actually have, (2) expert/referent influence on a *finite* deliverable, (3) a communication cadence, (4) what you will escalate through the charter's sponsor. Do not start a BUS401 theory-family tour unless they asked for one.

## 2. Worked shape (not an exam item)

Prompt shape: "We have to stand up a new client onboarding flow by Q4 and keep today's operation running."

Run the checks, short:

- A: unique result (new flow) + finite end (Q4) + operations keeps BAU → this is a **project** sitting on top of operations, not POLC of the standing team.
- B: you are probably still in initiate/plan if there is no baseline; if people are already "just building it," you skipped groups.
- C: charter (sponsor, Q4, out-of-scope: do not rewrite BAU), WBS of the flow, critical path into Q4, who is borrowed from ops, risks (ops will yank people at month-end).
- D: execute the build; monitor against Q4; change control when ops asks for extra screens.
- E: accept with the ops owner; lessons; release the borrowed people; do not leave a "hypercare forever" project.
- F: matrix — ops managers still own the people. Cadence with those managers is the political work.
