# Business law: sources, forum, wrongs, assets, entity (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks what law binds a US business decision, where a dispute should go (court vs ADR), whether the facts are a tort or a contract problem, what kind of property or IP is at stake, whether a worker is at-will, which entity is on the hook, or which regulatory regime (securities, SOX, antitrust, admin law) constrains the firm.

**Do not use when:** the job is reconstructing or taking a Saylor (or any) exam, dumping Lau / Mayer, running **BUS101** intro-business survey, **BUS200** virtue/utilitarianism/deontology/CSR, **BUS208** POLC management, **BUS301** HRM as a management function, **BUS403 / BUS641** negotiation-as-skill, **BUS651** entrepreneur legal lifecycle, **POLSC101/201/221/232/401/402** political science, **PHIL103** moral/political philosophy, **PRDV231** ethical leadership, a JD / bar / UCC-depth commercial-law clinic, or giving legal advice / evasion.

This is **one** skill. It is not "Unit 1 skill + Unit 2 skill…". BUS205's ten units are one argument: **business law is the legal environment managers must operate inside** — protect one party from another, fill ethics voids with statutes after Enron/2008-style failures, then walk sources → forum → private wrongs → assets → people/entity → regulation, under US survey rules, not as a lawyer.

## 1. Procedure — answer a business-law question in six checks

Run in order. Skip a check only if the user already constrained the frame.

### Check A — Identity: sources and courts (always)

**Law protects one party from another** (customers from firms, firms from firms, citizens/corporations from government). It is neither perfect nor all-encompassing. **Ethics fills the voids**; when a group has systematically violated those ethics, we **write laws** (Enron → accounting statutes; 2008 → proposed financial-services rules). A bank cannot charge any rate it wants; a carmaker cannot ignore pollution rules.

**Name the source before you name the rule:**

| Source (intro depth) | What a manager uses it for |
|---|---|
| Constitution | Outer bounds (commerce, Bill of Rights as they touch business). Not a POLSC course. |
| Statute | Legislature's command (SOX, Title VII, Sherman). |
| Regulation / administrative law | Agency rules that implement the statute. |
| Precedent / common law | What courts already did with similar facts. |

**Courts are the third branch.** Trial vs appellate at naming depth. Rule of law is a *business condition* (predictable enforcement), not a civics elective.

If the prompt is "how does power work in a political community / American government," **stop and hand off POLSC101 / POLSC232**. If it is "Aristotle vs Kant vs utility," **hand off BUS200**. If it is "close the books to SOX's satisfaction," **hand off BUS103** after you have named the statute here.

### Check B — Forum: litigate or ADR

Private legal problems go through **courts** or **less formal alternatives**. Court is expensive and slow. Managers try other ways first.

1. **Litigation on-ramp** (survey, not civ-pro): parties; **standing**; **personal jurisdiction**; pretrial; trial; appeal. If the court cannot hear you, stop — do not skip to damages.
2. **ADR menu:**
   - **Negotiation** — most informal and consensual. Historic public note names **BATNA / WATNA / bargaining zone**. Drawbacks exist (power imbalance, no record).
   - **Mediation** — third party helps the sides reach a deal; does not impose one.
   - **Arbitration** — non-governmental decision-maker; often **required by the contract**. Acts, in essence, as judge and jury in the matter.
3. Pick the forum that fits **cost, speed, relationship, and enforceability**. If ADR is unworkable from the start, say so and go to court.

**Hand off BUS403 / BUS641** when the job is *how to negotiate as a manager* (interests, ethics of bargaining, multicultural conduct). Stay here for "is this a lawsuit, an arbitration clause, or a conversation."

### Check C — Private wrongs: tort vs contract

Load-bearing split: **no contract → tort system; existing contract violated → contract system.** Mixing them is the classic undergrad error.

**Torts (CLO 4)** — civil wrongs; compensation, not (primarily) punishment:

| Type | One-line |
|---|---|
| Intentional | Tortfeasor acts with intent to cause the harm that results |
| Negligence | Failed to act as a reasonable person; **duty of care** + breach + causation + injury. You must prove the person was responsible for something and violated that responsibility. |
| Strict liability | Liable without regard to care (archive names dram-shop-style examples) |

Negligence per se (statute/ordinance violation that causes injury) at naming depth. Medical-negligence and car-crash-without-a-contract are the public exemplars.

**Contracts (CLOs 5–7)** — legally binding relationships. Usually: deliver a product or service at a specified time and date. **State law varies.**

1. **Formation / essential elements** (offer, acceptance, consideration, capacity, legality; formalities; oral vs unenforceable).
2. **Performance:** substantial vs strict; reasonable-person standard vs rarer personal-satisfaction standard.
3. **Failure and breach:** how a contract can fail; defenses.
4. **Remedies:** damages and equitable remedies.

Negotiable instruments, if they appear, are an **overview name**, not a UCC Article 3 course. Do not run Mayer's multi-chapter contracts stack.

If the prompt is "draft me a bulletproof founder contract / lease / sale-of-goods regime," **hand off BUS651** (and say this skill will not impersonate a lawyer). Stay here for "is there a contract, did it fail, what is the remedy — or is this a tort."

### Check D — Assets: property and IP

Two property categories:

- **Real property** — land and anything attached (house, fixtures). Interests and **how they pass** at survey depth.
- **Personal property** — everything else. The house-sale test: clothes leave; chandelier / built-in island / windows may not. Same object can be personal in one situation and real in another.

**Intellectual property** is anything developed through intellectual and creative processes. Firms invest because copying without permission would make the investment worthless. Encode the quartet, not a litigation clinic:

| Right | Manager question |
|---|---|
| Trademark | What can / cannot be marked; does this identify the source |
| Patent | What can / cannot be patented |
| Copyright | Has it been violated |
| Trade secret | Economic value from secrecy; stealing and defenses |

**Digital-era impact** is a live CLO (archive names DMCA / OCILLA / PRO-IP at *name* depth). The SNLS Forbes "IP is 80% of typical business value" line is an **archive claim** — do not reassert as a current statistic.

**Hand off CS406 / CS260** for security implementation. Stay here for "which right, if any, do we hold."

### Check E — People, crime, entity

**Employment (CLOs 12–13).** First question: **at-will or contract.** Then the statute overlay (names, not an HR manual): wage (minimum / prevailing), discrimination (Title VII and siblings), OSHA, union / collective bargaining / labor arbitration, whistleblower protection. Designed, in this school's public blurb, to protect the employee from the employer.

**Hand off BUS301** for HRM-as-management. **Hand off BUS209** for OB. Stay here for legal status + named statutes.

**Crime (CLOs 14–15).** Like torts, a wrong against a person or group — but **the state prosecutes**. Direct violation of an existing law. Prosecution, defense, often a jury. **White-collar crime** at naming/example depth. Same facts can be tort *and* crime; purpose differs (compensation vs punishment). Do not coach concealment.

**Entity (CLO 16).** Forms at survey depth:

| Form | Load-bearing tradeoff |
|---|---|
| Sole proprietorship | Simple; **personal** liability (archive: unpaid small-business loan → personal assets) |
| Partnership | Shared control; personal exposure unless limited |
| Limited-liability entity (LLC etc.) | Liability shield + extra formalities |
| Corporation | Liability shield for shareholders; **more regulation and fees**; what most employees work for |

Limited liability is the point of the unit, not a tax course. Corporate bankruptcy / expansion at **naming** depth only.

**Hand off BUS101** if they only need the intro-business "pick a form" chapter. **Hand off BUS651** if they are forming, capitalizing, or selling a venture.

### Check F — Regulation that binds the firm

**Major US business-regulating laws (CLO 17).** Archive names: **federal and state securities**, **Sarbanes-Oxley**, **antitrust**, **administrative law**. Live intro's Enron / 2008 pair lives here: ethics voids become statutes; SOX's manager-side consequence in the archive is firms going private to avoid it — **describe, do not prescribe**.

Watch what is proposed and what might be removed. This is not environmental law, immigration, or a consumer-protection clinic unless the live tree (still a gap) actually assigns them — say the gap.

**Hand off BUS103** to *book* the numbers. **Hand off BUS200** for CSR / integrity. **Hand off POLSC401** for ethics and public policy as political science. Stay here for "which regime binds this firm."

## 2. Worked micro-patterns

**"Can we charge whatever interest / skip the pollution gear?"**
Check A then F. Law constrains the decision. Ethics-void → statute is the public caution.

**"They botched the install — do we sue?"**
Check B then C. Negotiation → mediation → contract-required arbitration → court. Standing and jurisdiction before damages. If there was a contract, stay in contract; if not, tort.

**"Hit by a driver / surgeon took the wrong arm."**
Check C. No contract → tort. Name the type (usually negligence / duty of care). Do not jump to crime unless the state is prosecuting.

**"Is this a valid deal / they didn't perform / what do we get?"**
Check C contracts. Elements, how it fails, substantial vs strict, remedies. State law varies. Not a UCC clinic.

**"Does the chandelier stay with the house / can we take the island?"**
Check D. Real vs personal; fixture test.

**"Can they copy our logo / code / secret process?"**
Check D IP. Which right. Digital-era name if the copy is online. Not a takedown mill.

**"Can we fire them today / do we have to pay overtime / is this discrimination?"**
Check E. At-will vs contract first, then the named statute. HRM process → BUS301.

**"Should we incorporate so the bank can't take my house?"**
Check E entity. Limited liability vs extra regulation. Formation paperwork and capital raise → BUS651.

**"Is this Enron / do we need SOX / can we go private to dodge it?"**
Check F. Name the regime. Do not coach evasion. Booking the numbers → BUS103. "Was it ethical" → BUS200.

**"What's our BATNA in this vendor fight?"**
If they mean ADR vs lawsuit, Check B. If they mean how to bargain, **BUS403**.

**"Is this constitutional / how do we define legitimate governance?"**
Wrong home. **POLSC** (and Lau ch.5 is not a live unit). You may *name* that the Constitution is a source of law.

**"How do I pass the Saylor final / what's on the quiz?"**
Refuse. No items. No blueprint. Point at the public unit list and CLOs only. The SNLS Direct Credit box (50 MC / 2 hours / closed-book) is **archive**.
