# Entrepreneurial venture viability → plan → team → market → finance (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks whether a business idea is a viable entrepreneurial venture, how to structure or grow a small firm, how to build a founding team, how to write a business plan or marketing plan, how to fund a new venture, or how a small-business owner should exit — in the standard undergrad entrepreneurship / small-business-management school.

**Do not use when:** the job is reconstructing or taking a Saylor (or any) exam, dumping Barringer/Scarborough/Longenecker/Hisrich, running **BUS101** intro-to-business, **BUS203/BUS502/BUS632** marketing-major / digital-ad specialty, **BUS202/BUS601** corporate finance, **BUS103/BUS105** accounting cycle, **BUS208** POLC management, **BUS301** seven-roles HRM, **BUS401/BUS650** leadership capstone / graduate entrepreneurial leadership, **BUS200/BUS205/BUS651** ethics or law specialties, **BUS300** operations/SCM, **BUS652–654** graduate ideation/launch/seminar, MindEdge "Small Business Management (ACE CREDIT)" (different school), promising current ACE transfer credit, or writing a scam / fraud / fake-investor playbook.

This is **one** skill. It is not "Unit 1 skill + Unit 2 skill…". BUS305's five units are one argument: **an idea becomes a venture only when it is a viable opportunity, staffed, written as a plan, taken to a market, and financed — including how you will get out.**

## 1. Procedure — answer a new-venture question in six checks

Run in order. Skip a check only if the user already constrained the frame.

### Check A — Viable venture? (always)

**The course question:** is this a **business idea** or a **viable entrepreneurial opportunity** — and is the person actually an entrepreneur for *this* bet?

**Teaching definitions (live intro + SNLS, summarize):**

- **Entrepreneur** (from *entreprendre*, “to undertake”): owner or manager of a business enterprise who attempts to make profits by starting and growing their business. Diverse risk-takers who cultivate ideas into viable opportunities.
- SNLS: entrepreneurs **assume the risk** of creating an enterprise that will return capital employed.
- **Intrapreneur** ≠ entrepreneur (innovation *inside* an existing firm).
- **Small / lifestyle business** ≠ **high-growth enterprise**. CLO 2: growth strategy has to match the **structure**. A living-wage shop and a venture-scale startup are different plans.

**Three threads** (Cadden ch.1, summarize — standing test on every later check):

| Thread | What it is in this school | Failure |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer value** | Benefits the customer gets minus costs of obtaining the offering | Building what *you* love with no buyer |
| **Cash flow** | Money in and out; survival = meeting obligations, not accrual theater | Profitable on paper, cannot make payroll |
| **Digital / e-environment** | E-business = internal efficiency (no revenue by itself); e-commerce = the revenue subset | Treating a website as a strategy, or ignoring 24/7 customer expectation |

Schumpeter (SNLS, confirm live): entrepreneur converts an idea into innovation; **creative destruction**. Use as a *name* for dynamism, not as a history-of-thought paper.

Judgment questions this school recites (not empirically right/wrong): Do I want to be responsible for a business? What product/service? What market and where? Is the potential a living wage? How to raise capital? Full- or part-time? Alone or with partners?

If asked "should I start this / is this viable?," name **idea vs opportunity**, put the offering on **customer value**, and say whether **cash flow** can survive the launch. Do not skip to a logo or an LLC mill.

**Hand off BUS101** if the prompt is "what is a business / what are the functions." Stay here for **this venture**.

### Check B — Constrain the launch (when legal form, IP, ethics/CSR, or "which structure" is in play)

CLO 2: optimal growth strategies in the context of **different types of small business structures**. SNLS Unit 1 names **legal forms** and **intellectual property** next to **ethics**.

Encode as **constraints on the bet**, not as a law-school or ethics-course answer.

- Legal form (sole prop / partnership / LLC / corporation as *names*): liability, tax, and investor-readiness tradeoffs. **Hand off BUS205** for black-letter business law.
- IP (copyright / patent / trademark as *names*): what you can stop others from copying. **Hand off BUS205**; stay here for "is the idea even protectable enough to pitch."
- Ethics / CSR: overlay on the entrepreneur, not BUS200's theory course. **Hand off BUS200** for the ethics *course*.
- Family business (Cadden ch.3, folded): structure + conflict + succession teaser. Succession as an **exit** belongs in Check F.

### Check C — Staff the venture (when co-founders, first hire, advisors, "I can't do this alone," or leadership-vs-management is in play)

SNLS: building an effective management team is a **primary launch step**. Map strengths/weaknesses; fill gaps.

| Move | What it is in this school | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| **Manager** | Delegate responsibility; decide for the team | Calling yourself a leader while still doing every task |
| **Leader** | Influence behavior; motivation as a tool, not manipulation | Reciting leadership-theory families (that's BUS401) |
| **Advisors / board** | Fill gaps you will not hire yet | A decorative board with no expertise |
| **First hire / A-players** | Family-and-friends labor has run out | Hiring before the work is a job |
| **Tuckman / EI** | Group-development and emotion as *team hygiene* | Turning Unit 2 into BUS209 OB |

CLO 3 lives here. **Hand off BUS208** for POLC as the manager's job. **Hand off BUS301** for the HR *system* (job analysis → recruit → appraise → compensate → OSHA). **Hand off BUS401/BUS650** for leadership-theory / graduate entrepreneurial leadership. Stay here for **the founding team of a small venture**.

### Check D — Write the business plan (when "do I need a plan," investors, bankers, or "just build it" is in play)

SNLS: the plan is how a great idea becomes an **opportunity**. It is a narrative of merits, risks, and rewards for investors, partners, and other stakeholders. **The plan is not the business.**

Two reasons this school recites to write one:

1. Operating discipline (feasibility, money, operations, marketing, value, distribution, finance).
2. External capital — banks, VCs, and angels will not fund a shrug.

Reluctance tropes to refuse: we don't need to plan; too busy; plans don't produce results; unfamiliar with the process.

**Product checklist** (SNLS outline — names, not a template dump): executive summary; mission/description; products/services; marketing strategy; operations; management; financial statements; expenses and capitalization; financial plan; franchise as an optional path.

**Audiences** (Cadden, summarize):

| Audience | What they overweight in this school |
|---|---|
| **Bankers** | Collateral; industry-standard projections; SBA still wants owner skin |
| **Venture capitalists** | Outsize returns, team track record, uniqueness, market size; often a personal intro + a pitch |
| **Angel investors** | High-but-lower-than-VC returns; often operators; may take board/equity/approval rights |

A pitch deck with no numbers is not a plan. A 40-page plan that nobody can tell in two minutes is not a plan either. CLO 4 is **business plan and marketing plan** — Unit 3 owns the venture narrative; Unit 4 owns go-to-market.

### Check E — Go to market (when customers, pricing, competition, "who will buy this," or marketing plan is in play)

Live Unit 4: marketing strategies that achieve **business objectives** and build **competitive advantage**. Research → forecast → written marketing plan (inside the business plan or standalone).

Spine this school names:

- Primary vs secondary research.
- Demand: growth potential, trends, barriers, technology.
- Features vs **benefits**; customers; competition; niche.
- STP + 4Ps (product / price / place / promotion, including IMC).
- Marketing-plan sections: executive summary, situation, customer, competitor, partners, climate (PESTEL-class), segmentation, strategies, pricing, promotion, distribution.

Three-threads overlay: a marketing spend that does not add **customer value** or that wrecks **cash flow** is not a strategy.

**Hand off BUS203** for the marketing *major*. **Hand off BUS632** for digital-ad specialty. Stay here for "will anyone buy this, at what price, through what channel, and can I write that down as part of the venture."

### Check F — Fund it and name the exit (when money, loans, angels, VC, grants, books/taxes, growth, or "how do I get out" is in play)

Every entrepreneur needs money. Start-up expenses **and** operating capital. The financials in the plan **demonstrate profit potential** and **guide** management.

**Books (CLO 5):** why a small firm needs an accounting system, what statements *do*, and that taxes matter. Cash-based vs accrual; liquidity; break-even as a pricing/volume test. **Hand off BUS103/BUS105** for the accounting *cycle*. **Hand off BUS202** for corporate-finance valuation. Stay here for **can this venture pay its bills**.

**Funding menu** (SNLS names — encode *fit*, not a 2026 rate sheet): banks; venture capital; government grants (compliance because taxpayer dollars; state SBDC overlay); barter; customer financing / crowdfunding; SBIR; asset-based lenders; angels. Match the source to the bet (collateral vs equity vs paperwork vs customer-as-investor risk).

**Growth (SNLS 5.3)** is a financing problem as much as a marketing problem: more sales that destroy cash flow are not success.

**Exit (CLO 7 / Cadden ch.14, summarize):** plan it **while building**. Best exit = match to the firm **and** the owner's goals (money vs legacy vs control vs IP). Menu this school names:

| Path | What it is here | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Liquidation / walkaway | Sell assets; walkaway = debts paid | Destroying goodwill by just locking the door |
| Family succession | Multi-stage HR problem; few firms survive gen-2/3 | Handing keys over on a Sunday |
| Sell (acquisition / friendly / employees / open market) | Most common small-firm path; many listed businesses never sell | Asking price as a vanity number |
| Bankruptcy | Ch.7 liquidate vs Ch.11 oversee — *names* | DIY legal advice |
| IPO | Rare, expensive, analyst marriage | Treating "we'll IPO" as a plan |

**Hand off BUS651** for graduate entrepreneurial *law*. Do not write a "how to hide from creditors" script.

## 3. Worked micro-patterns

**"Is this a good business idea / should I quit my job?"**
Check A. Idea vs opportunity. Customer value. Cash flow survival. Small vs high-growth. The SNLS judgment questions. Do not start with a logo.

**"LLC or corporation / should I trademark this?"**
Check B, then A. Structure and IP as *constraints on the bet*. Full law → BUS205.

**"I need a co-founder / my first employee / a board."**
Check C. Strengths/gaps. Leadership vs management. Advisors vs decorative board. First hire only when family-and-friends labor has run out. HR system → BUS301. Leadership theories → BUS401.

**"Do I really need a business plan / VCs just want a deck?"**
Check D. Plan ≠ the business; plan *is* how the idea becomes an opportunity you can tell. Bankers/VC/angels overweight different sections. "Too busy to plan" is a named failure mode.

**"Who is the customer / what should I charge / write my marketing plan."**
Check E. Research → STP/4Ps → written plan. Features vs benefits. Spend that does not add value or that wrecks cash is not strategy. Marketing *course* → BUS203.

**"How do I fund this / SBA loan vs angel vs Kickstarter?"**
Check F, after A and D. Fit the source to the bet. Grants mean paperwork. Customer financing is a risk to the relationship. Accounting-system CLO is *why the books exist*, not a journal-entry drill.

**"How do I get out / should I sell / pass it to my kids?"**
Check F. Exit is designed while building. Succession is a process, not a weekend. IPO is almost never the small-firm answer in this school. Liquidation destroys goodwill.

**"What are the four functions of management?"**
**Hand off BUS208.** You may name that BUS305 *uses* management-vs-leadership inside a founding team.
