# Planning and strategy: goals, vision, change (clustered)

**Use when:** a prompt needs **introduction to business planning and strategy** — managers planning and coordinating work toward goals; identifying resources (people, finances, equipment); company-wide strategy plus interim short-term goals; vision statements; planning and executing change — in the Saylor PRDV227 **Introduction to Business Planning and Strategy** school (6 h). **Never send.** Building mode: never pitch.

**Do not use when:** reconstructing exams; **PRDV221** why-managers-exist; **PRDV228** decision-making *as the course*; **PRDV229** organization structure / change / future of management *as the course*; hive four-blank / Product GTM send; inventing SWOT, Porter, or other framework names; treating the reused late-1800s **description** as this course’s job; promising ACE; **sending**.

## 1. Procedure — seven checks

Run in order. Stop if Check A says another school or send.

### Check A — Identity overlay (always)

| Prompt smells like | Hand off |
|---|---|
| "Why do managers exist / four functions dump" | **PRDV221** |
| "How do I *decide* / decision process" | **PRDV228** |
| "Org chart / structure / future of management" | **PRDV229** |
| "Plan goals, strategy, vision, execute change" | **this skill** |
| "Send / pitch / GTM this plan" | **HITL — refuse send** |

**Primary goal:** a manager who plans resources, sets clear goals (long and interim), and ties them to strategy without sending a deck. Named failure mode: invented framework names, pitching, or treating this as 228/229.

### Check B — Clearly identified goals (when "we just stay busy" is in play)

CLO 1. Units 3 and 5 overlay.

**Explain the importance of developing clearly identified goals and objectives.** Live intro: short-term interim goals focus activities and provide direction to employees.

Shape: goals as focusing devices, not wallpaper. Do not invent SMART as live unless a later harvest shows it (guest did not).

### Check C — Components of business strategy (when "what even is strategy" is in play)

CLO 2. Units 1, 6, 7.

**Illustrate the components of business strategy.** Unit 1 title: Management Strategy Frameworks. Guest named **no** framework.

Shape: components as a job. **Do not name SWOT, Porter, BCG, or any other model.** Same unnamed rule as Tuckman / motivation theories. Unit 7: strategy is essential for management success — overlay, not a fifth CLO.

### Check D — Goals, objectives, vision statements (when "write the vision" is in play)

CLO 3. Units 3–5.

**Explain how to develop and achieve organizational goals, objectives, and vision statements.** Vision statements are **live**. Goal-setting strategies unit is overview, unnamed.

Shape: develop *and* achieve. Vision is in this CLO. Do not send the vision email.

### Check E — Connect goals to corporate strategy (when "goals don't match the company" is in play)

Unit 4.

Live unit: Connecting Goals and Objectives with Corporate Strategy. Intro: company-wide strategy and long-term goals sit beside interim short-term goals.

Shape: line-of-sight from daily goals to corporate strategy. Not a GTM funnel. Not four-blank SKU.

### Check F — Managers make choices (when "we need a pick" is in play)

Unit 2 overlay.

Live unit title: Managers Have to Make Choices. If the prompt is the *decision process* as the course, hand **PRDV228**. Here it is strategy-choice, not the 228 school.

### Check G — Plan and execute change (when "we're changing how we work" is in play)

CLO 4.

**Analyze the significance of properly planning and executing change in an organization.** Live CLO. If the prompt is org *structure*, future of management, or change as the whole course, hand **PRDV229**. Here: change is planned and executed as part of strategy, not 229.
