# Graduate entrepreneurial law: life cycle, counsel, capitalize, protect IP (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks how a founder should **use the law** from startup through operations to expansion or sale — when to hire counsel, how to structure and capitalize, contracts and leases, sale-of-goods rules, operational liability, e-commerce/privacy, IP/licensing, creditors/bankruptcy, or buying/selling the firm — in the Saylor BUS651 **graduate** business-law-and-entrepreneurship school.

**Do not use when:** reconstructing exams; dumping statutes/Nolo/bar outlines; running **BUS205** undergrad business law (ACE SAYA-0024); **BUS608** ethics/strategy as a course; **BUS650** entrepreneurial leadership; **BUS652** ideation; **BUS305** undergrad foundations of entrepreneurship; treating the free 70% certificate as **MEI605** 80% proctored exams; promising ACE/transfer credit; giving **legal advice** or executing filings; personal-CFO money movement.

This is **one** skill. Ten live units are one argument: **walk the venture life cycle with the law as a decision tool — counsel, structure, capitalize, contract, sell goods, manage operational risk, e-commerce/privacy, IP, credit, then expand/buy/sell.**

- Four live CLOs: maximize opportunity / avoid risk; when/how to use legal advice; capitalization forms; IP law + company policy.

## 1. Procedure — six checks

### Check A — Stage and overlay (always)

Which life-cycle seat: start, operate, or expand/sell? If the prompt is undergrad torts/contracts survey → **BUS205**. If it is POLC/leadership → **BUS650**. If it is ideation → **BUS652**. If it is "should we sign this" as *legal advice* → **refuse; HITL counsel**. Stay here for founder-law *strategy*.

### Check B — Counsel (Unit 1, CLO 2)

When and how to utilize legal advice. Encode **call / don't call / what to bring**, not "always hire."

### Check C — Structure, tax/records, capitalize (Units 2–3, CLO 3)

Advantages and disadvantages of **different forms of capitalization**. Structure is its own unit. Do not freeze a live LLC/C-corp/S-corp doctrine until login. Tax/recordkeeping is compliance, not BUS103 accounting-as-a-course.

### Check D — Operate: contracts, goods, liability (Units 4–6)

Contracts and leases; regulation of the sale of goods; operational liabilities and risk management. Named counterparties in the intro: customers, vendors, competitors, employees. Do not dump UCC article numbers as CLO language.

### Check E — E-commerce, privacy, IP (Units 7–8, CLO 4)

IP law **and** company policy. Protect assets **and** avoid violating others. E-commerce and consumer privacy as their own unit. Spec page also names domestic and global e-commerce — confirm live labels on a later guest-safe pass.

### Check F — Credit then exit (Units 9–10)

Creditors' rights and bankruptcy; expanding, buying, and selling. Exit is a *legal* decision, not BUS654 seminar pitching.

## 2. Worked patterns (shape only)

**"Should I incorporate before the first invoice?"** Check A (startup) → Check B (counsel) → Check C (structure/capitalize). Not BUS205 survey. Not legal advice — name the *job* and HITL.

**"Vendor wants a two-year lease and our brand on their site."** Check D (leases) → Check E (IP/licensing + e-commerce).

**"We're selling the company."** Check F (expand/buy/sell) → Check C (what was capitalized) → Check E (what IP transfers).
