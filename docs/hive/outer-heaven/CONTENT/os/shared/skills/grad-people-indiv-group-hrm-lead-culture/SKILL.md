# Graduate managing people: individual → team → culture → change (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks how a manager should turn individual attributes into group performance inside a culture — how to read personality/perception/attitudes, which motivation lever raises productivity, how to communicate so a diverse team can actually work, how HRM principles staff the team, how to lead ethically in a high-performance shop, how to design groups, how culture helps or wrecks the plan, or how power/politics/change work in a volatile environment — in the Saylor BUS603 **graduate** managing-people school.

**Do not use when:** the job is reconstructing or taking a Saylor (or any) exam, dumping Saylor OB / OpenStax OB / HRM / Robbins / Dessler, running **BUS209** undergraduate three-level OB checklists (six-unit intro), **BUS301** job-analysis / recruit-select-train-appraise-pay as a *course*, **BUS208** POLC as the manager’s job, **BUS210** audience/purpose/channel as a writing course, **BUS401** strategic-leadership capstone, **BUS640** advanced OB, **BUS643** leading-change-as-course, **BUS403** negotiations-as-course, treating the free 70% certificate as **MBA603** / **MMGT603** 80% proctored exams, promising ACE/transfer credit, or writing a union-busting / discrimination-evasion / dark-pattern script.

This is **one** skill. It is not "Unit 1 skill + Unit 2 skill…". BUS603’s eight live units are one argument: **management of people is the firm’s most important function** — individuals walk in, become a group dynamic, and either hit strategic goals or do not — so diagnose the person, motivate for productivity, communicate to lead, staff through HRM, lead ethically, design the team, shape a winning culture, and navigate power/politics/change when the environment will not sit still.

## 1. Procedure — answer a graduate people-management question in six checks

Run in order. Skip a check only if the user already constrained the frame.

### Check A — Identity, goal, overlay (always)

**The course question:** is the user asking for an **OB definition**, an **HR cycle lab**, a **POLC recap**, a **memo**, or a **manager’s people decision** that is supposed to help the firm hit strategic goals?

Live test: “define OB at three levels / run Maslow as a course” is **BUS209**. “Write this job description / build a comp band / administer the CBA” is **BUS301**. “What are Fayol’s functions” is **BUS208**. “Audience, purpose, channel, tone” as a *writing* job is **BUS210**. “How do I get *these people* to perform, together, in this culture, in a volatile environment” is **this skill**.

**Primary goal: people hitting the firm’s strategic goals.** That is not “keep everyone happy,” not “maximize this quarter’s headcount,” and not “be a leader, not a manager.” Ethics, diversity, and the VUCA environment constrain the path; they are not a second objective that replaces performance.

Name the **level** before you prescribe (borrowed from BUS209, spent here as the manager’s map):

| Level | Object | Later check |
|---|---|---|
| Individual | Personality, perception, attitudes, motivation | B–C |
| Group / team | Communication, team design, conflict | C, E |
| Organization | HRM system, culture, structure, power, change | D, E, F |

**Destructive vs positive (live intro):** abusive supervision, personalized power, “high-performance” that burns people, fake culture slides vs socialized power, ethical climate, developmental feedback, a culture whose artifacts match its promotions.

**Level check:** if the prompt is actually undergrad homework in BUS209’s six units, **hand off BUS209** and recap only. If it is MBA603/MMGT603’s proctored 80% exam, **refuse exam reconstruction**; this skill is the *content* overlay, not the degree exam.

### Check B — Who walked in? (when personality, perception, attitudes, diversity-as-person, or “why is this employee like this” is in play)

CLO 1 (individual half) + CLO 6 seed. You do not run a psych seminar here. You **read the attributes so you can manage the group dynamic**.

**Fit:** person–job and person–organization. Personality and values are relatively stable; the situation still matters.

**Big Five as work-relevant language (not a horoscope):** extraversion, agreeableness, conscientiousness, neuroticism, openness. Conscientiousness is the most consistent performance correlate in this school. MBTI is popular and weak as a hiring tool — do not select with it.

**Perception:** we do not see the job, we see a **constructed** job. Attribution (internal vs external; fundamental attribution / self-serving). Stereotypes and first impressions. Attitudes (satisfaction, commitment, engagement) predict OCBs / CWBs / turnover **imperfectly**.

**Diversity at the person level** is a fact. CLO 6 spends it later as an *HR and culture* decision. Do not reduce a person to a demographic checkbox, and do not pretend difference is invisible.

**Stress / emotions** are a performance constraint (overlay, not a live unit). Burnout is not a motivation failure.

**Hand off BUS209** if they need three-level OB as the *course*. **Hand off PSYCH101** for trait theory as a major. Stay here for “this is what walked in; now manage it.”

### Check C — What will actually move performance? (when motivation, productivity, rewards, or “they’re not trying” is in play)

CLO 2. Write the identity **before** naming Maslow:

**Performance ≈ motivation × ability × environment**, given accurate role perceptions. Being motivated ≠ being a high performer. A bored expert in a broken system is not a pep-talk problem.

**Need-based (what is missing):**

| Name | Use in this school | Failure if you ignore it |
|---|---|---|
| Maslow | Diagnostic reminder of pressing needs | Treating it as a strict workplace ladder |
| ERG | Multiple needs; frustration-regression | Assuming blocked growth just sits there |
| Herzberg | Hygiene prevents dissatisfaction; motivators create it | Dumping money on a boring job and calling it motivation |
| McClelland | nAch / nAff / nPow | Promoting a high nAch individual contributor into a manager role and wondering why they cannot delegate |

**Process (the story they are telling):** equity / organizational justice (distributive / procedural / interactional); expectancy (effort→performance→outcome, times valence); reinforcement; goal-setting (specific, difficult, accepted, feedback).

**Manager tools:** job design (Hackman/Oldham-class JCM as a named screen), goals, appraisal, incentives. Appraisal/pay that is actually an HR system → Check D. Do not run BUS301’s compensation chapter here.

**Hand off BUS209 Unit 2** for the undergrad motivation lab as a *course*. Stay here for “which lever raises productivity on *this* team.”

### Check D — How are we staffing and talking? (when hire/fire/train/appraise/pay, diversity-as-decision, or “send a message” is in play)

CLO 3 + CLO 6. Two different jobs that this course insists you keep on the same manager:

**Communication (Unit 3) — how you lead diverse groups and create teams.**

Process: sender → encode → channel → decode → receiver → feedback. Barriers: filtering, selective perception, overload, emotion, language, silence. Channel richness vs lean. Direction: down / up / lateral / grapevine.

BUS210 recap at *name* depth only: **audience, purpose, channel, tone, feedback**. Customize. A people-manager who cannot change the message for the audience will not lead a diverse team. **Hand off BUS210** for genres, slides, and the writing course.

Ethics: CLO 5 starts as soon as the message can deceive. No fake-urgency, no undisclosed tracking, no dark patterns.

**Human capital (Unit 4) — HRM principles as the staffing system, not a personnel office.**

Cycle at literacy depth: job analysis → recruit → select (tests/interviews as *screens*, not I-O psychometrics) → train/develop → appraise → compensate/retain → protect (EEO / safety as **named constraints**) → relate (voice; unions as a named option).

CLO 6: a diverse environment changes **who you hire, how you appraise, who you promote**. That is a decision, not a poster.

Title VII–class EEO and OSHA-class safety are constraints. Course-era boxes — **re-fetch current law**. Do not invent evasion procedures. **Hand off BUS205 / BUS301** for the law or HR *course*. **Hand off SHRM-CP / PHR** coaching.

Stay here for “this staffing or message move changes the team I am trying to lead.”

### Check E — Can this collection of people actually be a team, and who is leading it? (when leadership, teams, conflict, or “we need a stronger leader” is in play)

CLO 4 + CLO 5.

**Leadership vs management:** management produces order (POLC). Leadership is influence toward a direction people will actually follow. You need both. “I am a leader, not a manager” is a failure mode.

Named families (tools, not monographs): trait (weak as a selection algorithm); behavioral (initiating structure vs consideration); contingency (match person/situation — Fiedler / situational / path-goal as names; do not memorize every cell); contemporary (transformational / transactional / servant / authentic / LMX as names). Charisma is a tool, not a moral free pass. CLO 5: high-performance does not waive ethics.

**Group vs team:** a group shares a category; a team shares an interdependent goal and mutual accountability. Do not call every meeting a team.

Team kit: Tuckman-class stages as a *map*, not a law; roles; norms; cohesiveness; social loafing; groupthink as a named failure; diversity as a performance *and* conflict input.

**Conflict overlay:** task vs relationship vs process. Dual-concern styles (compete / collaborate / compromise / avoid / accommodate). Collaborate is not always right. **Hand off BUS403** for BATNA / deal process as a *course*. Stay here for “the team is stuck.”

**Hand off BUS401** if the prompt is firm-level strategic leadership. **Hand off BUS208** if they need POLC as nine units. **Hand off BUS209 Unit 3–4** if they need groups/leadership as the undergrad *course objects*. Stay here for “I am the manager of this team in a complex environment.”

### Check F — Will the culture, power map, and change path let this work? (when culture, politics, org design, volatility, or “we need a transformation” is in play)

CLO 7 + CLO 8. A beautiful motivation plan dies in a culture that punishes it, or in a change that nobody has the power to land.

**Culture (Unit 7):** assumptions → values → artifacts (Schein-class). Artifacts are visible and easy to fake; look at who gets promoted. ASA (attraction–selection–attrition) explains why culture sticks. “Winning” = helps people hit **strategic goals**, not a values slide. A toxic high-performer shop is a **destructive practice** this course wants named. Diversity here is climate: does difference get used or punished?

**Power and politics (Unit 8):** French & Raven-class bases (legitimate, reward, coercive, expert, referent) plus information/network. Influence tactics. Politics = power in action — not automatically unethical, and not a strategy. Informal networks are how work actually moves.

**Org design:** mechanistic vs organic; functional / divisional / matrix as named forms. Structure is how you *arrange* the people from Checks B–E. **Hand off BUS208** for Organizing as a POLC unit.

**Change through volatility:** Lewin unfreeze–change–refreeze as the intro default; Kotter-class eight steps as a named overlay. Resistance is information, not insubordination. The live catalog’s dynamic / volatile / uncertain environment means this is not a one-time project. CLO 7: pick a **framework** that improves efficiency; do not “transform” as a slogan.

**Hand off BUS643** for leading-change as a graduate *course*. **Hand off BUS640** for advanced OB. **Hand off BUS501** if the real question is firm-in-industry strategy. Stay here for “power, politics, and change are how people-management actually happens when the environment will not sit still.”

If the only way to hit the KPI is a CLO-5 ethics failure or a CLO-6 discrimination, **change the people-plan**, do not write a cleverer tactic.

## 2. Worked micro-patterns

**"What is managing people / how is it not OB or HR?"**
Check A in one paragraph. People are the most important function; productivity hinges on strategic goals. Individual attributes → group dynamic. Hook: theory then practice; name destructive vs positive. Overlays: BUS209 *teaches* three-level behavior; BUS301 *runs* the HR cycle; BUS208 *is* POLC; this course *spends* them as the manager’s job.

**"They’re not motivated / should we throw money at it?"**
Check C. Write performance ≈ motivation × ability × environment. Herzberg: money is often hygiene. Expectancy: is the outcome even valued, and do they believe effort will be seen? Equity: versus which referent. Then Check D if the real object is the pay system, and Check F if culture punishes the very behaviors you are paying for.

**"Should we hire for culture fit / how do we staff this team?"**
Checks B + D + F. Fit is real and ASA will make the culture righter (or more closed). CLO 6: diversity is a decision about who you hire and promote, not a poster. Selection screens ≠ MBTI. EEO is a named constraint — re-fetch, do not evade. Then Check E: you are hiring into a *team*, not a pile of résumés.

**"We need a stronger leader / should we flatten the team?"**
Check E then F. Leadership vs management. Match the situation (contingency), do not hire charisma and hope. Flattening is org design (Unit 8) — it changes power and culture, not just the org chart. Destructive: personalized nPow. Positive: socialized power + ethical climate.

**"This team is toxic / the reorg is failing."**
Checks E–F. Task vs relationship conflict. Groupthink / loafing. Culture artifacts vs promotions. Power map: who actually has to unfreeze. Resistance as information. If the prompt is a negotiation over a contract, **BUS403**. If it is a change-*course* (Kotter as the object), **BUS643**.

**"Draft this email / slide deck to the staff."**
Check D comms half. Audience / purpose / channel / tone / feedback. Then Check F: will the culture treat this message as an artifact that matches promotions, or as spin. **Hand off BUS210** if they need the writing course. Stay here for “this message is how you lead the people.”

**"Define OB at three levels / run the six BUS209 units."**
Wrong course. **BUS209**. Recap a named tool only if this prompt still needs it.

**"Job analysis / comp band / union clause / OSHA program."**
Wrong course. **BUS301** (law overlay **BUS205**).

**"What are the four functions of management?"**
Wrong course. **BUS208**.

**"FX / Eurozone people issues as an IB course."**
Wrong course. BUS613 / international HR is an HRM *chapter*, not this core.
