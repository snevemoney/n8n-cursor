# Hard-money Q&A checklists (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks whether fractional-reserve banking can live on Bitcoin, how a Bitcoin standard could arrive without blowing up credit, why mining consumes energy, whether Bitcoin can be killed, how Lightning/layers scale a hard base, how fiat distorts food, or how national reserve currencies produce BoP crises in developing countries — especially in an ECON105 / Bitcoin Standard / Ammous / "hard money" frame.

**Do not use when:** the job is taking or reconstructing a Saylor exam; defining value, the action axiom, or diamond-water (`austrian-intro-checklists` / ECON103); running socialist calculation, originary interest, fiduciary-media *theory*, or ABCT mechanics (`austrian-capital-cycle-checklists` / ECON104); framing "what is money" as an energy network or splitting crypto-asset vs crypto-currency (`what-is-money-framing` / ECON135); operational wallets/KYC (PRDV151); or a pure ECON102 IS-LM / ESG-TWh homework with no school overlay requested.

This is **one** skill. It is not "Unit 1 skill + Unit 2 skill…". ECON105's ten units are seven applied questions from ECO31: banking on a hard base → monetization path → mining as security → fiat side-effects (food, survival, developing-world money, layers).

If the user still needs subjective value / praxeology, **hand off to `austrian-intro-checklists`**. If they need interest-as-TP, money-vs-credit typology, or boom–bust mechanics, **hand off to `austrian-capital-cycle-checklists`**, then come back here to apply those tools to Bitcoin. If they asked "what *is* money?", **hand off to `what-is-money-framing`**.

## 0. Hygiene

- **Label school.** This is the **Austrian School applied to a Bitcoin standard**, as taught in Saylor ECON105 (Ammous *Bitcoin Standard* / ECO31 pedagogy, Mises–Rothbard banking overlay, Hayek on monetary nationalism). It is **not** Keynesian inflate-or-die, **not** "just raise the block size," **not** ESG energy accounting as the last word, **not** ECON103-only intro, **not** ECON135 anthropology-of-energy.
- **Claim vs data.** TWh/hash figures, nutrient lists, "BTC will shrink credit," and "difficulty makes energy cheaper" are **course/Ammous claims**. Re-fetch live mining or macro series if the user needs current numbers. Nutrition claims are not medical advice.
- **In-school splits to surface, not hide:** White/Selgin free banking vs Hoppe/Huerta de Soto 100% reserve; orderly monetization vs hyperinflation-before-liquidity; Lightning cheaper/faster vs weaker than on-chain finality.
- No investment advice. No "buy Bitcoin." No exam items. Do not dump copyrighted papers or *The Fiat Standard*.

## 1. Procedure — run four Q&A checklists

Run in order unless the user already constrained the frame. Skip a checklist only when the question is already inside it. Fiat-food (U6) and developing-world/1971 (U8–U9) are **satellites after D**, not separate skills.

### Checklist A — FRB on a hard base (Unit 1; LOs 1–2)

**Question this checklist answers:** what *is* fractional-reserve banking, and can/must it exist on Bitcoin?

ECON104 already distinguished money vs fiduciary media and said credit expansion ≠ saving. Here you **apply** that to a Bitcoin base. Do not restart the typology lecture.

| # | Test | What to write |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Define FRB** | Issuing demand claims (deposits, IOUs, wrapped tokens) against a reserve ratio **< 100%**. Full-reserve / warehouse = every demand claim is matched by the base asset. |
| A2 | **Is FRB *necessary* for growth?** | This school: **no**. Growth is saving → capital goods (ECON104 LO 4). Credit *markets* (lending of saved BTC) ≠ manufacturing unmatched demand deposits. |
| A3 | **Can FRB survive a free market?** | **Split, labeled:** White/Selgin — optional fiduciary media, option clauses, note discounts; Hoppe/Hülsmann/Block/Huerta de Soto — unmatched demand deposits are unsound and tend to need a backstop. Do not fake a Saylor consensus. |
| A4 | **On Bitcoin L1?** | L1 coins are bearer, auditable with a node, settled without a vault visit. **Covert** vault-style FRB is harder than under gold. That is not a proof that *nobody* will issue BTC-IOUs. |
| A5 | **On Bitcoin L2 / custody?** | Exchange balances, wrapped BTC, custodial apps **are** the FRB/warehouse choice. Market may price them at a discount to native coins (Finney-style). Central banks holding BTC as *reserve* is treated as **unlikely**: a non-printable reserve binds the issuer. |

**Worked micro-pattern — "Does a Bitcoin standard abolish banks?"**
A2+A5: banks as **custody and credit matching** remain (Unit 10). What the course challenges is treating unmatched demand deposits as *money indistinguishable from BTC*.

**Worked micro-pattern — "We need FRB or the economy can't grow."**
A2: that is the inflate-credit-as-capital claim ECON104 already rejected. Point at saving vs fiduciary media; do not rerun ABCT unless asked.

### Checklist B — Monetization path (Units 2–3; LO 3)

**Question this checklist answers:** if Bitcoin became global money, *how*, and does that blow up credit money?

| # | Test | What to write |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | **Monetization ≠ price** | BTC used as money (SoV + medium entering calculation) by a widening set of actors. Price is a symptom. Functions-of-money lecture → `what-is-money-framing`. |
| B2 | **Path A — orderly upgrade** | Hard SoV competes with *debt held as money* → less demand to issue new credit-as-SoV → **demonetization of debt**, lower indebtedness. Guest/ECO31 claim: continuous BTC growth **reduces credit creation**, so it is **unlikely** to *cause* a hyperinflationary collapse of credit money. |
| B3 | **Path B — disorderly** | National credit-moneys hyperinflate **before** BTC has liquidity/depth. That is a **risk to Bitcoin** (also Checklist D), not the bull case. |
| B4 | **BTC in a credit freeze** | L1 has no maturity mismatch and no issuer to halt. It does **not** print a Bagehot backstop. In this school that is the point; say the rival view (LOLR needed) out loud. |
| B5 | **Do not import ABCT as the answer** | If the user needs *why* credit booms bust, hand off to `austrian-capital-cycle-checklists`, then apply B2–B4: BTC is not the cycle's cause and not the LOLR. |

**Worked micro-pattern — "Bitcoin will pop the credit bubble and kill the dollar overnight."**
B2 vs B3: the taught central path is slower substitution that *shrinks* credit-as-SoV. Overnight fiat death with a thin BTC book is the scenario the course treats as dangerous.

**Worked micro-pattern — "Hard money causes depressions because prices fall."**
Do not start a new deflation essay from ECON102. One labeled reply: this school separates **productivity deflation** (benign) from **credit collapse deflation**; a Bitcoin standard is argued to be the first. Details of ABCT → ECON104.

### Checklist C — Mining as energy-security (Units 4–5; LO 4)

**Question this checklist answers:** why does Bitcoin burn energy, and is that waste?

This is **physical kWh bound to chain security**, not ECON135's metaphor that money *is* an energy network through time and space. If the user asked that metaphor, hand off.

| # | Test | What to write |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | **What the energy buys** | Unforgeable costliness. Cost to rewrite history ≈ energy already sunk + energy to outrun honest hash. **Energy consumption *is* the security budget** (LO 4). |
| C2 | **Difficulty adjustment** | Hash comes and goes; difficulty retargets so block time stays ~constant. High-cost miners are the residual; they exit first when hashprice falls. |
| C3 | **Who stays** | Mining is only reliably profitable at **very low** electricity cost. Search is global → **stranded / off-grid / flared / wet-season hydro** joules that other buyers did not want. |
| C4 | **ESG objection** | Label it. Reply in-school: (1) the burn is the product; (2) miners often buy otherwise-unsold power; (3) compare to the energy-and-violence cost of the incumbent monetary system. **Do not invent TWh.** Re-fetch if the user needs a current figure. |
| C5 | **Not a resource-depletion reboot** | Julian Simon / "resources aren't depleted" is ECON103 LO 5. One-line handoff if it comes up; this unit's novel claim is **security ← watts**, not geology. |

**Worked micro-pattern — "Bitcoin uses as much electricity as country X, therefore ban it."**
C1+C3+C4: name the security function, the difficulty residual, the stranded-power search. Then say you will not launder a live TWh comparison without a source dated now.

**Worked micro-pattern — "Just switch Bitcoin to Proof of Stake to save energy."**
C1: in this course, dropping the energy cost **is** dropping the unforgeable-costliness argument. PoS is a different security/politics bundle (issuer-like staking, nothing-at-stake). Do not pretend ECON105 is a protocol-design class; state the economic claim and stop.

### Checklist D — Standard architecture: survival + layers (Units 7, 10; LOs 6, 8)

**Question this checklist answers:** what would actually kill Bitcoin, and how can a hard, scarce chain serve more users than it can settle on-chain?

| # | Test | What to write |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | **Weak kill-shots** | Software bugs, government bans, "fees won't fund security." Taught reply: these do **not** remove the **incentive to use** BTC. Bugs get patched if holders still want it; bans relocate demand; fees can reprice block space. |
| D2 | **Real kill-shots (demand)** | (1) Governments **improve** money/finance enough that the exit is not demanded. (2) National currencies **hyperinflate before** BTC has liquidity — flight has nowhere deep to land (twin of B3). |
| D3 | **Why layers** | Demand for hard money is argued to **exceed on-chain capacity**. Second layers are **necessary**, not a betrayal of "be your own bank." |
| D4 | **Lightning, economically** | Multisig channels; local updates; on-chain open/close. Faster/cheaper. **Not as final as L1.** Inbound liquidity, hubs, watchtowers = operational risks to *name*, not to spec as if from a Saylor quiz. |
| D5 | **Custody and credit remain** | Market services. L2 can be full-reserve or fractional (Checklist A returns). Gold's failure mode: the public held bank paper, gold centralized, then gold was frozen. Bitcoin's hoped-for difference: self-custody + LN. **Claim, not a guarantee.** |
| D6 | **Credit ≠ FRB** | Time-preference matching of saved BTC can exist without unmatched demand deposits. Do not collapse U10 "credit allocation" into U1 "fiduciary media." |

**Worked micro-pattern — "A ban or a bug will kill Bitcoin."**
D1: implementation shocks vs incentive. Then D2: ask whether anyone still *wants* a non-political hard asset.

**Worked micro-pattern — "If it can't do Visa-volume on-chain it failed as money."**
D3–D5: this course's scaling thesis is **layered**, same family as gold+notes but trying not to repeat gold's custody centralization. ECON135's "L1 is low-frequency settlement" is compatible; do not restart the functions table.

### Satellite — Fiat food (Unit 6; LO 5)

Run when the question is diet, soil, "why do Bitcoiners talk about meat/seed oils," or money→food quality.

1. **Channel 1 — time preference:** suppressed/centrally planned rates + high-TP money → maximize this year's yield, deplete soil, prefer industrial throughput over stewardship.
2. **Channel 2 — inflation-fighting calories:** policy ("get big or get out," cheap grain/sugar/oil) to hide price rises with cheap calories at the cost of nutrient density/safety.
3. **Fiat-foods list (Ammous public talks, labeled):** refined flour, sugar, industrial seed oils, HFCS, soy protein isolates, low-fat products, breakfast cereals. Not an RCT. Not medical advice.
4. **"Mises met Weston Price"** = monetary theory × traditional-foods nutrition. Do not dump Price.

If the user only needed TP as interest theory, that was ECON104 Checklist B — do not restart it; apply it to farms.

### Satellite — Developing world and 1971 (Units 8–9; LO 7)

Run when the question is IMF, BoP crises, "Bitcoin for the poor," remittances, or Bretton Woods.

1. **Name the history LO 7 wants:** post-WWII Bretton Woods gold-exchange / dollar center → **15 Aug 1971** end of official gold convertibility (Nixon shock). This school treats 1971 as the fiat standard becoming fully operational, not as trivia.
2. **Mechanism:** national currencies used as **global reserves** → reserve issuer supplies the world with its liabilities (structural deficit / imported policy); everyone else lives inside that political money. Hayek *Monetary Nationalism*: international money as a technical problem, not a patriotism problem.
3. **Developing-world symptom:** recurring BoP crises, hyperinflations, sovereign lending without a hard funding constraint (ECO31: Easterly / Perkins texture — **cite as course pile**, not as hive geopolitics).
4. **Bitcoin's claimed job:** a **technical, non-political** global unit. **Not primarily** cheaper Western Union. Remittances are a side benefit. Legal overlays on *exchanges* ≠ issuance control of the unit — say both.

**Worked micro-pattern — "Bitcoin helps the developing world because fees beat MoneyGram."**
Satellite step 4: that is the small-payments story the course **downgrades**. Lead with reserve architecture.
