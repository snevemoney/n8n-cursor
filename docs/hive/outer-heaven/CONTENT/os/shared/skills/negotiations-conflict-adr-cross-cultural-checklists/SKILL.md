# Negotiations and conflict: phases, styles, ADR, culture (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks how to prepare or run a negotiation, whether the pie is fixed or expandable, which business venue (sales, supplier, labor, M&A, agent, multiparty) is in play, whether workplace conflict is functional or dysfunctional, which conflict-handling style or ADR rung to use, or how culture/politics/law changes the conversation.

**Do not use when:** the job is reconstructing or taking a Saylor (or any) exam, dumping Fisher & Ury *Getting to Yes* / Lewicki *Essentials of Negotiation* / TECEP NEG-401, running **BUS402** process groups on a temporary unique project, **BUS401** leadership-vs-management theory overlay, **BUS209** three-level OB specialty, **BUS641** graduate strategic negotiations, **BUS301** HR system design, or writing a manipulation / hardball script.

This is **one** skill. It is not "Unit 1 skill + Unit 2 skill…". BUS403's five units are one argument: **negotiation is a conversation toward agreement; conflict is diagnosed then climbed through styles and an ADR ladder; culture is a constraint on that conversation, not a poster.**

## 1. Procedure — answer a negotiations-and-conflict question in six checks

Run in order. Skip a check only if the user already constrained the frame.

### Check A — Frame the conversation (always)

Write this in one sentence before you name a tactic:

1. **Who** is at the table (and who is missing)?
2. **What** is the issue, and is the **relationship** also at stake?
3. **What happens if there is no deal** (each side's BATNA)?

**Negotiation** (live glossary): a productive conversation that addresses each other's points of view when two or more parties disagree and at least one party feels strongly enough about the **importance of the outcome or their relationship**.

**Five phases** (live CLO 2 / Course Map 1b; CC spine — **not** glossary entries):

| Phase | Job | You are not done until |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Investigation | Gather information; brutally honest goals, concessions, walk-aways | You can say what you want, what you will trade, and what you will not |
| 2. BATNA | Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement (Fisher & Ury, named tool) | You can accept/reject an offer against a real fallback, not a mood |
| 3. Presentation | Assemble facts that support the position (value created, constraints) | The other party can see *why*, not just *how much* |
| 4. Bargaining | Discuss goals; **concessions** (give X to get Y) — not a sign of weakness | A package is on the table; questions uncovered the real constraint |
| 5. Closure | Agree, or walk because the final offer is worse than BATNA | Terms are explicit — or you learned *why* it died (investigative close) |

**BATNA** (glossary): brainstormed alternatives used if the negotiation does not lead to a favorable outcome. Improve it. **Do not volunteer it** if it is weak (CC warning). The party with the better BATNA has the better position.

**ZOPA** (glossary: Zone of Possible Agreement): acceptable bargaining range guided by market parameters and BATNA. No ZOPA → do not force a deal; improve BATNA or walk.

**SMART goals** (glossary; Unit 1.3): specific, measurable, achievable, realistic, time-oriented. Vague "I want more" is not a strategy.

**Emotional intelligence** (glossary; Unit 1.6/1i): sense, understand, and apply emotions as energy, information, connection, and influence. A positive-sum *attitude* is a live resource title — it is not a law that cheerfulness expands the pie.

**Game theory warning** (Unit 1.5–1.6): zero-sum games and "what is a zero-sum game, and how do I play?" sit next to integrative / positive-sum readings. Use game theory to *see the trap* (Prisoner's Dilemma-class: each side outplays the other into the worst joint outcome — M&A lesson on a possibly-stale page). Do not treat every deal as a payoff matrix.

**Group decision** (Unit 1.2 / 1d): problem-solving in groups is how some negotiations happen. **Groupthink** (glossary): the group stops generating solutions because members will not risk acceptance. If the "team" already agrees too fast, you do not have a strategy.

If asked "what should I do first?," name investigation + BATNA + ZOPA before tactics.

### Check B — Pick the pie: distributive vs integrative (when "win," "split," or relationship is in play)

Live CLO 2 / Course Map 1h. Glossary definitions — use these words.

| | Distributive (win-lose / lose-lose) | Integrative (win-win attempt) |
|---|---|---|
| When | The **issue** matters more than preserving or establishing the relationship | Both the issue **and** a long-standing relationship matter |
| Picture | Fixed pie; each tries to get more of a divided pie | Expand the pie; concessions, trade-offs, creative ideas |
| Failure | Ego, first-offer grab, zero-sum game-theory cosplay | Fake "win-win" that is actually a hidden distributive grab |
| Move | Claim value inside the ZOPA; know reservation price | Create value first (different preferences, logrolling), then claim |

CC spine mistakes to refuse (summarize, do not dump the toolbox): accepting the first offer; letting ego/pride drive (PointCast-class illustration); unrealistic expectations; getting overly emotional; letting a past failed negotiation poison this one. Silence after an offer is a tool; filling silence is amateur hour.

**Cooperation vs competition** (CLO 2): competition tries to take limited rewards and may reduce the other's success. Cooperation trusts that joint behavior can enlarge the outcome. Dual-concern logic (CC social-psych neighbor): high self-concern *and* high other-concern = **problem-solver** — often the best negotiator because they search past the dilemma. High self / low other = contend. Low self / high other = yield. Low/low = inactive.

If asked "which theory is right?," answer with **whether the relationship is a goal**. Then name the other family so you have not stacked them.

### Check C — Name the business venue (when it is not a generic two-person table)

Unit 2. Do not skip this: the same five phases run, but the *object* and the *power* change.

| Venue | Question this school asks | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| **Sales** | Perceived value, concessions, price (glossary: sales negotiation). Closing starts at the beginning; persuasion principles. | Discounting as the only lever |
| **Suppliers** | "Essence of negotiation" — cost, time, quality, continuity | Treating every PO as a one-shot distributive fight |
| **Labor / collective bargaining** | Employees (often union) negotiate shared employment conditions with employers (glossary). US labor-negotiation *history* is CLO 2c. | Turning this into BUS301 HR specialty or a union-busting script |
| **M&A** | Merger = two firms proceed as one new company. Acquisition = stronger firm takes operational control; weaker keeps operating under the stronger name (glossary). Pride and "social issues" kill financially pretty deals (CC PointCast-class illustration). | Valuation theater with no BATNA |
| **Principal vs agent** | Principal engages; agent owes a **fiduciary** duty to act strictly in the principal's best interest (glossary). Implied authority, independent contractor vs employee, torts in scope of employment — live case titles. | Assuming the agent at the table *is* the principal |
| **Multiparty** | More parties ⇒ more BATNAs, more coalitions, more delay. Name +/− (CLO 2f). | Treating a room of six as two |
| **Timing and power** | Course Map 2g: timing + **"six power tactics."** **Login gap:** the live "Sources of Power" list is not in the glossary. Do not fake Cialdini-six or French-and-Raven-six as harvested. Name *that power and time change the ZOPA*, then ask which base the user actually has (title, expertise, relationship, resources). | Announcing "I have no power" instead of naming timing |

If asked "how do I negotiate this deal?," name the venue in one line, then return to Check A.

### Check D — Diagnose workplace conflict (when the problem is inside the firm)

Unit 3 / CLO 3. **Do not skip diagnosis.** Style (Check E) without diagnosis is theater.

**Functional vs dysfunctional** (glossary + live intro):

| | Functional | Dysfunctional |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Positive force for change; healthy, respectful debate of ideas; free voice as a form of negotiation | Misunderstandings and personal clashes; caustic environment; lower morale and productivity |
| Use | Innovation, surfacing bad assumptions, participation | Stop it; it will not "work itself out" |
| Inverted-U (CC) | Moderate *task* conflict can help performance | Relationship/personal conflict is not a dose to increase |

**Conflict management** (glossary) = contain or manage, not necessarily erase. **Conflict resolution** (glossary) = improve or fully settle so parties are satisfied or hit win-win. Pick which job you are being asked to do.

**Causes this course names** (Course Map 3.2–3.10; CC six-pack, summarized):

1. **Organizational structure** — centralization / formalization (glossary); matrix dual-boss; communication and transparency fail (CLO 3b).
2. **Scarcity of resources** — money, time, tools; economics as named Unit 3.4 reading (CLO 3c).
3. **Task interdependence** (glossary) — your goal needs their task; teamwork models (CLO 3e–3f).
4. **Leadership commitment** — whether bosses will actually resolve (CLO 3d).
5. **Active listening, role clarity, resistance to change** (CLO 3g).
6. **Personality types** (CLO 3h) — differences are normal; do not moralize style.
7. **Stereotypes, prejudice, cultural bias** (CLO 3i–3j) — stereotyping = biased thoughts from a category; prejudice = biased *emotional* attitudes from group membership (glossary). Gender, sexual harassment, ageism, culturally-based bias are **named live subsections**. Inclusivity is a CLO, not a slogan. Do not essentialize; do not skip harassment into "personality clash."

**Five benefits of functional disagreement** (CLO 3a; live "Five Good Things about Conflict"): encode as *the course wants a list* — broader ideas, surfaced assumptions, participation/creativity, clarified views, learning — without faking the live article's exact five until login.

If asked "how do we fix this team?," name functional vs dysfunctional, then the cause, *then* a style. Do not start with a ropes course (that is BUS401/teams). Do not start with a Gantt chart (that is BUS402).

### Check E — Resolve: styles, then the ADR ladder (when "what do we do" is the question)

Unit 4 / CLO 4.

**Thomas-Kilmann five styles** (live 4.1; dual-concern grid: assertiveness × cooperation). Glossary does **not** name the five; Course Map does. Use the live labels:

| Style | Assert / cooperate | Use when | Burns when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Competing** | High / low | Safety, ethics line, true zero-sum, time-critical | Every Tuesday; wrecks the relationship BUS403 is trying to keep |
| **Collaborating** | High / high | Issue *and* relationship matter; integrative (Check B) | You fake it because "win-win" sounds nice |
| **Compromising** | Mid / mid | Split the difference; time-boxed; both can give | It becomes the only move and nobody's goal is met |
| **Avoiding** | Low / low | Trivial, cooling-off, no standing | Habit; important issues rot |
| **Accommodating** | Low / high | Other's issue >> yours; relationship repair | You disappear; resentment |

No best style. Match the situation. CC claim: managers often *force*; subordinates avoid/accommodate/compromise — expect the mismatch.

**ADR ladder** (live CLO 4; glossary for mediation/arbitration). Climb only as far as you must. Informal first.

| Rung | Who decides | Character | Use when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Negotiation** | The parties | Informal, consensual; this course's default | Parties can still talk; BATNAs exist |
| **Mediation** | Parties, with a facilitator | Neutral third party **does not impose**; understanding, common ground, action plan (glossary) | Talk stalled; relationship still worth saving |
| **Arbitration** | Arbitrator | Formal ADR; award. **Binding** (glossary) = almost no court appeal | Need a decision without a trial; labor grievance-class |
| **Litigation** | Judge/jury | Legal standing required (glossary: only those whose interests are directly affected). Class actions, jury process, reputational/financial **risk analysis** (glossary) are named live LOs | Rights, precedent, the other rungs failed — last, not first |

**Mandatory arbitration** (CLO 4d) has live +/− (employer-side "Epic Day" reading). Encode as a *trade*: speed/cost vs waived court access. Do not write a "force employees into arb" how-to.

**Unions / collective bargaining** (CLO 4c) reappear as a conflict-resolution *channel*, not as BUS301. Progressive discipline (glossary; CLO 4l–4m): verbal counseling → written warnings → suspension without pay → termination, after a reasonable chance to correct. Performance conversation and "hard truth to underperformers" are live titles. Encode as **fairness + documentation**, not as a firing script.

**Common goal** (CLO 4j–4k): understand each party's perspective; work toward a shared goal; name barriers; make a plan. "Project Completion" as a Unit 4 resource is **closing the conflict plan**, not BUS402 process-group closing.

If asked "should we sue?," name standing, risk analysis, and which ADR rung has not been tried. Litigation is the last comparison, not the identity of this course.

### Check F — Culture as constraint (when the table is not the same culture)

Unit 5 / CLO 5. Culture is not vibe and not BUS401 "build a culture given goals."

**Culture** (glossary): a group/community/society's shared traditions, beliefs, values, mindsets, customs, history, folklore, or practices that form how they think, communicate, and work.

**Cultural awareness** (glossary): understand another culture's values and perspectives. Operating principle: **no culture is right, wrong, better, or worse.**

**Cultural competence** (glossary): manage knowledge, awareness, and sensitivity so the *organization* works in cross-cultural settings.

**Cultural Intelligence** (glossary): adapt **behaviorally, motivationally, and metacognitively**.

**Nonverbal** (glossary): gestures, posture, societal behaviors that can carry greater truths than the spoken word.

**Saving face** (glossary): deference toward supervisors and elder co-workers; avoid public questioning. Live China readings ("Importance of Face," "Begin Slowly with Praise") are illustrations, not a China-manual.

**Named dimension tools** (CLO 5e–5f). Glossary names Trompenaars without listing seven; Hofstede six are a CLO, not glossary entries. Use the **standard lists this school is pointing at**; confirm wording on login.

Hofstede six (named tool): power distance · individualism/collectivism · masculinity/femininity (achievement vs nurture) · uncertainty avoidance · long-term vs short-term orientation · indulgence vs restraint.

Trompenaars seven (named tool): universalism vs particularism · individualism vs communitarianism · specific vs diffuse · neutral vs affective · achievement vs ascription · sequential vs synchronic time · internal vs external control.

Do not dump instrument manuals. Do not stereotype a nationality from a dimension score. Use dimensions to **ask better questions** (who decides, how direct, how much relationship before business, what "yes" means).

**Political and legal** (CLO 5h–5i): international talks sit inside trade law, governments, and the **WTO** (glossary: forum to negotiate and adjudicate trade agreements and disputes for goods, services, IP). Live titles: FTAs/GATT-class history, "Effect of Politics on Global Negotiations," "Be Proactive with Your Government Relationships." Encode as *the table is not only the two firms*.

Live regional cases (China face; US–Arab world; Mexico; Latino vs Hispanic) are **illustrations**. Re-fetch current practice; do not freeze 2008-era travel advice as law.

If asked "how do we negotiate with [country]?," refuse the stereotype. Name (1) culture definition, (2) which dimension might move the process (relationship-first vs deal-first; face; time), (3) nonverbal, (4) political-legal/WTO overlay, (5) BATNA still exists.

## 2. Worked shape (not an exam item)

Prompt shape: "A supplier missed a delivery, the plant manager wants to sue, and the supplier's team is in another country and stopped answering email."

Run the checks, short:

- A: two firms + missing decision-makers. Issue = delivery/cost; relationship = ongoing supply. Each BATNA (other supplier vs lost customer). No ZOPA until someone talks.
- B: if you will buy from them next year, this is integrative with a distributive *claim* on the missed-cost number. Zero-sum "destroy them" is the game-theory trap.
- C: supplier venue + multiparty (plant, procurement, their sales, maybe a government inspector) + principal-agent (is the person who went silent even authorized?).
- D: inside your firm this may already be dysfunctional (blame, stereotypes about "how they work"). Functional version: debate process redesign.
- E: negotiation first (reopen the channel). Mediation if talk is toxic. Arbitration if the contract says so. Litigation last — standing, risk analysis, relationship death. Competing style on safety/stop-ship; collaborating on a recovery plan.
- F: silence may be face, hierarchy, or simply a dead agent. Cultural awareness ≠ excuse for missed delivery. Ask how decisions get made *there*; do not send a public accusation that makes saving-face impossible.
