# Manager POLC checklists (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks what management is, how leadership differs from management, how to plan/organize/lead/control a unit, how to choose or diagnose an org structure, or how to motivate / empower people in the standard undergrad intro-management school.

**Do not use when:** the job is reconstructing or taking a Saylor (or any) exam, dumping Daft or Griffin, running BUS209 organizational-behavior specialty models, treating the prompt as BUS401 strategic-leadership capstone, or writing a plant SOP / PMP schedule.

This is **one** skill. It is not "Unit 1 skill + Unit 2 skill…". BUS208's nine units are one argument: management = **POLC done with and through people**; leadership is the influence half of Leading; structure is Organizing made visible; motivation is how Leading actually moves effort.

## 0. Hygiene

- Label **school**. This is the **standard undergraduate principles-of-management intro** (Saylor BUS208; Fayol P-O-L-C; Mintzberg roles; Follett "through others." OpenStax and Saylor *Principles of Management* v1.1 teach the same spine). It is not the only management theory (critical management, practice theory, TOC, complex adaptive systems). Say so if the user is not inside this course.
- Label **claim vs data**. Hawthorne interpretations, "most admired" lists, 2011 "middle managers are dying because of email," and case-sidebar figures are **course-era illustrations**. Re-fetch if the user needs current numbers.
- Management ≠ the manager's job title ≠ leadership ≠ entrepreneurship ≠ strategy. This school uses all four; it does not treat them as synonyms.
- No exam items. No all-rights-reserved chapter dumps. CC OER: summarize.

## 1. Procedure — answer a management question in four checks

Run in order. Skip a check only if the user already constrained the frame.

### Check A — POLC (always)

**Management** is the organization and coordination of work, through other people, to produce a desired result. Follett's line in this school: the art of getting things done through the efforts of other people. A **manager** marshals people, money, facilities, and equipment toward marketplace (or mission) outcomes.

The teaching model of the job is **P-O-L-C**. Real Tuesdays are fragmented (Mintzberg/Kotter: short contacts, interruptions, talk *is* the work). Still run POLC as the checklist — it classifies the work even when the day does not arrive in four neat blocks.

| Function | Ask | Manager moves |
|---|---|---|
| **Planning** | Where are we going, and how? | Scan the environment; forecast; set objectives; name alternatives; choose; implement; evaluate. Horizons: **strategic** (3+ yrs, whole org, top) / **tactical** (1–3 yrs, middle, means) / **operational** (<1 yr, steps). Mission/vision/values feed this. Strategy = integrated, external concept of *how* we hit objectives — including what we **stop** doing. |
| **Organizing** | Who does what, with which resources? | Draw the structure (Check C). Design jobs (specialization vs enrichment/empowerment/teamwork). Departmentalize. Allocate people. |
| **Leading** | Why would they actually move? | Influence (Check B). Motivation (Check D). Communication. Team design. Conflict. You cannot skip this and still call it management. |
| **Controlling** | Are we on standard? | (1) set performance standards, (2) compare actuals, (3) correct. Standards may be money, units, quality, or service. Budgets and audits are tools. Controlling is **not** manipulating personalities. No plan → no honest standard → control is theater. |

**Mintzberg roles** (use when "what do they *actually* do all day?" is the question):

| Family | Roles |
|---|---|
| Interpersonal | figurehead, **leader**, liaison |
| Informational | monitor, disseminator, spokesperson |
| Decisional | entrepreneur, disturbance handler, resource allocator, negotiator |

Supervisors lean disturbance-handler / negotiator / short horizon; executives lean figurehead / spokesperson / whole-firm bets. The **leader** role shows up at every level.

**Who is the manager?** Top (steward of mission/strategy) · functional (one discipline) · supervisory/team · line (touches the offer) vs staff (support) · project · general (a P&L unit). Contemporary picture: upside-down pyramid — executives exist to support the people who serve customers (**empowerment** = authorize thinking, acting, and local decision).

**Performance hygiene:** triple bottom line (economic / social / environmental) is in-scope, not extra credit. Individual performance = in-role + OCBs. A group is not a team until there is a small set of people, complementary skills, a shared purpose, and mutual accountability.

If asked "what should the manager *do*?," name the POLC loop for *this* problem before naming a theory.

### Check B — Leadership vs management (when influence, vision, "are they a leader?," or followership is in play)

Do not collapse the words.

| | Management | Leadership |
|---|---|---|
| Job | Get work done through others via **systems**: plans, structure, standards, resources | Mobilize people to **want** to struggle toward a goal via **influence** (informal as well as formal) |
| Typical tools | POLC, budgets, org charts, job design, control loops | Vision, values, example, meaning-making, coaching, conflict |
| Relationship | Subordinates (Barnard) | Followers (Barnard) |
| Object | Things *and* people, but the stereotype is "things/systems" (Hopper) | People |
| Failure mode | Overmanaged, underled: the machine is tidy and nobody is going anywhere (Bennis) | Charisma without systems: people are inspired and the work is a mess |

This school wants **both**. Good management requires leadership; leadership without organizing/controlling is a pep talk.

When diagnosing a person or a role, ask:

1. Do they have **position power** (they can hire, rate, budget)? That is management authority. It is not automatic leadership.
2. Do people **choose** to follow when the org chart is silent? That is leadership.
3. Which leadership **theory family** is the prompt actually in? Do not stack them.

   - **Trait** — who emerges; useful for selection; incomplete.
   - **Behavioral** — initiating structure vs consideration (Ohio State). What they *do*.
   - **Contingency / situational** — match style to the situation (Fiedler: style is relatively stable, so *match or move*; path-goal; Vroom). No one best style.
   - **Contemporary** — transactional (exchange, management-by-exception) vs **transformational** / visionary / charismatic; servant; authentic.
   - **Substitutes / neutralizers** — sometimes the job, the professional norms, or the team replace the hero. Do not prescribe "more leadership" if the design already substitutes for it.

Teams sit here as the vehicle: design for purpose and complementary skill; watch process loss, loafing, groupthink. Empowerment is a leadership *and* organizing choice.

### Check C — Org structure (when reporting lines, growth, "who decides?," silos, or change is in play)

**Structure** = the system that connects activities so people know their work, dependencies, and purpose. **Design** = choosing that system. **Change** = the shifts (planned or not). **OD** = the specialist practice of running change without wrecking people.

Always name both:

- **Formal** organization — chart, roles, reporting. How it *should* work.
- **Informal** organization — emergent network of who actually talks to whom. How it *does* work. You can map it; you cannot decree it. It can save or sink the formal design.

**Weber's five knobs** (bureaucracy as a design toolkit, not as an insult):

| Knob | Mechanistic (tall, stable, reliable) | Organic (flat, turbulent, innovative) |
|---|---|---|
| Specialization | High / narrow | Low / broad (specialist *and* generalist) |
| Command-and-control | Strong vertical | Distributed |
| Span of control | Narrow (more layers) | Broad (fewer layers, more reliance on people) |
| Centralization | Resources and decisions at the top | Pushed to where the work is |
| Formalization | SOPs, tight role definitions | Roles emerge; self-organize |

Match to the environment. A reliability business that "goes agile" without changing the work is costume. An innovation shop that installs a military SOP is costume the other way.

**Departmentalization picker:**

| Form | When | Watch-out |
|---|---|---|
| **Functional** | Deep expertise, stable mix | Silos; "not my job" |
| **Product / divisional** | Distinct lines of business | Duplicated staff; weak sharing |
| **Geographic** | Territory really is the customer | Inconsistent offer across regions |
| **Customer** | Segments need different processes | Complexity; internal competition |
| **Matrix** | Need both expertise *and* product/region at once | Two bosses; conflict unless the bosses are aligned |
| Team / network / virtual | Project work, partners, thin middle | Informal org does the coordinating — if it can |

Line vs staff: line creates the offer; staff (finance, HR, legal) supports. Do not let staff become a second operating company unless that is the strategy.

**Change skeleton (Lewin, teaching level):** unfreeze (need + safety) → change → refreeze (new standard, or it snaps back). Pair with Check A control: the new way needs a standard or it was only a speech. Technology and real-time communication have already cut a class of middle-manager "gatekeeper" jobs — that is a design pressure (fewer layers, faster information), not a reason to skip organizing.

### Check D — Motivation (when effort, performance, empowerment, pay, or "they just don't care" is in play)

Write the performance identity before naming Maslow:

**Performance ≈ ability × motivation × environment**, given **accurate role perceptions**.

**Motivation = direction × intensity.** Direction = the target they are trying to hit (may not be yours). Intensity = effort and persistence. Managers **own direction** (clarify the outcome, the process, quality vs speed vs cost). Intensity without direction is absenteeism-on-the-job or a hobby pursued on company time.

There is **no grand theory**. Content theories ask *what* is missing. Process theories ask *how* the person is thinking about the situation. Use both; pick the one the symptom points at.

**Content (need) — diagnose the missing fuel:**

| Theory | Use | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Maslow | Hierarchy as a reminder that unmet lower needs crowd out higher ones | Not a personality test; not always stepwise at work |
| ERG (Alderfer) | Existence / relatedness / growth; **frustration-regression** (blocked growth → extra relatedness or pay-seeking) | More flexible than Maslow; still not a law |
| Two-factor (Herzberg) | **Hygiene** (pay, conditions, policy, supervision) prevents dissatisfaction; **motivators** (achievement, the work itself, recognition, responsibility, growth) create satisfaction | Dumping money on a boring job is hygiene. Enrich the job if you want motivation. |
| Acquired needs (McClelland) | nAch / nAff / nPow — match task and climate | High nAch wants feedback and moderate risk, not a committee |

**Process — diagnose the story they are telling:**

| Theory | Ask |
|---|---|
| Equity / justice | Is the outcome/input ratio fair vs referents? Distributive *and* procedural *and* interactional. |
| Expectancy | Do they believe effort → performance (E→P), performance → outcome (P→O), and that the outcome is wanted (valence)? Break any link and effort dies. |
| Goal-setting | Specific + difficult + accepted + feedback. Vague "do your best" is not a goal. SMART is the course's spelling. |
| Reinforcement | What is actually being rewarded, ignored, or punished? You get the behavior you pay for, including the accidental kind. |

**Empowerment / conflict (Unit 5 overlay):** empowerment is organizing (Check C) in service of motivation — authority to think and act locally. Conflict is not a failure by default (Follett). Dual-concern grid at school-common level: compete / collaborate / compromise / avoid / accommodate — pick from stakes × relationship, then confirm Saylor's live labels if the user is inside the course.

If ability is missing, train or redesign. If the environment (tools, staffing, contradictory goals) is the constraint, POLC the environment — do not "motivate" a broken printer.

## 2. How to answer

- Start from Check A unless the user already named POLC. A motivation speech with no plan, structure, or standard is not an answer in this school.
- Use the table the check provides. Do not recite all four checks if only one is in play.
- If manufacturing vs service, project vs routine, or global vs local is mixed, split the transformation: POLC still runs; the *contents* of the plan and the structure change.
- Neighbor courses: **BUS209** is the OB specialty (go deeper on attitudes, perception, group process). **BUS300** is the operations transformation inside Organizing/Controlling. **BUS206** is the information system inside those processes. **BUS401** is strategic leadership as a capstone — do not import its depth unless asked.
- Stop at manager level. No exam keys, no "what will be on the Saylor final," no Daft/Griffin dump.

## 3. Worked micro-patterns

**"What is management / what do managers do?"**
Check A in one paragraph. Follett definition. Name POLC. Add Mintzberg only if they asked about the actual day. Add manager types if they asked "am I a manager?"

**"Leader or manager?"**
Check B table. Then: which theory family? If they have the title but no followers, they are managing without leading. If they have followers but no standards, they are leading without managing. This school wants the pair.

**"How should we organize?"**
Check C. Formal vs informal first. Then mechanistic vs organic against the environment. Then one departmentalization form, with its failure mode. Matrix only when they truly need two axes and can align the two bosses.

**"Why won't they work?"**
Check D identity first (ability / role perception / environment / motivation). Then direction vs intensity. Then one content theory (what's missing) and one process theory (expectancy or equity, usually). Do not start with Maslow's pyramid as a poster.

**"We need a plan."**
Check A planning column: scan → forecast → objectives → alternatives → choose → implement → evaluate. Name the horizon (strategic / tactical / operational). Tie control standards to the objectives or the plan is a wish.

**"Culture / ethics / diversity problem."**
Those are constraints on Leading and HR in this school, not a separate religion. Culture must fit the environment (Check C). Ethics is individual + organizational (values vs compliance) + CSR. Unmanaged diversity has costs; "we hired diversely" is not the Leading step.
