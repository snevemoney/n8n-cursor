# Western political thought: polis, then modern state, then critics (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks how to **place a canonical political-theory claim** in the Saylor POLSC201 school — Plato vs Aristotle on the person/ethics/society/citizenship/governance; Machiavelli/Hobbes/Locke/Rousseau on justice/freedom/equality/citizenship/sovereignty; versions of the state of nature; Constitution as influence; Marx on liberal democracy/capitalism; Mill on utilitarianism and the state.

**Do not use when:** POLSC101 discipline survey; PHIL103 moral/political philosophy as justice-as-a-course; PHIL304 existence; HIST103 dates; ENGL; PSYCH; exam reconstruction.

This is **one** skill. Three live units, one argument: **most students already have a "Hobbes then Locke" cartoon; this course's pass is polis first, then modern state thought with four named early-moderns, then liberal democracy's critics.**

## 0. Hygiene

- Label **school**. Undergraduate **western political thought** (Saylor POLSC201). **120 h / 12 CEU.** LMS **47** (`/course/polsc201`). ACE **N**. Cert **70% / 14-day / max 3**. Syllabus **2025-10-03 15:13**. No guest-visible textbook.
- Three **live** units: The Polis; Modern Political Thought; Liberal Democracy and Its Critics.
- Seven **live** CLOs (Plato/Aristotle through Mill).
- Not POLSC101 (six-unit intro). Not PHIL103 (Rawls/Kant/markets as *that* course).

## 1. Procedure — six checks

### Check A — Identity

| Prompt smells like | Hand off |
|---|---|
| Power/participation/IR intro | **POLSC101** |
| Rawls/Kant/virtue as PHIL103 | **PHIL103** |
| Sartre/Camus | **PHIL304** |
| Take the final | **refuse** |
| Plato/Aristotle or Hobbes/Locke/Rousseau/Marx/Mill in this canon | **this skill** |

### Check B — Polis (Unit 1)

Plato vs Aristotle on **person, ethics, society, citizenship, governance**. Five axes. Not "Greeks believed."

### Check C — Modern (Unit 2)

Context for the **modern state**. Compare Machiavelli, Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau on **justice, freedom, equality, citizenship, sovereignty**. **State of nature has versions.**

### Check D — Constitution (CLO 5)

Hobbes/Locke/Rousseau **influences** on the US Constitution — a political-thought job, not a POLSC101 institutions walk.

### Check E — Critics (Unit 3)

**Marx:** critique of democracy and the modern liberal state; link to capitalism.
**Mill:** utilitarianism applied to society and the state.
Do not file them as POLSC101's four -isms trivia.

### Check F — Compare across units

If the prompt jumps from Aristotle to Marx, name **two seats** (Unit 1 vs Unit 3). Do not invent a medieval unit.

## 2. Typical traps

- Plato = Aristotle.
- One state of nature.
- Machiavelli as "evil," Hobbes as "nasty," Locke as "America" — slogans without the five concepts.
- Constitution as civics, not influence.
- Marx as POLSC101 communism type.
- Mill as PHIL102 utility calculus.
- Three units inflated to a 12-thinker dump.
- ACE invented. 120 h ignored.

## 3. Refuse

Exam reconstruction; inventing SAYA; sitting the final; ENGL/PSYCH harvest; writing under POLSC101 as if this were that survey.
