# What-is-money framing (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks what money is, how money works, how to classify an asset as money, how Bitcoin differs from gold/fiat/altcoins, or how to reason about inflation, SoV, MoE, and unit of account without collapsing them.

**Do not use when:** the job is operational Bitcoin (keys, exchanges, AML), a conventional M1/M2 money-multiplier problem only, or taking/reconstructing a Saylor exam.

This is **one** skill. It is not "Unit 1 skill + Unit 2 skill…". ECON135's five units are one argument: anthropology → energy networks → functions/resilience → sociopolitical transformation → immortality/security/applications.

## 0. Hygiene

- Label **school**. Standard textbook functions are shared. The energy-network / "engineered money" overlay is the Saylor–Breedlove (ECON135) school, Austrian-adjacent, Bitcoin-maximal in conclusion. Do not present it as the only monetary theory.
- Label **claim vs data**. Multipliers (1000× faster, 50,000× cheaper), inflation half-lives (7% → 10 y), and 2020–22 money-supply percentages are **course claims from ~2021–22**. Re-fetch live macro series if the user needs current numbers.
- No investment advice. No "buy Bitcoin." Analyze properties.
- Do not dump copyrighted book text or exam items.

## 1. Procedure — answer "what is money?" in five checks

Run in order. Skip a check only if the user already constrained the frame.

### Check A — Function (textbook, always)

Money is whatever reliably does **all three**:

1. **Medium of exchange** — generally accepted in trade (avoids barter coincidence).
2. **Unit of account** — prices and debts are denominated in it.
3. **Store of value** — holds purchasing power across time (never perfect; a *better* SoV loses less).

Optional fourth: **standard of deferred payment** (unit for contracts over time). Treat as UoA + legal overlay.

If an object does only one function, say so. Cigarettes in a POW camp are MoE locally. A house is a lumpy SoV, bad MoE. A stablecoin may be MoE/UoA inside a venue and a poor long-horizon SoV if the peg/issuer fails.

### Check B — Type (how the token is issued)

| Type | Rough test | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| **Commodity** | Has non-monetary use; costly to produce | Producers dilute holders ("miners are the enemy of owners") |
| **Fiat** | Token by political decree / tax receivable; cheap to issue | Debasement / leakage; political capture |
| **Credit / representative** | Claim on a reserve or issuer | Counterparty + duration risk ("over a long enough timeline, mortality is 100%") |
| **Engineered digital (ECON135)** | Scarce bearer digital good, issuance bound to physics (PoW) + consensus rules, no issuer | Protocol mutation, capture of hash/governance, legal overlay on *use* not on *issuance* |

ECON135's distinctive claim: Bitcoin is the first **engineered** monetary system — designed to thermodynamics rather than to a political preference. Gold is a *mechanical approximation* of that idea; fiat is energy with a leak.

### Check C — Energy network (ECON135 overlay)

Ask: **how lossy is this as a battery and as a wire?**

- Through **space**: cost, speed, seizure risk to move a large amount of value.
- Through **time**: dilution (new supply), confiscation, decay, counterparty death, protocol change.

Saylor's teaching model: compare copper grids (high bleed, short range), gold (better short-haul, bad century battery, physical centralization), real estate/equities (people monetize them when the currency is not a SoV), Bitcoin (claimed "superconducting" encrypted energy — cheap/fast to move, hard-capped, no issuer).

Inflation in this frame is **leakage**, not a consumer-basket inconvenience. A 7%/yr leak halves the battery in ~10 years (rule of 72). CPI is a *managed basket*; do not treat CPI as the leakage rate without saying so.

### Check D — Competitor / crypto split (LO 3)

Do not dump all "crypto" in one bucket. Segment:

| Bucket | Job | ECON135 example |
|---|---|---|
| **Crypto-asset** | Long-duration SoV; immortal sovereignty | Bitcoin (only one treated as market-proven) |
| **Crypto-currency** | Spendable unit that is *not* a taxable volatile asset | Stablecoins (Tether-class). BTC fails this test under US capital-gains treatment |
| **Crypto-application** | Functionality (DeFi, title, privacy, DEX) | Smart-contract platforms |
| **Digital network** (centralized) | Economy, performance, functionality, compliance | Apple Pay, Visa, Coinbase, banks |

Tradeoff to recite: crypto networks buy **trust, security, duration** and pay with **economy, performance, functionality, compliance**. High-frequency retail payments belong on digital networks; the L1 job is low-frequency settlement + high-frequency SoV (HODLing *is* the use).

Forks that only raise transaction speed are treated as **non-differentiating mutations** (still a million times slower than Visa, while weakening the SoV). Privacy-as-base-layer is treated as a regulatory bullseye, not as a second digital gold.

### Check E — Antifragility / singularity (LOs 4–5)

Ask whether hostility **strengthens** the system.

**Seven-layer grid** (apply to any protocol; Bitcoin is the worked example):

1. Energy — what physical power backs issuance/security?
2. Technology — what specialized capital (ASICs, etc.) is sunk?
3. Political — which jurisdictions have something to lose?
4. Financial — which balance sheets defend the asset?
5. Network — miners vs nodes vs holders as mutual vetoes?
6. Spatial — can an attacker concentrate in one place?
7. Temporal — how long would an attack take, and can the protocol's constants be changed?

Singularity test (course conclusion, not a law of nature): one apex-predator digital SoV; features that prove useful can be absorbed; changing block size/frequency after genesis is treated as changing the speed of light. **State this as the ECON135 claim.** Alternative monetary theories (Chartalist/MMT, quantity theory without energy metaphor, multi-coin SoV) exist — flag them if the user is not inside this course.

## 2. Worked micro-patterns

**"Is X money?"**
Run A→E. Output: functions it performs, type, lossiness in space/time, which crypto/digital bucket, which security layers it lacks.

**"Functions of money" homework**
Give the textbook triad first. Then add the ECON135 reweighting: for Bitcoin, SoV is the high-frequency function; MoE is low-frequency/deterrent (ability to exit); UoA is the long-run language of energy, while local fiat still prices goods.

**"Bitcoin vs Ethereum / vs gold / vs USD"**
- vs gold: same SoV *aspiration*; gold loses on speed, seizure, miner-vs-holder, physical centralization.
- vs USD: USD wins MoE/UoA/compliance now; loses SoV if leakage > productivity. Do not pretend BTC is legal tender.
- vs ETH/alts: different *job* (app platform vs asset). PoS vs PoW is a security-layer argument, not a slogan.

**"Why not pay payroll in Bitcoin?" (ep 8)**
Tax lot per transfer, accelerated cap-gains, accounting explosion, employees still need local-currency bills. Pay in the unit the tax code treats as currency; let the counterparty buy the SoV themselves. Ethos: self-responsibility, not paternal MoE.

**"Immortal sovereignty"**
Long-horizon preference projection (endowments, parks, bodies of knowledge) without trusting a state/foundation that can hijack the purse. Base layer = bearer energy; execution = people + digital networks + multisig rules. Rockefeller did this with gold + 501(c)(3) + parks; the course claims BTC collapses the cost.

## 3. Anthropology stack (when the prompt is historical)

Keep it short unless asked for the full series:

Fire → missiles → hydraulics → industrial force (harder/smarter/faster/stronger) → **money as the highest form of channeled energy** → Bitcoin as fire in cyberspace (security in the virtual domain).

War and disruption compress adoption. Absent that, monetary-regime change is generational.
