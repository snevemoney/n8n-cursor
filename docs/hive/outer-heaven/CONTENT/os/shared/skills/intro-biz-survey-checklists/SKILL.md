# Intro-to-business survey checklists (clustered)

**Use when:** a task asks what a business is, why anyone studies business, how economic indicators or cycles change a decision, which legal form to pick, what entrepreneurs and small firms do, how to aim a marketing mix at a researched segment, or how to read an income statement, balance sheet, or a few financial ratios in the standard undergrad intro-to-business school.

**Do not use when:** the job is reconstructing or taking a Saylor (or any) exam, dumping Gitman/Nickels/Pride, running **BUS208 POLC** manager-job checklists, **BUS209** three-level OB, **BUS210** audience/channel/tone, **BUS300** operations toolkits, **BUS401** leadership-vs-management overlay, BUS103's accounting cycle, BUS202 capital budgeting, BUS203's full marketing process, or BUS501 firm strategy.

This is **one** skill. It is not "Unit 1 skill + Unit 2 skill…". BUS101's five units are one argument: **a business creates, communicates, and captures value inside an economic, legal, and social context**; you read that context, pick a form, aim a mix, read the books, and manage at survey depth — then you **hand off** to the specialty courses.

## 0. Hygiene

- Label **school**. This is the **standard undergraduate introduction-to-business survey** (Saylor BUS101; OpenStax *Introduction to Business*, Gitman / McDaniel et al., CC BY 4.0). Shared with almost every undergrad BUS 101. It is not the only way to introduce the firm (critical management, political economy, firm-as-nexus-of-contracts). Say so if the user is not inside this course.
- Label **claim vs data**. 2008 AIG/GM/Lehman cases, OpenStax 2018 firm-count table (~72% sole props / 18% corporations / corporations ~81% of revenues), Team Rubicon 2016 volunteer stats, Truman's recession/depression line, and archive "68 hours" are **course-era or stale**. Re-fetch if the user needs current numbers.
- Intro-to-business ≠ principles of management ≠ OB ≠ bizcomm ≠ operations ≠ strategy. BUS101 *names* POLC, teams, culture, and communication. It does not own them.
- No exam items. No all-rights-reserved chapter dumps. CC OER: summarize.

## 1. Procedure — answer an intro-to-business question in five checks

Run in order. Skip a check only if the user already constrained the frame.

### Check A — Context (always)

A **business** is an organization that creates goods, services, or ideas to satisfy needs and wants. For-profits take risk for a return. Not-for-profits still run the same functions (people, money, marketing, operations) against a **mission**. Both live in an environment that changes.

**Why study business (CLO 8):** to get a working vocabulary, to make better decisions as an employee/owner/citizen, to map a career, and to see that other countries' business models often reflect their most salient characteristics. It is not "to become Buffett/Jobs/Bezos." Those names are the course's hook, not the outcome.

Before recommending a product, a form, or a loan, name the **environment** that will punch the plan:

| Sector | What moves | Typical business question |
|---|---|---|
| Economic | Growth, unemployment, inflation, interest rates, cycles | Do we hire, hold cash, or raise prices? |
| Demographic / social | Age mix, values, household form, who works from where | Who is the customer *now*? |
| Political / legal | Tax, regulation, trade, liability rules | What are we even allowed to be? |
| Competitive | Rivals, substitutes, new entrants | Why would they pick us? |
| Technological | What is suddenly cheap or possible | Is our process about to be copied or obsolete? |
| Global | Other countries' models, FX, supply paths, culture | Are we local-pretending-to-be-global? |

**Indicators → decisions (CLO 1), not a macro problem set:**

- **Real GDP** (growth) ≠ **nominal** GDP. Per-capita GDP is the living-standard cousin.
- **Unemployment** vs full employment. "Your neighbor lost a job" is not a definition of recession; it is Truman's joke. Recession / depression / recovery / boom are **cycle phases**. Depression ≠ recession.
- **Inflation / CPI** = purchasing power. Price stability is a policy goal, not a promise.
- **Fiscal policy:** tax and spend; deficit (spend > revenue) vs surplus. **Monetary policy:** the Fed changes the **cost and availability of credit** (money supply / rates). 2008 (Lehman collapse; AIG and GM bailouts) is the course's worked crisis. Treat it as a **cycle + policy** case, not as current events.

**Market-structure vocabulary** (enough to classify, not to model): perfect competition, monopolistic competition, oligopoly, monopoly. Prices in the survey school are set by demand and supply unless a structure says otherwise.

If the prompt is actually "what is GDP / what is a Phillips curve / how do I run IS-LM," hand off to **ECON101/102**. BUS101 uses indicators as **context for a business decision**.

### Check B — Legal form + entrepreneurship

One of the first strategic decisions is **what legal thing this firm is**. Form drives liability, tax, control, capital, and how hard it is to die or grow. Form can **change** as the firm grows; picking sole-prop forever is a choice, not a law.

Run this table. Do not skip liability.

| Form | Control / profits | Liability | Tax (survey) | Capital / life | When it fits |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sole proprietorship** | Owner is the firm | **Unlimited** personal | Pass-through; once | Hard to raise; dies with owner | One person, low risk, wants out-easy |
| **General partnership** | Shared | Unlimited for generals | Pass-through | Better than sole; still limited | Complementary skills, trust, still small |
| **Limited partnership** | Generals run; limiteds fund | Limiteds capped at stake | Pass-through | Brings silent capital | Need money without giving ops away |
| **C corporation** | Shareholders; managers may differ | **Limited** to investment | Entity tax; dividends taxed again | Easiest large capital; perpetual | Scale, many owners, public path |
| **S corp / LLC** (specialized) | Varies by operating agreement | Limited | Pass-through *if* they qualify | Middle path | Want corporate shield without C-tax |

OpenStax 2018 counts (label the date): most *firms* are sole props (~72%); corporations are fewer (~18%) but take most revenue (~81%) and a majority of profits (~58%). "Most businesses" and "most of the economy" are different sentences.

**Questions that pick the row:**

1. Whose money is on the line if this fails? (liability)
2. Who decides, and can that person leave? (control / continuity)
3. How will we be taxed, and can we even get a loan or an investor? (tax + capital)
4. Will we need to **change form** when we hire, take on a partner, or raise a round?

**Entrepreneurship (survey, not BUS305):**

- Qualities the course cares about: initiative, tolerance for risk/ambiguity, ability to assemble resources, persistence. Not a personality cult.
- Small firms are a large **count** of businesses and a real share of jobs/innovation; they are not most of GDP. The **SBA** is the U.S. public-infrastructure pointer (advice, some guarantees) — not a funding entitlement.
- **Business plan** = the one-pager-to-document that forces form, customer, and money onto the same page.
- **Break-even** (planning tool): fixed cost / contribution per unit. "How many do we have to sell before this form even makes sense?" Not BUS105 CVP.

Hand off **BUS305** for a real plan, **BUS205 / BUS651** for law, **BUS103** if they start booking transactions.

### Check C — Marketing mix + segmentation (survey)

Marketing is **creating, communicating, delivering, and exchanging** offerings that have value for customers, clients, partners, and society. It is not advertising. Promotion is the visible piece; treating it as the whole is the standard wrong answer.

**Do not run BUS203's full process or BUS630 consumer-behavior labs.** Do run:

1. **Who is the target?** Segmentation + research exist so you do not market to everyone. A target market is the specific group (people or organizations) you aim at. Research (and later CRM, culture, social media listening) is how you learn them. No segment → the mix is a wish.

2. **Aim a 4P mix (CLO 4).** Saylor's outcome names four. OpenStax also teaches People (employees as the intangible half) and later Packaging — mention only if the user is in the book, not as if the CLO requires a 5th/6th P.

   | P | Question | Failure if ignored |
   |---|---|---|
   | **Product** | What is exchanged — good, service, idea? Tangible + intangible? | Feature-dumping a thing nobody is buying |
   | **Price** | What do they give up (money, time, attention)? Also called rent/fee/charge. | Pricing in a vacuum, or confusing price with cost |
   | **Place** | How does it get to them (path, inventory, location, online, atmospherics)? | A great product stuck in the wrong channel |
   | **Promotion** | How do we inform and influence — ads, sales promo, PR, selling, social, e-commerce? | "We did Facebook" as a strategy |

3. **Scan the same environment as Check A, customer-facing:** cultural/social, demographic, economic, technological, political-legal, competitive. If Unit 1 said "recession," Unit 3 does not launch a luxury line without saying so.

4. **Advantage type (survey, not Porter):** cost (erodes as rivals copy the cheap method), product differentiation, service differentiation, niche (often the only viable path for a small firm facing giants). Pick one primary. Do not stack all four.

5. **PLC (if stage is in play):** introduction / growth / maturity / decline — mix should match stage (awareness vs share vs harvest). Enough to avoid selling a decline product as if it were a launch.

E-commerce / e-business / social media are **channels and promo tools**, not a different theory of marketing.

Hand off **BUS203** for the full marketing process, **BUS502/BUS632** for strategy/digital depth.

### Check D — Books: IS, BS, ratios, credit (survey)

Every day the owner decides payroll, cash, and growth. Accounting is how raw **data** becomes **information**. This school asks you to **read** statements, not to run the cycle.

**Two statements the CLO actually names:**

| Statement | Time | Question it answers | Skeleton |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Income statement** | A **period** | Did we earn? | Revenues − expenses = net income (gross profit / operating income as stops along the way) |
| **Balance sheet** | A **snapshot** | What do we own and owe *right now*? | **Assets = liabilities + equity** |

The **statement of cash flows** is the third statement you will meet in any real packet of books. The Saylor CLO does not require you to build one. If cash and profit have diverged, *say so* and hand off rather than inventing a cash-flow worksheet.

**Ratios (CLO 6) — implications for future performance, with a baseline.** A lone ratio is a number, not an implication. Compare to last period, to a competitor, or to the industry.

| Family | Asks | Survey examples | Future-performance read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Liquidity | Can we pay soon? | Current; acid-test | Weak liquidity → credit, hiring, and inventory plans are fragile |
| Profitability | Are we earning on sales/assets/equity? | Net margin; ROA; ROE | Weak or falling → the mix or the cost structure is not working |
| Leverage | How much is borrowed? | Debt-to-equity; TIE | High leverage + a Unit 1 rate hike = the Fed just entered the P&L |
| Activity | Are assets working? | Inventory turnover; receivables days | Slow turns → cash is trapped; TVM makes that expensive |

**TVM:** a dollar today ≠ a dollar later. Refuse "just add year-3 cash to year-1 cash." Do not run NPV/IRR (that is BUS202).

**Banks / Fed / financing:**

- The Fed's tools change the **cost and availability of credit** firms can get. 2008 is again the worked case.
- Credit analysis (survey): will this borrower pay — capacity, collateral, character. You are not a loan officer.
- Financing options: internal (retained earnings / bootstrap) vs **debt** (obligation, keep control) vs **equity** (dilute, no scheduled repay). Pick from growth need + Check B form + Check D leverage.

Hand off **BUS103** (cycle, adjusting, full statements), **BUS105** (managerial), **BUS202** (finance).

### Check E — Management at survey depth (then stop)

For-profit and nonprofit hit goals by coordinating **human, financial, and informational** resources. The survey course **names** four functions — planning, organizing, leading, controlling — as vocabulary for "what managers do."

**Do not run POLC checklists here.** That is **BUS208**. If the user needs a manager's job diagnosed (planning horizons, structure, motivation, control loops), say you are leaving the survey school and switch skills.

What this check **may** still do:

- **Three skill families:** technical (the work itself), conceptual (see the whole), interpersonal (people). Weight shifts as the job gets more general; a first-line supervisor who cannot do the work and a CEO who cannot see the whole are both mis-skilled.
- **Ethics, CSR, mission, culture** are direction and constraints, not posters. A plan that "wins" by cheating stakeholders is not a valid intro-to-business answer. Depth on culture/conflict/motivation → **BUS209**. Depth on the message → **BUS210**. Depth on leading vs managing, change, power without title → **BUS401**.
- HRM, benefits, labor negotiation, teams: **named**. Do not staff a union campaign or a selection battery from this skill (**BUS301 / BUS209**).
- Operations as a function exists. Do not size capacity or set reorder points (**BUS300**).

CLO 7 is **skills necessary for a successful business**, not "become a theorist of leadership."

## 2. Worked micro-patterns

**"What is a business / why study this?"**
Check A in one paragraph. For-profit vs nonprofit. Environment table in one breath. CLO 8: vocabulary, decisions, career, global literacy.

**"Should I incorporate?"**
Check B table. Ask liability, control, tax, capital. If they are still testing a small idea with no employees and low risk, sole-prop/LLC is the usual survey answer; if they need outside equity or want to survive the founder, corporation. Remind them the form can change.

**"We're in a recession — what do we do?"**
Name the cycle phase (A). Then: mix (C) probably does not launch a premium line; books (D) watch liquidity and leverage because the Fed and customers both just moved; form (B) does not magically save a cash-poor sole prop. Do not output a macro forecast.

**"How do we market this?"**
Name the target first. Then 4Ps. Promotion last, not first. If they said "we need to go viral," they skipped segmentation.

**"Are we healthy?"**
IS for the period, BS for the snapshot, then **one ratio from each family against a baseline**. "Current ratio is 1.1" is incomplete. "Current ratio fell from 2.0 to 1.1 while debt-to-equity rose, in a tightening-rate environment" is an implication.

**"What makes a good manager?"**
Skills (technical / conceptual / interpersonal) + ethics/CSR/mission. Then **hand off** to BUS208 if they want POLC done as a job, to BUS401 if they want leading vs managing.
