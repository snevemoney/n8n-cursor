## 1. Procedure — answer an environmental-ethics question in six checks

Run in order. Skip a check only if the user already constrained the frame.

### Check B — Key concepts; anthropocentrism vs bio/ecocentrism (when "does nature count / who is the center of value" is in play)

CLO 1. Unit 1.

Name, in order:

1. **What environmental ethics is.** Live intro: a philosophy that **extends** ethical concepts traditionally applied to **human behavior** to the **natural world**. Not "be nice to trees." Not PHIL102 "is there an argument."
2. **The pass-level map.** **Anthropocentrism** (human-centered value, including future generations as a human-time horizon) vs **biocentrism** (life-centered) vs **ecocentrism** (system/ecosystem-centered). Confirm live wording on login; encode the *split* even when the resource list is still login-only.
3. **Future generations and species.** Live intro names both. A future-generations argument can still be anthropocentric; a species/ecosystem argument is competing for the center.

**Hand off Unit 2** if they want which approach or religious world view. Stay here for "who/what counts, and which value-center is in play."

### Check C — Approaches and world views, including religious teachings (when "which view / which tradition / how should we reason about nature" is in play)

CLOs 2–3. Unit 2.

Name the **seat** before naming a pioneer or a law:

1. **Approaches and viewpoints (CLO 2).** List them as *types of reasoning about nature*, not as a timeline. Do not invent a catalog (deep ecology, ecofeminism, stewardship, …) as live Saylor titles — name only what the user or a live resource already named, and keep the *job*.
2. **Religious teachings and beliefs throughout the world (CLO 3).** This is **inside Unit 2**, not a sixth unit. Relating a concept to a tradition ≠ writing a world-religions survey.
3. **World view vs law.** Live intro: ethical beliefs and moral worldview later **shape laws**. Do not skip to Unit 5.

Quiz trap: **Approach ≠ pioneer.** **World view ≠ statute.** **Not PHIL103 MacIntyre.** **Not PHIL304.**

**Hand off Unit 3** when the prompt is "when / who pioneered." Stay here for "which approach or world view is doing the work."

### Check D — History and pioneers (when "when did this start / who is the pioneer / how did attitudes change" is in play)

CLOs 4–6. Unit 3.

Pick the **timeline seat** before listing approaches again:

1. **Change over time (CLO 4).** Moral attitudes towards the natural world **changed**. Live intro: debated **since the 1800s**; many still call it a **relatively new discipline**.
2. **Events (CLO 5).** Key events in the **environmental ethics movement**. Names are login-only this harvest — encode the *job*, not a guessed Earth Day / Silent Spring item.
3. **Pioneers (CLO 6).** Important pioneers. **Do not invent** Muir / Leopold / Carson as live Saylor names. If the user names one, treat it as *this* unit's job unless they actually want the approach (Unit 2) or the statute (Unit 5).

Quiz trap: **"Since the 1800s" is not "Aristotle already covered this."** **Pioneer ≠ value-center.** **Event ≠ law.** **Not PHIL103's Plato-to-Rawls roster.** **Not HIST363's industrial timeline** even when industry caused the harm.

**Hand off Unit 4** for "who is harmed." Stay here for "what changed, when, and who pushed it."

### Check E — Environmental justice (when "who is harmed / which community / why this group" is in play)

CLO 7. Unit 4.

1. **Certain social groups at greater risk.** That is the live CLO. Disregard for the environment **negatively impacts entire communities** (live intro).
2. **Not PHIL103.** Rawls's veil, Crito, and the value of a human life are the neighbor course. This seat is **environmental injustice** — who bears environmental harm — not "what would we choose if we did not know our place."
3. **Not yet law.** Illegal is Unit 5. A community can be at greater risk **before** or **without** a matching statute.

Quiz trap: **Justice ≠ ecocentrism.** **Injustice ≠ illegal.** **Not GEOG101 regions.** **Do not invent Warren County / Cancer Alley as live Saylor cases.**

**Hand off Unit 5** for "which law / US vs EU." Stay here for "why this group is at greater risk."

### Check F — Environmental law, US and EU (when "which statute / regulation / political effort" is in play)

CLO 8. Unit 5.

1. **Major environmental laws worldwide, in particular the United States and the European Union.** Live intro: political efforts promoting **environmental sustainability in the United States and Europe**.
2. **Worldview shapes law.** Ethical beliefs and moral worldview help shape the laws and regulations (live intro). Do not treat a statute as value-free, and do not treat a value-center as already a statute.
3. **US and EU, not "American civics only."** Name the **legal seat**. Named titles are login-only this harvest — encode the *job*. **Do not invent** NEPA / Clean Air Act / ESA / EU directives as live Saylor titles.

Quiz trap: **Law ≠ justice.** **US ≠ EU.** **Not BUS200 compliance.** **Not PHIL102 "is this a valid argument for a law."**

**Hand off PHIL102** for validity. **Hand off PHIL103** for justice-as-moral-and-political-theory. **Hand off PHIL304** for existentialism as a sequence. Stay here for "which legal seat, and what ethical view is it encoding."
