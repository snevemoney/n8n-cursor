### Check B — Limits, continuity, differentiability (when "limit / continuous / L'Hopital" is in play)

CLOs 1–2. Unit 2. Intro: limits begin the study of calculus.

**Limits from formulas, graphs, or tables using properties of limits and L'Hopital's Rule. Continuous or differentiable at a point or on an interval, with justification.**

Shape: limit then continuity/differentiability. Encode **L'Hopital** as written. Do not invent epsilon-delta as live.

### Check C — What a derivative is (when "rate of change / units of dy/dx" is in play)

CLO 3. Unit 3 start. Intro: derivatives handle things that change.

**Average and instantaneous rates of change in context; meaning and units of the derivative for graphical functions.**

Shape: rate then units. Compound-interest intro is the color, not a CFO job.

### Check D — Computing derivatives (when "differentiate this" is in play)

CLO 4. Unit 3.

**Derivatives of polynomial, rational, and common transcendental functions, compositions thereof, and implicitly defined functions.** Parametric slopes/rates live in CLO 5, not here.

Shape: pick the live family, then differentiate. **Chain / product / quotient are not Saylor-titled.** Compositions and implicit are live. Transcendental unnamed (do not mint sin/exp as syllabus titles; they may appear in the prompt).

### Check E — Derivative applications and graphs (when "related rates / max / shape / Newton" is in play)

CLOs 5–9, 10 overlay. Unit 4.

**Max/min and related rates; parametric slopes and rates; extreme values of modeling functions; predict/construct/interpret graph shapes; Newton's method; linear approximations using differentials.** Restate applied answers with units (CLO 10).

Shape: application then graph then numerical (Newton) then linearization. Newton's method is live by name. Do not invent concavity/inflection as Saylor-titled (graph-shape CLO is the bucket).

### Check F — Mathematical statements (when "what's the hypothesis" is in play)

CLO 11.

**State which parts of a mathematical statement are assumptions (hypotheses) and which are conclusions.**

Shape: hypothesis vs conclusion. Not a logic course. Not ENGL*.

### Check G — Integrals (when "antiderivative / definite integral" is in play)

CLOs 12–13. Unit 5 The Integral.

**Antiderivatives by changing variables and using tables. Calculate definite integrals.**

Shape: antiderivative then definite integral. **FTC is not live by name.** Changing variables is live (do not rename it u-substitution as a Saylor title). Tables are live. Do not invent Riemann sums as live titles.
