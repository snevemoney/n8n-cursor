# Presentations: audience, structure, deliver, including online and nerves (clustered)

**Use when:** a workplace talk must be **prepared and delivered** — audience, topic/occasion, outline and speech pattern, supporting content and sources, presentation aids created then used, vocal and nonverbal delivery, **online** as well as face-to-face, and **anxiety / the unexpected** — in the Saylor PRDV008 **Preparing and Delivering Presentations** school (23 h adult-learner PD).

**Do not use when:** reconstructing exams; **PRDV002** professional *writing* (email/report/blog); **PRDV103** interviewing; **PRDV211** foundations of human communication as a *survey*; **ENGL*** composition (Publishing — do not write those packets); **COMM*** as a degree-track speech course unless assigned; promising ACE; treating anxiety as clinical care; **sending** or posting the talk for the operator; Toastmasters/vendor cert dumps.

This is **one** skill. It is not eleven unit skills. Live intro is two parts: **prepare a presentation that informs, persuades, or entertains** then **deliver it** (language, voice, body, online, nerves, unexpected). Eleven live units sit on that loop.

## 1. Procedure — six checks

Run in order.

### Check A — Identity overlay (always)

| Prompt smells like | Hand off |
|---|---|
| "Write the email / report / blog" | **PRDV002** |
| "Interview answers / résumé talk" | **PRDV103** / career interview skill; **never send** |
| "What is communication as a survey" | **PRDV211** |
| "Essay / composition" | **ENGL*** (Publishing — not this desk) |
| "Build or give this workplace talk, including online and nerves" | **this skill** |

**Primary goal:** a clear, appropriate, effective oral presentation for *this* audience and occasion, delivered with control — including online. Named failure mode: starting with slides (Unit 6) before audience, topic, and outline exist.

### Check B — Communication elements and audience (when "who is this for / what even is the talk" is in play)

CLO 1–4. Units 1–3.

**Recognize elements of communication** when preparing and giving. **Analyze an audience.** **Choose a topic** from expertise, audience, and occasion. **Choose careful language** appropriate for the audience. Inform, persuade, or entertain is live intro — pick the job.

### Check C — Structure, pattern, supporting content (when "what's the outline / what's my point" is in play)

CLO 5–7. Units 4–5.

**Develop an outline** with a **clear central message** and supporting main points. **Choose a speech pattern** to organize those points. **Choose strong, appropriate content** that supports the central message. Live intro: evidence and **references** for credibility.

**Hand off PRDV002** if they actually need the written artifact. Stay here for the *spoken* structure.

### Check D — Create then use aids (when "slides / handout / demo" is in play)

CLO 8, CLO 9 overlay. Units 6 and **10**.

**Identify strategies for creating presentation aids that enhance the verbal message** (Unit 6). Later **use** them (Unit 10) so they support, not replace, the talk. Live intro: tailor with compelling aids.

Do not collapse create vs use. Do not turn this into PRDV007 chart-building as a spreadsheet course.

### Check E — Deliver, including online and categories (when "how do I say it / Zoom / which mode" is in play)

CLO 9–10. Units 7–9.

**Explain cohesive oral techniques** for style and language, **citing authoritative sources**, and appropriate aids. **Identify fundamentals**: tone, volume, rate, modulation, body language, eye contact. Unit 8: **categories of presentation delivery**. Unit 9: **speaking to an online audience** is first-class, not a footnote.

### Check F — Anxiety and the unexpected (when "I'm terrified / what if the deck dies" is in play)

CLO 11. Unit 11.

**Identify methods for addressing public speaking anxiety.** Live intro also: **plan for the unexpected**. This is PD, not clinical. If it turns into a health crisis, stop.

## 2. Worked patterns (shape only — not exam items)

**"Friday pitch to a vendor on Zoom."**
Check A (oral, online) then B (vendor audience; persuade) then C (central message + pattern) then D (aids that survive screenshare) then E (online delivery) then F (if nerves). Not PRDV002's email. Not PRDV217 sales-as-a-course unless they need that overlay.

**"I have 40 slides and no point."**
Check C first (central message, outline, pattern) then D (create *then* use — cut aids that do not enhance the verbal message). Failure mode: Unit 6 before Unit 4.

**"I freeze in the room even when the outline is good."**
Check F (anxiety methods as PD) then E (delivery fundamentals). Not therapy. Not PSYCH101.
