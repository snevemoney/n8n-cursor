# Value-based pricing

Price automation/AI work off client value, not hours (after first 2–3 learning projects).

## Steps
1. Discovery: listen/repeat/poke; Why this / Why now / Why me — do not blurt price early.
2. Size pain: time today, people touching it, failure cost.
3. Annualize year-1 value; price ~10–20% of that band; narrate carefully — no hard 10× guarantees.
4. Tiered proposal (starter/growth/scale) so decision is which not whether.
5. Objective milestones (~30 days); maintenance retainer separate from features.
6. Client owns API/token costs; your fee = design/build/test.
7. Scope creep → V2 backlog; capture baselines at 30/60/90 for upsell proof.

## Do not
- Cite unverified educator income claims
- Guarantee ROI

## Enrich 2026-10-11 (signals-2mo-backlog, yt:DoHPZf7jEQ4 Nate Herk talk, full transcript)
- Worked example checked (`recreations/20261011/run1030/pricing_math.py` PASS): support rep $40/h × 10 h/week × 52 = $20,800/yr; 10% ⇒ $2,080. Rule of thumb on tape: the client should see ~10× their spend (narrate, never guarantee).
- Manual-process cost isn't only wages: missed calls × job value, opportunity cost of a funnel that never shipped.
- Assume they will ask "how did you get that number?"; walk the math, then stop talking.
- **Cost doesn't justify price, value does.** If value is $15k and budget is $7k, don't discount: cut scope to a V1 worth ~$7k and park the rest for next quarter. Dropping price on first pushback teaches them to push.
- Hourly pricing rewards the slower builder; fine only as a foot-in-the-door on the first 1–2 projects.
- Retainer comes after proof: one value-priced project → show the agreed KPI moved at 1–3 months → propose a retainer to keep removing the next constraint.
