## user

Should i cook steaks with butter or olive oil on this type of pan

[User attached 1 image; image contents were not included]

## assistant

That looks like a **stainless-steel pan**, and for steak I’d use **oil first, butter later**.

For this pan:
1. **Preheat the empty pan** over medium-high heat for about 2–3 minutes.
2. Add a small amount of **high-heat oil**. Olive oil works, but **avocado/canola oil** is even better for a hard sear. If using olive oil, preferably use refined/light olive oil rather than extra virgin.
3. Put your **dry, seasoned steak** in once the oil is shimmering.
4. Leave it alone long enough to develop a crust before flipping. Stainless steel will initially grab the steak; once a crust develops, it releases much more easily.
5. **After flipping, lower the heat a little and add butter** (about 1–2 tbsp). Garlic + thyme/rosemary are optional. Tilt the pan and spoon the foaming butter over the steak.
6. Remove and rest for 5–10 minutes.

**Don't start with butter alone** on a very hot stainless pan—it can burn before you get a good crust.

So: **oil = searing 🔥 → butter = flavor/basting 🧈.**

If you tell me **how thick the steaks are and what doneness you want** (medium-rare, medium, etc.), I can give you the exact heat and approximate minutes per side.

## user

Is it healthy

## assistant

Yes. **Steak cooked in a stainless-steel pan with a modest amount of oil and butter can absolutely fit into a healthy diet.**

For a healthier version, I'd do **1–2 teaspoons of olive or avocado oil for the sear**, then only **½–1 tablespoon of butter** near the end for flavor. You don't need enough fat for the steak to swim in it.

The bigger nutritional factors are the **cut, portion size, and frequency**. A leaner steak gives you plenty of protein, iron, zinc and B12 with less saturated fat. Butter adds saturated fat, so olive oil as the primary cooking fat is generally the better everyday choice.

If you're specifically trying to make your meals **better for cardiovascular health while losing fat**, I'd lean toward **lean steak + olive oil + vegetables**, with just a little butter for the finish rather than butter-heavy basting.
