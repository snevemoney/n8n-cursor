## user

When will be able to spend 10k for fun

## assistant

If you mean **“At what point in my wealth-building journey can I spend $10,000 purely for fun without feeling like I just sabotaged myself?”** — I’d base it on **net worth and investable assets**, not salary.

A useful rule for you would be: **a $10K fun purchase should ideally be ≤2–3% of your liquid/investable wealth**, after your emergency fund is protected.

So roughly:

| Investable wealth | $10K represents | My view |
|---:|---:|---|
| $25K | 40% | ❌ Way too early |
| $50K | 20% | ❌ Major setback |
| $100K | 10% | ⚠️ Possible, but expensive |
| $200K | 5% | 🟡 Occasionally |
| **$300K** | **3.3%** | 🟢 Reasonable |
| **$500K** | **2%** | 🟢 Comfortable |
| $1M | 1% | 🟢 Very easy to justify |

For **your goal of aggressively building wealth**, I’d make **$300K invested/net investable assets** the milestone where you unlock a $10K guilt-free splurge.

Even better: don't withdraw the $10K from the $300K. Build to $300K, **keep it invested**, then accumulate the next $10K separately as your reward. That turns your first $300K into a permanent wealth engine rather than something you repeatedly raid.

At **$500K+**, I'd loosen the rule significantly. You could have an annual luxury/fun budget of perhaps **$10–15K** while still prioritizing compounding, assuming your income and savings rate support it.

The key distinction is that **“I have $10K” and “I can afford to blow $10K” are completely different levels of wealth.** For the kind of wealth you're trying to build, I'd rather see you reach the point where losing $10K barely changes the trajectory.

## user

## assistant

Yes — but I’d treat **vacations more generously than random luxury spending**.

You don’t need $300K invested before taking a great vacation. That threshold was for being able to casually spend **$10K purely for fun** without meaningfully affecting wealth-building.

For a **$10K vacation**, I’d think about it this way:

| Financial position | $10K vacation |
|---|---|
| <$25K liquid/invested | 🔴 Too large |
| $25–50K | 🟠 Only for something exceptional |
| $50–100K | 🟡 Possible with a dedicated vacation fund |
| $100–200K | 🟢 Reasonable occasionally |
| $200K+ | 🟢 Comfortable |
| $500K+ | 🟢 Could reasonably become recurring |

The biggest rule: **don't sell investments to vacation.** Save the vacation money from *new income* while continuing your normal investing.

For example, once you've built a solid investment base, you could create a **10K Vacation Fund** and put $400–$500/month into it. After ~20–25 months, you can take a pretty ridiculous vacation without touching the portfolio.

And I wouldn't postpone *all* major experiences until you're wealthy. There's a balance: **build assets aggressively while you're young, but don't make your entire 20s and 30s a waiting room for your 40s.**
