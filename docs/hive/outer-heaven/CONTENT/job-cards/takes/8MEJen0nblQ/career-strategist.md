## B. Atomic Knowledge

### If it is not selling, the story is wrong — you are not selling AI
- **Claim:** Builders who can scope and deliver still freeze on sales because the story is unclear. Leaders at 10-person and 100k-employee shops all want the benefits. AI is not valuable in itself. HVAC: they cannot answer the phone / dispatch — sell “I pick up the phone,” not a model.
- **Reasoning:** Intent in prompts and intent in sales are the same skill. Table-stakes language (secure, compliant, certs) does not close; a graspable accountability story does.
- **Mechanism:** Specific demographic + their operator pain + a story a leader can answer for.
- **Evidence:** “if you are having trouble selling, the issue is that you are having trouble storytelling… You shouldn’t be selling AI.”
- **Conditions:** A real demographic (he uses HVAC in Seattle), not “SMBs.”
- **Exceptions:** Unconvinced buyers still exist — sister tapes say leave clean (`Pi-m8R068r4`).

### Accountability story beats certification theater

## C. Mental Models
Selling failure is a story failure. AI is the wire; the factory redesign is the job. Verification loop is what business will pay for as vibe-coding gets cheap. AI-native is reach-order, not a job title. Human attention is scarce gold in an agent-flood. Demographic specificity creates tactics. Monster stories at dinner are an industry own-goal. Token volume is a culture metric, not a spend trophy. Small passionate teams beat org-wide theater. Jobs survive as missions. Taste is a zag that later commoditizes. Open-source + optimistic countries may set the future more than closed benches.

## D. Procedures
1. Write the outcome story in operator language (“phone gets answered”) before any model name.
2. Pick a demographic specific enough that they are not an online commodity (his example: HVAC Seattle) — gym only.
3. Ask what the buyer must defend (court, board, Monday standup). Make that visible.
4. Tool: does this take weight off a load I already have? Else skip.
5. Enablement: one close, slightly scary shared project; team leads first; CEO uses the tool.
6. Do not hand out credits and walk away.
7. If you lack domain: read AI-native *and* domain-expert practice, then decide tool-call vs build-IP.

Questions: What is the story? What must they defend? What is the load? Who is the SDM that will champion? Are we selling AI or the phone?

Signals: they lean in on the pain sentence. Red: “it’s secure, trust us” as the whole pitch.

## F. Decision Rules
- If the pitch names the model first, rewrite it as the operator outcome.
- If certs are the whole story, you are table stakes.
- If the demographic is “anyone who wants AI,” you do not have intent yet.
- If a tool does not remove a known load, skip it.
- If the CEO does not use the stack, do not expect the org to.
- If the board’s AI plan is fire people, he reads that as out of ideas — not a place to build a mission career.
- If you only have tasks, you are easier to delete than if you have a mission.
