## C. Mental Models
The business model is a ladder; freeze is a skipped rung. The hour is a relationship deposit and paid discovery, not a siloed side hustle. Owner holds the SME; you hold tool fluency — you cannot correctly build their OS for them. Overwhelm management is the product of the hour. “I don’t know, I’ll test it before next time” is competence, not fraud. Imposter syndrome is chronic and informational (uninformed optimist is not on this tape — that’s `diY71x7GUjI`). Hours do not scale; they buy the right to a project. Internal CAIO is a first-class alternative to agency. Build-your-own-OS-first is a prerequisite, not a nice-to-have.

## D. Procedures
1. Build your own AI OS (he points at a free 2-hour Skool course + GitHub — on-tape; hive does not join).
2. Pitch language (gym only): help set up the OS — connect data, extract how they think, reduce key-man risk — “operating system, not an AI tutorial.”
3. In the hour: desktop vs editor; connect sources; simple MCP/API words; watch for lost-thread; ask “does this make sense?”; stop ~60 min; book next.
4. If you do not know: say so; research/test; bring it next time.
5. Over-deliver early (extra 10 min, follow-up with 2–3 tries). Price for reps ($50–$99) before cash.
6. Find-people ladder (his): friends → known owners free → intros → community help → public artifacts → natural project ask → local only with proof.
7. When you have seen a real workflow: “next session I want to go deep on X; I’ll come back with a proposal.”
8. If they will not climb: “I’m a text away. No pressure.” Leave clean.
9. If selling/teaching is not you: partner with a seller, or run internal workshops where you already work.

Questions: Does this make sense? What’s next? Any owner-friends who want the same hour? Have you felt ROI yet?

Signals: they book session two; they say the lift is bigger than they thought; they ask you to build it. Disqualify: you have to drag them.
