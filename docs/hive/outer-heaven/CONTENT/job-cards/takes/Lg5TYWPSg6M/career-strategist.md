- **Reasoning:** A guessed rate cannot be defended. A fraction of their annualized pain can.

## C. Mental Models
Price sits between your floor and their ceiling; the ceiling is found in conversation, not in a rate card. Hourly inverts skill (faster = poorer). 10× year-one is a sales story he likes, not a law — and he used ~7.5× plus a flywheel projection he will not guarantee. Hours saved are the beginner ceiling; error dollars and closed-deal value are the later ceiling. Three options change the question from “should we hire you?” to “which package?” Middle tier is designed to win. Maintenance is uptime of the agreed scope. Tokens are a utility bill in their name. Discovery refusal is a vendor-comparison tell. LRP + Why this / Why now / Why me surfaces the intern/nephew objection while you can still answer it.

## D. Procedures
1. If they ask price first: you need complexity + integration depth before a ballpark.
2. Let them dump (LRP: listen, repeat, poke). Notes. Then: after we’re done, best case, what changes.
3. Why this (is the asked artifact the real fix — maybe a script + Slack, not an agent)? Why now (window / competitor)? Why me (why not intern / vibe-code / nephew)?
4. Nephew answer: don’t argue. Who watches it at 2am when the model updates? Who scales throughput? Stop. Let them talk.
5. If they only want a quote to shop vendors: walk. Proof, not cheapest labor.
6. Size: time today / people who touch it / what happens when it fails + proxies.
7. Marketplace: assume volume from the post; say the assumption; invite 15 min to adjust.
8. If you cannot defend a number, do not write the proposal.
9. Pick 10–20% of first-year annualized (his example ~13% / $5,500 on $41,600). Be ready to walk the math.
10. Proposal top: three short paragraphs — where they are (their words + numbers), where they said they want to be, why you — then starter / growth / scale (he sketches 10% / 25% / 50% of a $100k value as a toy).
11. Split pay on 30-day objective milestones (start / mid / production).
12. Creep → “great, v2 backlog; let’s hit the gates we already set.”
13. “Too expensive” → smaller first slice, same rate logic.
14. Client card for production tokens; you eat test spend (or bake $1–3k into price); swap keys at go-live.
15. Record baseline; resurface at 30/60/90 days.

Questions: How long today? How many people? What happens when it fails? Why this / why now / why me? Can you walk me through that price? (you should already have the answer)

Signals: they state a cost-of-problem; they answer Why me with “I need someone watching it.” Red flag: number-only, no discovery.

## F. Decision Rules
